I do not remember exactly how young I was when my relationship with music began. I only know that I heard a fragment of piano music before I had the language to name it. That sound stayed with me. Years later, while watching Somewhere in Time, I recognized it: Rhapsody on a Theme of Paganini, Op. 43 by Sergei Rachmaninoff. That discovery made Rachmaninoff my favorite piano composer.

I started taking guitar seriously when a classmate in middle school asked me to take his acoustic guitar home because he did not feel like carrying it. I kept it for several weeks. It came with a magazine covering basic chords, notation, and hand positions. Within days, I realized I could learn by ear and play what I heard in my mind. My mother later gave me my first acoustic guitar, and my father bought me a Dean VMNT United Abominations, an electric guitar associated with Megadeth. I no longer have it, but it is still my favorite electric guitar.

My taste in guitar music ranged through metal, thrash, and rock, including The Doors, Metallica, Megadeth, and Nirvana. Later, the technical side of guitar led me to music by Animals as Leaders, Guthrie Govan, Thank You Scientist, An Endless Sporadic, CHON, and John Coltrane. I am currently focused on someday obtaining an Abasi Concept 8-string guitar.

Piano became a real part of my life later, when I finally bought one at 25. It felt even more intuitive than I expected. My ability to learn by ear carried over immediately, and piano became the instrument where I can improvise, compose, and give sound to my thoughts. After I bought it, my father told me he had always wanted to play piano himself, which made the instrument more personal.
