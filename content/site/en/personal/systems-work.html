This section is about systems work as part of my life. It explains how work, technology, and pressure became a path of survival and technical growth.

My professional career did not begin under favorable conditions. At 17, still a minor, the only work available to me was a factory job dedicated to manufacturing the disposable plastic cutlery commonly used at gatherings across Mexico. The conditions were harsh. The role required carrying heavy loads of materials repeatedly throughout the shift, and within the first week I sustained an injury. I lasted one week. That experience made one thing clear: I needed to build a life in which my mind would be my main tool. I have never enjoyed physical labor, and that was when I understood I needed to pursue a more technical and intellectual path.

At 18, I relocated to Querétaro and began working at a Skechers retail store as a salesperson. I moved up to assistant manager within a relatively short period of time. A customer came in, and during our interaction he noticed I spoke fluent English. That small detail changed the direction of my career entirely. He connected me with a contact of his who was looking to fill an IT position. I went through the interviews and landed my first IT job.

That transition was possible because computing was already familiar to me. My first computer classes were at Instituto Valle. I continued taking computer classes at Colegio Watson y Crick, followed later by informatics at IPN. Along the way, I also learned in internet cafés and through my interest in technology and music. By the time that first IT opportunity appeared, computing, using software, hardware, and the logic of systems were not new to me. I have never left the field since. Every position has been within the technology sector, and each has taken me further.

Over the years my career has evolved considerably. I moved from general IT support into data analysis, and from there into systems engineering and data and systems integration. I currently work as a Data and Systems Integration Tier 2 Analyst at BlackLine, operating at the intersection of enterprise software, financial systems, and technical problem solving. The role demands precision, analytical thinking, and a thorough understanding of how complex systems communicate and fail, all of which align naturally with how I have always approached any problem placed in front of me.

My interest in programming extends past the workplace because it helps me think with more precision. For this site and related work, the practical tools are VS Code, HTML, CSS, JavaScript, content structure, and interface logic. I am also studying Python, but I am not presenting it here as a core tool behind this site.
