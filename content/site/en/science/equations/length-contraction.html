Length Contraction

Equation

$$L' = \frac{L}{\gamma}$$

Literal Reading

L prime equals L divided by gamma.

Common Reading

A moving object's length along the direction of motion being measured shorter than its rest length.

Symbols

\(L\): proper length measured in the object's rest frame.

\(L'\): contracted length measured by a relatively moving observer.

\(\gamma\): Lorentz factor.

Meaning of the Equation

Length contraction applies along the direction of relative motion and depends on simultaneity in the observer frame.

Conditions and units

Both lengths are in the same length units. The dimensionless factor is \(\gamma=(1-v^2/c^2)^{-1/2}\), with inertial relative speed \(|v|<c\) and \(c=299792458\) m/s. The observer must measure the two endpoints simultaneously in that observer’s frame. This is a coordinate measurement of an object at rest in its own inertial frame, not the optical appearance in a photograph.

Worked example or interpretation

At relative speed 0.6c, γ=1.25. A rod of proper length 10 m is measured as 8 m along its motion when its endpoints are recorded simultaneously in the measuring frame.

Source

Sean M. Carroll, Lecture Notes on General Relativity.
