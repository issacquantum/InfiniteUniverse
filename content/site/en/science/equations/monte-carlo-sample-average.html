Monte Carlo Sample Average

Equation

$$\hat\mu_N=\frac1N\sum_{k=1}^N f(X_k)$$

Literal reading

Mu-hat subscript N equals one divided by N times the sum from k equals one to N of f evaluated at X subscript k.

Conceptual reading

Average the observable over random draws to estimate its expectation.

Symbols

\(\hat\mu_N\) is the scalar estimate based on N samples; the hat denotes estimation, not an operator. \(N\) is a positive integer sample count, \(k\) the integer index from 1 through N, \(X_k\) sample k, and \(f\) the function measured. \(\sum\) adds its values, and division by N forms the arithmetic mean.

Values, units and domains

The estimate has the units of f. Counts and indices are dimensionless. In simple Monte Carlo the draws are independent and identically distributed under the target law. The law of large numbers requires integrability; the usual inverse-square-root standard error also requires finite variance.

Meaning

The estimate converges to the target expectation under the stated sampling assumptions. More samples do not guarantee a smaller realized error at every step. The probability law and the algorithm used to sample it are different objects.

Assumptions and limits

Correlated samples require a correlation-aware error estimate. Importance sampling requires weights. An incorrect sampling law can produce a wrong limiting average regardless of sample size.

Example

For f(x)=x, the illustrative dimensionless draws 0.2, 0.4, 0.8 and 0.6 sum to 2.0. Dividing by four gives 0.5. This chosen sample happens to equal the uniform [0,1] expectation; another sample need not.

Source

Art B. Owen, Monte Carlo theory, methods and examples, Chapter 2.
