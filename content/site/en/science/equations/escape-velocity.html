Escape Velocity

Equation

$$v_e = \sqrt{\frac{2GM}{r}}$$

Literal Reading

v subscript e equals the square root of two G M divided by r.

Common Reading

The minimum speed needed to escape a spherical gravitating body without further propulsion.

Symbols

\(v_e\): escape velocity.

\(G\): Newton's gravitational constant.

\(M\): mass of the gravitating object.

\(r\): distance from the object's center.

Meaning of the Equation

Escape speed is not about reaching a height; it is about total mechanical energy becoming zero or positive. At that speed, an object can coast away forever in the ideal no-drag, no-thrust case.

Scope and Limits

The equation is Newtonian, so it is best used as intuition. General relativity replaces escape-speed language with horizon geometry and causal structure.

Conditions and units

\(v_e\) is a speed in m/s, \(r\) is in meters, \(M\) in kg and \(G\) in m³ kg⁻¹ s⁻². The test body starts outside a spherical mass distribution with potential zero at infinity, in an inertial frame where the source is at rest. Collisions, atmosphere, other gravitating bodies and relativistic strong fields are excluded.

Worked example or interpretation

For an illustrative spherical source with GM=4 × 10¹⁴ m³/s² and starting radius 8 × 10⁶ m, the escape speed is 10⁴ m/s. This is the initial speed for zero asymptotic speed in the ideal isolated Newtonian problem; propulsion after launch and atmospheric drag are excluded.
