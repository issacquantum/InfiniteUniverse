Canonical Partition Function

Equation

$$Z=\operatorname{Tr}(e^{-\beta H})$$

Literal reading

Z equals the trace of the exponential of minus beta times H.

Conceptual reading

The partition function sums thermal weights over the energy states.

Symbols

\(Z\) is the partition function, \(H\) the self-adjoint Hamiltonian and \(\beta\) inverse temperature. The operator exponential uses natural base e. \(\operatorname{Tr}\) takes the trace of the entire exponential, counting energy-level degeneracies through the state sum.

Values, units and domains

H is in J and beta in J⁻¹; beta is the reciprocal of k_B T, with exact k_B = 1.380649 × 10⁻²³ J/K and temperature T in K. Z and the exponent are dimensionless. The canonical state requires a convergent positive trace.

Meaning

Z is the normalization of thermal probabilities and also encodes equilibrium thermodynamic information through its parameter dependence. It is not the probability of one state.

Assumptions and limits

Canonical equilibrium with a trace-class Boltzmann operator. Infinite systems may require a finite-volume construction and controlled thermodynamic limit.

Example

For two levels at energies zero and Δ with beta times Δ = ln 2, Z = 1 + 1/2 = 3/2. If the upper level is doubly degenerate, Z becomes 1 + 2 × 1/2 = 2.

Source

David Tong, Statistical Physics; NIST, CODATA 2022.
