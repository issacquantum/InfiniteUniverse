Shannon Entropy

Equation

$$ H(X) = -\sum_x p_x \log_2 p_x $$

Literal Reading

H of X equals minus the sum over x of p subscript x times the base-two logarithm of p subscript x.

Common Reading

Shannon entropy is the expected uncertainty or surprise of a classical random variable.

Symbols

\(H(X)\): the Shannon entropy of the random variable \(X\).

\(p_x\): the probability of outcome \(x\).

\(\sum_x\): sum over all possible outcomes.

\(\log_2\): logarithm base two, so the entropy is measured in bits.

Meaning of the Equation

Entropy is the expected information per symbol from a source. High entropy means lower predictability, so less compression is possible without losing information.

Conditions and units

This is a discrete distribution with \(p_x\ge0\), \(\sum_xp_x=1\), and \(0\log0=0\). A finite alphabet gives a finite entropy in bits. Compression claims use an appropriate source-coding model; correlated sequences are governed by entropy rate rather than single-symbol entropy alone. Differential entropy for a continuous variable is a different quantity.
