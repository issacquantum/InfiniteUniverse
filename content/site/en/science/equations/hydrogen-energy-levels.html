Hydrogen Energy Levels

Equation

$$ E_n = -\frac{13.6 \text{ eV}}{n^2}, \quad n = 1, 2, 3, \ldots $$

Literal Reading

E subscript n equals minus thirteen point six electron volts divided by n squared, with n equal to one, two, three, and so on.

Common Reading

The bound-state energy of hydrogen is minus thirteen point six electron volts divided by the square of the principal quantum number.

Symbols

\(E_n\): the allowed energy of the state labeled by \(n\).

\(13.6\,\text{eV}\): the ground-state binding energy of hydrogen.

\(n\): the principal quantum number, which can take the values 1, 2, 3, and so on.

Meaning of the Equation

This is the nonrelativistic Bohr/Schrödinger hydrogen approximation; it ignores fine structure, Lamb shift, hyperfine structure, and finite nuclear mass unless corrected.

Conditions and units

The zero of energy is a separated proton and electron at rest at infinite separation. Negative \(E_n\) therefore denotes binding; \(n\) is dimensionless and 13.6 eV is a rounded atomic energy scale, not an exact universal constant. One eV equals \(1.602176634\times10^{-19}\) J exactly. Emission between levels carries photon energy equal to the positive difference between initial and final atomic energies.

Worked example or interpretation

The displayed approximation gives −13.6 eV for n=1 and −3.4 eV for n=2. A transition from n=2 to n=1 releases 10.2 eV before the omitted corrections are considered. Raising n approaches zero from below rather than producing positive bound-state energies.
