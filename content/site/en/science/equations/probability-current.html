Probability Current

Equation

$$ \mathbf{J} = \frac{\hbar}{2mi}\left(\Psi^*\nabla\Psi - \Psi\nabla\Psi^*\right) $$

Literal Reading

J equals h-bar divided by two m i, times the quantity psi complex conjugate times the gradient of psi minus psi times the gradient of psi complex conjugate.

Common Reading

The probability current density built from the wavefunction and its spatial gradients.

Symbols

\(\mathbf{J}\): the probability current density vector.

\(\hbar\): the reduced Planck constant.

\(m\): the particle mass.

\(i\): the imaginary unit.

\(\Psi^*\): the complex conjugate of the wavefunction.

\(\nabla\Psi\): the spatial gradient of the wavefunction.

Meaning of the Equation

This expression applies to a spinless nonrelativistic particle with the usual kinetic-energy operator and no electromagnetic vector potential. For a charged particle with a vector potential, the covariant derivative adds a contribution to the current.

Conditions and units

\(\Psi(\mathbf r,t)\) is the spatial wavefunction and \(i^2=-1\). For a normalized three-dimensional state, \(\Psi\) is in m⁻³ᐟ², \(m\) in kg, and \(\hbar\) in J s, giving current units m⁻² s⁻¹. Surface integration gives probability per unit time, not electric charge per unit time. Together with a real scalar potential and unitary Schrödinger evolution, this current satisfies the probability continuity equation.

Worked example or interpretation

A box-normalized plane wave of momentum p has probability density 1/V and current p/(mV). A real stationary spatial wavefunction instead gives zero current in this formula. The reduced Planck constant is approximately 1.054571817 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s.
