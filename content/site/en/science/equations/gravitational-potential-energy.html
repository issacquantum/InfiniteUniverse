Newtonian Gravitational Potential Energy

Equation

$$U=-\frac{GMm}{r}$$

Literal reading

U equals minus G times M times m divided by r.

Conceptual reading

The pair has negative gravitational potential energy when zero is assigned to infinite separation.

Symbols

\(U\) is potential energy; \(G\) the gravitational constant; \(M,m\) the two masses; \(r\) their positive separation. The minus sign encodes the chosen reference and attraction. The fraction divides the product GMm by distance.

Values, units and domains

G = 6.67430(15) × 10⁻¹¹ m³·kg⁻¹·s⁻²; parentheses give the standard uncertainty in the last digits. Masses are in kg, separation in m and potential energy in J. Energy zero is a reference choice, not a measured absolute value.

Meaning

Increasing separation raises the potential toward zero and requires work against attraction. This is pair energy, not energy per unit mass.

Assumptions and limits

Isolated Newtonian point masses or nonoverlapping spherical bodies, with potential set to zero at infinity. This is not a general relativistic gravitational-energy formula.

Example

Two 1 kg point masses 1 m apart have potential energy about −6.67430 × 10⁻¹¹ J. Doubling separation makes it half as negative.

Source

Newton, Philosophiae Naturalis Principia Mathematica, Book I; NIST, CODATA 2022.
