Kraus Operator-Sum Representation

Equation

$$\mathcal E(\rho)=\sum_k K_k\rho K_k^\dagger$$

Literal reading

Calligraphic E applied to rho equals the sum over k of K sub k, times rho, times K sub k dagger, in that order. Dagger denotes the adjoint.

Conceptual reading

An unobserved quantum operation combines the contributions of its Kraus operators.

Symbols

\(\mathcal E\) is a linear map from input operators to output operators, \(\rho\) the input density operator, \(k\) a Kraus-term index, and \(K_k\) a linear operator from the input Hilbert space to the output space. \(K_k^\dagger\) is its adjoint, or conjugate transpose in orthonormal bases. \(\sum_k\) adds every Kraus term; multiplication means ordered operator composition.

Values, units and domains

For finite-dimensional normalized quantum states, rho is positive semidefinite with trace one. All operators in this expression are dimensionless. Input and output dimensions may differ. The sum can be finite; no universal values are assigned to its entries.

Meaning

This representation guarantees complete positivity. It describes a trace-preserving channel only when the Kraus completeness condition holds. Different Kraus families may represent the same map; the index is not a unique physical history.

Assumptions and limits

The displayed form alone does not guarantee trace preservation. A selected measurement outcome can use a trace-decreasing map; dividing the output by its nonzero trace then gives the conditional state. The finite-dimensional channel setting avoids convergence issues of infinite operator sums.

Example

Let K₀ and K₁ be the projectors onto the orthonormal qubit states 0 and 1. Applied to the equal superposition, the two terms give half the projector onto 0 and half onto 1. The sum is a mixed state with equal populations and no off-diagonal coherence in this basis.

Source

John Watrous, The Theory of Quantum Information, Chapter 2.
