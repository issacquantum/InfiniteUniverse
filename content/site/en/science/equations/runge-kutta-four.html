Runge Kutta Four

Equation

$$ y_{n+1}=y_n+\frac{h}{6}(k_1+2k_2+2k_3+k_4) $$

Literal Reading

y sub n plus 1 equals y sub n plus h over 6 the quantity k1 plus 2k2 plus 2k3 plus k4.

Common Reading

RK4 (fourth-order Runge-Kutta) samples the derivative within a step to get a more accurate update than Euler's method.

Symbols

\(h\): step size.

\(k_1,k_2,k_3,k_4\): slope estimates inside the current step.

\(y_n\) and \(y_{n+1}\): current and next states.

Meaning of the Equation

RK4 estimates several slopes within one time step and combines them. That gives a better approximation to the curved solution path than a single Euler slope.

Conditions and units

For \(dy/dt=f(t,y)\), the stages are \(k_1=f(t_n,y_n)\), \(k_2=f(t_n+h/2,y_n+hk_1/2)\), \(k_3=f(t_n+h/2,y_n+hk_2/2)\), and \(k_4=f(t_n+h,y_n+hk_3)\). The index n counts steps, \(t_{n+1}=t_n+h\), and each slope has state-units per time, so h times a slope is a state increment. These stage definitions, not the weighted sum alone, give classical explicit RK4. Fourth-order convergence needs a sufficiently smooth problem and a stable step choice; this is not unconditionally stable.
