Boltzmann Entropy

Equation

$$S_B=k_B\log W$$

Literal Reading

S subscript B equals k subscript B times the logarithm of W.

Common Reading

Boltzmann entropy is proportional to the logarithm of the number of microstates compatible with the macrostate.

Symbols

  • \(S_B\): Boltzmann entropy.
  • \(k_B\): Boltzmann constant.
  • \(W\): number of compatible microstates.

Meaning of the Equation

It is not just a vague word for disorder; it counts how many microscopic arrangements realize the same macroscopic description.

Conditions and units

Here log is the natural logarithm, \(W\ge1\) is a dimensionless count and \(S_B\) is in J/K. \(k_B=1.380649\times10^{-23}\) J/K exactly. Equal weights for the counted microstates give this statistical entropy; a continuous phase-space count requires a reference cell or measure. Changing the macroscopic constraints changes \(W\).

Example: counting two microscopic arrangements

What is being counted by \(W\)? Consider two distinguishable spins, each with two possible states along a chosen axis: up or down. A microstate specifies both spins individually. The coarser description, or macrostate, says only that one is up and the other is down. Two microstates fit: up-down and down-up.

If these two arrangements have equal statistical weight, \(W=2\), so \(S_B=k_B\ln 2\approx9.57\times10^{-24}\) J/K. Here \(\ln\) is the natural logarithm; \(k_B\) converts the dimensionless count into entropy units. A different constraint requiring both spins to be up permits only one microstate, giving \(W=1\) and zero entropy in this counting convention.

This is a small counting example, not a model of an entire material. The difference comes from which macroscopic constraints are specified. For unequal statistical weights, a probability-weighted entropy is needed instead of merely counting arrangements.
