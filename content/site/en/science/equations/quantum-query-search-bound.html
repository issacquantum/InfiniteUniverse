Quantum Query Search Bound

Equation

$$ Q_{\mathrm{search}}(N)=\Theta(\sqrt{N}) $$

Literal Reading

Q subscript search of N equals big Theta of the square root of N.

Common Reading

Grover search gives a quadratic speedup, and that speedup is optimal for black-box search.

Symbols

\(Q_{\mathrm{search}}(N)\): number of oracle queries needed by a quantum search algorithm.

\(N\): number of possible items.

\(\Theta(\sqrt{N})\): both upper and lower bounds scale like \(\sqrt{N}\), up to constant factors.

Meaning of the Equation

The bound proves Grover's scaling is optimal in the black-box query model. No generic quantum search can do much better without extra structure.

Conditions and units

The problem has an unstructured N-item domain with a coherent membership oracle and fixed bounded error, including the worst case of a single marked item. The count is dimensionless and measures oracle calls, not total circuit time, data loading, or oracle implementation cost. More marked items or additional structure can change the relevant bound.

Worked example or interpretation

Increasing an unstructured search domain from N to 100N multiplies the reference √N query scale by 10, not 100. Theta notation bounds the optimum above and below up to constants; it does not prescribe exactly √N queries for every finite instance.

Source

Bennett, Bernstein, Brassard and Vazirani, Strengths and Weaknesses of Quantum Computing.
