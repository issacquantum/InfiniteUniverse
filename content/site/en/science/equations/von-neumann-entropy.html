Von Neumann Entropy

Equation

$$ S(\rho) = -\operatorname{Tr}(\rho \log_2 \rho) $$

Literal Reading

S of rho equals minus the trace of rho multiplied by the base-two operator logarithm of rho.

Common Reading

Entropy measures uncertainty, disorder, or missing information in the relevant physical or informational state.

Symbols

\(S(\rho)\): the von Neumann entropy of the quantum state.

\(\rho\): the density matrix describing the state.

\(\operatorname{Tr}\): the trace operation, which sums the diagonal values of an operator in a basis independent way.

\(\log_2\): logarithm base two, used so entropy is measured in bits.

Meaning of the Equation

A pure quantum state has zero von Neumann entropy, while mixed or reduced states can have more. The quantity tracks quantum uncertainty and entanglement structure.

Conditions and units

For a positive trace-one density operator, the entropy is \(-\sum_j\lambda_j\log_2\lambda_j\), where \(\lambda_j\) are its eigenvalues and \(0\log0=0\). It is in bits and bounded by \(\log_2d\) in dimension d. A pure state can give uncertain measurement outcomes despite having zero entropy. Reduced-state entropy measures entanglement only when the joint bipartite state is pure; “disorder” is not a precise general definition.
