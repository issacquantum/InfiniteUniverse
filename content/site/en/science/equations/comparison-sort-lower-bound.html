Comparison Sort Lower Bound

Equation

$$ \Omega(n\log_2 n) $$

Literal Reading

Big Omega of n times the base-two logarithm of n.

Common Reading

This is an information limit: sorting must distinguish among \(n!\) possible input orders, and comparisons reveal limited information.

Symbols

\(\Omega\): asymptotic lower bound.

\(n\): number of items being sorted.

\(\log_2 n\): logarithmic growth measured in binary decisions.

Meaning of the Equation

There are too many possible orderings for fewer comparisons to distinguish in the worst case. This is why comparison sorting cannot generally beat n log n scaling.

Conditions and units

The bound counts dimensionless comparisons for arbitrary distinct keys in a comparison-only model. The decision tree needs at least n! leaves. Algorithms exploiting bounded integer keys or other extra structure can avoid this comparison-model bound.

Worked example or interpretation

Four distinct items admit 4!=24 possible orders. A binary comparison tree of depth four has at most 16 leaves, so some input requires at least five comparisons. The asymptotic lower bound follows by applying this counting argument as n grows.

Notation details

Omega states a lower bound by a positive constant multiple for sufficiently large n. Here n is a positive integer, the product n log₂ n sets the comparison-count scale, and the factorial n! counts permutations.
