Vacuum Two-Point Correlation

Equation

$$ G(x,y)=\langle 0|\hat{\phi}(x)\hat{\phi}(y)|0\rangle $$

Literal Reading

G of x and y equals vacuum bra zero, followed by phi-hat of x times phi-hat of y, followed by vacuum ket zero.

Common Reading

A vacuum correlation function: how field fluctuations at two spacetime points are related.

Symbols

\(G(x,y)\): two-point correlation function.

\(|0\rangle\): the vacuum state of the field.

\(\hat{\phi}(x)\): field operator evaluated at spacetime point \(x\).

\(x,y\): spacetime points.

Meaning of the Equation

The vacuum has structured field correlations even when no particles are present. This function tells how strongly field values at separated points are statistically linked.

Conditions and units

The operator order shown defines a Wightman function, not a time-ordered Feynman propagator. The vacuum is normalized; G has the units of the field squared and need not be a real classical covariance. For a scalar field in four-dimensional natural units it has mass dimension two. Coincident-point products are singular distributions requiring a regulator or renormalized definition. Spacelike correlations do not permit faster-than-light signalling.
