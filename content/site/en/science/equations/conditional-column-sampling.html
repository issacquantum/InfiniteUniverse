Conditional Length-Square Column Sampling

Equation

$$\Pr[j\mid i]=\frac{|A_{ij}|^2}{\|A_{i,*}\|_2^2}$$

Literal reading

The probability of column j given row i equals the squared modulus of entry A subscript i j divided by the squared Euclidean norm of row i.

Conceptual reading

After choosing a row, select an entry according to its squared magnitude within that row.

Symbols

\(A\) is a matrix, \(i\) the selected row and \(j\) a column index. \(A_{ij}\) is that entry, and \(A_{i,*}\) is the whole row. Single bars take complex modulus, double bars with subscript 2 take the Euclidean norm, and the superscript squares them. The vertical bar inside \(\Pr[j\mid i]\) means conditional on row i, not modulus.

Values, units and domains

The selected row must have positive squared norm. Numerator and denominator share the same squared entry units, so probabilities are dimensionless. Zero entries get zero probability; a zero row cannot use this conditional formula.

Meaning

The column probabilities sum to one within each nonzero row. Combined with length-square row sampling, this yields entry probabilities proportional to squared entry magnitude across the matrix.

Assumptions and limits

This formula assumes the row has already been selected. Efficient sampling still depends on data access or preprocessing, not just the probability identity.

Example

Within row (3,4), the squared row norm is 25. The two column probabilities are 9/25=0.36 and 16/25=0.64, which sum to one. Negative or complex entries use modulus squared and still produce nonnegative probabilities.

Source

Ewin Tang, A quantum-inspired classical algorithm for recommendation systems.
