Penrose Process

Equation

$$E_\infty < 0\quad \text{inside the ergosphere}$$

Literal Reading

E subscript infinity is less than zero inside the ergosphere.

Common Reading

The condition that allows rotational energy to be extracted from a Kerr black hole.

Symbols

\(E_\infty\): energy as measured by a distant observer.

Ergosphere: region outside the horizon where frame dragging prevents static observers.

Meaning of the Equation

Inside the ergosphere, one fragment can carry negative energy relative to infinity. The escaping fragment then leaves with extracted rotational energy.

Scope and Limits

The inequality states the possibility of negative energy states in the ergosphere. Turning that possibility into an efficient astrophysical extraction process requires additional dynamics, not just the inequality.

Conditions and units

\(E_\infty\) is the conserved Killing energy associated with stationary time translation, not the energy locally measured by an observer at the fragment. With the usual normalization it has units J. A future-directed physical particle still has positive local energy; the Killing energy can be negative because the stationary Killing vector becomes spacelike in the ergosphere. Only suitable trajectories have negative energy. In the extraction process that fragment falls into the hole and an allowed partner escapes.

Worked example or interpretation

If an incoming fragment has conserved energy 10 J, an allowed split with captured Killing energy −1 J leaves 11 J for the escaping partner by energy conservation. These illustrative values describe the accounting only; allowed trajectories and momentum conservation must also be satisfied.

Source

Sean M. Carroll, Lecture Notes on General Relativity.
