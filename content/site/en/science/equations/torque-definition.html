Torque about an Origin

Equation

$$\boldsymbol\tau=\mathbf r\times\mathbf F$$

Literal reading

Vector tau equals vector r cross vector F.

Conceptual reading

Torque measures the rotational effect of a force about a specified origin.

Symbols

\(\boldsymbol\tau\) is torque; \(\mathbf r\) is displacement from the origin to the force application point; \(\mathbf F\) is force. The cross denotes a vector product with the right-hand orientation convention.

Values, units and domains

Displacement is in m, force in N and torque in N·m. Torque is an axial vector, not scalar work, even though the dimensions agree.

Meaning

Only the force component perpendicular to the lever arm contributes. A force directed through the origin has zero torque about that origin.

Assumptions and limits

The origin must be specified. This definition alone does not assert that all components of torque equal a scalar moment of inertia times angular acceleration.

Example

A 10 N perpendicular force applied 0.2 m from the origin gives torque magnitude 2 N·m.
