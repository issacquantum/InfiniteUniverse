FLRW Particle Horizon

Equation

$$d_p(t)=a(t)\int_0^t\frac{c\,dt'}{a(t')}$$

Literal reading

D subscript p of t equals a of t times the integral from zero to t of c divided by a of t prime, with respect to t prime.

Conceptual reading

The particle horizon measures the greatest radial distance light could have covered since the assumed initial boundary, expressed as proper distance at time t.

Symbols

\(d_p\) is particle-horizon proper distance; p labels particle. \(a(t)\) is the FLRW scale factor; \(t\) is cosmic time; \(t^{\prime}\) is a dummy integration time, not a derivative. \(c\) is light speed and \(dt^{\prime}\) the time differential. The outer a is evaluated at the observation time; the denominator varies inside the integral.

Values, units and domains

c = 299792458 m/s exactly. Times are in s and distance in m. Scale-factor normalization cancels; one may use a dimensionless a with comoving distances in m. Require positive a on the integration interval.

Meaning

The integral accounts for the changing distance scale during propagation. It concerns past causal contact, not merely the instantaneous Hubble radius.

Assumptions and limits

Homogeneous isotropic FLRW geometry and radial proper distance on a constant-time slice. The lower limit assumes a past boundary at t = 0; another history requires another limit. A finite particle horizon requires convergence near that boundary.

Example

In an illustrative matter-dominated flat model with a proportional to t^(2/3), the integral gives d_p = 3ct. At a specified cosmic age, the horizon distance therefore exceeds c times that age because expansion occurred during light travel.

Source

Sean M. Carroll, Lecture Notes on General Relativity, cosmology chapter.
