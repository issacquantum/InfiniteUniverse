Radiative Temperature Gradient

Equation

$$\frac{dT}{dr}=-\frac{3\kappa\rho L}{16\pi ac r^2T^3}$$

Literal reading

Temperature decreases outward at a rate set by opacity, density, and the luminosity carried by radiation.

Symbols and units

T: K; r: m; kappa: Rosseland mean opacity, m²/kg; rho: kg/m³; L: radiative luminosity, W; a: J m⁻³ K⁻⁴; c: speed of light, m/s; pi: dimensionless. The gradient is K/m.

Physical meaning

Higher opacity requires a steeper gradient to transport the same luminosity locally.

Derivation

Radiative diffusion gives flux proportional to the negative temperature gradient, with coefficient 4acT³/(3 kappa rho). Multiply flux by spherical area and solve for the gradient.

Assumptions and limits

Optically thick, near local thermodynamic equilibrium, spherical diffusion. If L is total luminosity, this assumes radiation carries it all. Convection, conduction, and transparent surface layers require different transport treatment.

Worked example

Doubling opacity at fixed local T, rho, radius, and L doubles the required gradient magnitude. The full star will readjust, so this is a local comparison.

Source

Princeton: Equations of Stellar Structure
