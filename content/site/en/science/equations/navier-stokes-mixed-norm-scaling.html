Scaling of a Mixed Velocity Norm

Equation

$$\|\mathbf u_\lambda\|_{L^p_tL^q_x}=\lambda^{1-2/p-3/q}\|\mathbf u\|_{L^p_tL^q_x}$$

Literal reading

The L p in time, L q in space norm of u subscript lambda equals lambda to the power one minus two divided by p minus three divided by q, times that norm of u.

Conceptual reading

Velocity amplification competes with the shrinking space-time integration domain.

Symbols

\(\mathbf u\) is velocity and \(\mathbf u_\lambda(x,t)=\lambda\mathbf u(\lambda x,\lambda^2t)\) its parabolic rescaling. \(x\) is a three-dimensional position, \(t\) time and \(\lambda>0\) the scale factor. \(p,q\) are time and space integrability exponents, not pressure. Double bars with \(L^p_tL^q_x\) mean take the spatial L q norm and then the temporal L p norm. The superscript on lambda is the full scaling exponent.

Values, units and domains

Lambda is dimensionless; p and q range from 1 to infinity with reciprocal infinity interpreted as zero. For velocity in m/s the norm has units (m/s)·s^(1/p)·m^(3/q). The identity uses corresponding rescaled time intervals and the whole space.

Meaning

The norm is critical when 2/p + 3/q = 1: the scale factor then has exponent zero. Criticality is a scaling statement, not by itself a regularity theorem.

Assumptions and limits

Whole-space change of variables with finite norm, or the corresponding essential-supremum case. Arbitrary dilation is not a symmetry of a fixed unit torus. Regularity results need further hypotheses on exponents and solutions.

Example

For p = infinity and q = 3, the exponent is 1 − 0 − 1 = 0, so the norm is unchanged. For p = q = 4, it is −1/4, so doubling lambda multiplies the norm by 2^(−1/4).

Source

Charles L. Fefferman, Existence and Smoothness of the Navier-Stokes Equation.
