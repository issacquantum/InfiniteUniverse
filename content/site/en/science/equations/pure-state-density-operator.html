Pure-State Density Operator

Equation

$$\rho=|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|$$

Literal reading

Rho equals ket psi times bra psi: the outer product of the state with its adjoint.

Conceptual reading

A normalized pure state defines a rank-one projector.

Symbols

\(\rho\) is the density operator. \(|\psi\rangle\) is a normalized state vector; \(\langle\psi|\) its adjoint, or bra. Their juxtaposition is an outer product, not the scalar inner product obtained in the reverse order.

Values, units and domains

In a normalized finite-dimensional basis, the state coefficients and density-matrix entries are dimensionless. Psi has unit norm; rho has trace one and is positive. There is no universal value for its entries.

Meaning

The operator contains all measurement statistics of the pure state and is unchanged by multiplying the ket by a global phase.

Assumptions and limits

Pure normalized state. A statistical mixture generally cannot be represented by a single rank-one projector, and different ensembles can yield the same mixed density operator.

Example

For the qubit state |0⟩, rho in the basis {|0⟩, |1⟩} is the diagonal matrix with entries 1 and 0. Measurement in that basis gives outcome 0 with certainty.

Source

John Watrous, The Theory of Quantum Information (2018).
