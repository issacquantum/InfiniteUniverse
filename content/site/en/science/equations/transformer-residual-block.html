Transformer Residual Block

Equation

$$ y=\operatorname{LayerNorm}(x+\operatorname{Sublayer}(x)) $$

Literal Reading

y equals LayerNorm of x plus Sublayer of x.

Common Reading

A residual block adds a learned transformation back to the input so information and gradients can pass through deep networks more easily.

Symbols

\(x\): incoming representation.

\(\operatorname{Sublayer}(x)\): attention or feed-forward transformation.

\(\operatorname{LayerNorm}\): normalization that stabilizes activations.

Meaning of the Equation

This is a post-normalization residual block: normalization follows the residual addition. Pre-normalization architectures normalize the sublayer input instead. The displayed ordering is an architectural choice, not a universal formula for all transformers.

Conditions and units

\(y\) is the output representation, with the same shape as x and the sublayer output so the addition is defined. Layer normalization subtracts a feature mean and divides by the square root of feature variance plus a positive stabilizer, usually followed by learned scale and bias. It acts over specified feature dimensions, not automatically over every token or the batch. Representations are normally dimensionless; dropout and other implementation choices are omitted.

Worked example or interpretation

For a two-feature input (1,3) and zero sublayer output, the residual sum is (1,3). Its feature mean is 2 and variance is 1. With unit learned scale and zero bias, layer normalization gives (−1,1)/√(1+epsilon), where epsilon is its positive numerical stabilizer. Even a zero sublayer does not make this post-normalized block the identity.

Source

Vaswani et al., Attention Is All You Need.
