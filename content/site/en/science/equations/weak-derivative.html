Weak Derivative

Equation

$$\int_\Omega u\,\partial_i\varphi\,dx=-\int_\Omega v\varphi\,dx\quad(\varphi\in C_c^\infty(\Omega))$$

Literal Reading

The integral over Omega of u times the partial derivative of phi with respect to coordinate i, with volume element d x, equals minus the integral over Omega of v times phi with volume element d x, for every smooth compactly supported phi in Omega.

Common Reading

Move the derivative onto a test function.

Symbols

u, v: locally integrable functions; φ: smooth compactly supported test function; Ω: open domain.

Meaning of the Equation

If the identity holds for every test function, v is the weak derivative ∂ᵢu. Compact support removes boundary terms; boundary conditions in a PDE require separate treatment.

Conditions and units

The index i selects one of the d spatial coordinates and dx denotes d-dimensional volume. If coordinates have length units, v has the units of u divided by length, giving matching units on both sides. The space \(C_c^\infty(\Omega)\) consists of infinitely differentiable test functions supported away from the boundary. A distributional derivative need not be an ordinary function; this definition specifically asks for a locally integrable representative v.

Worked example or interpretation

For u(x)=|x| on (−1,1), the weak derivative is −1 to the left of zero and +1 to the right; its value at zero does not affect the integrals. Splitting the integral at zero and integrating by parts gives the displayed identity, even though u has no ordinary derivative at zero.

Source

John K. Hunter, Notes on Partial Differential Equations, Sobolev spaces.
