Fluctuation Field Sample

Equation

$$ \phi_\Lambda(\mathbf r,t)\propto \sum_{\mathbf{k}} \frac{e^{-(|\mathbf{k}|/\Lambda)^4}}{\sqrt{2\omega_{\mathbf{k}}}} \Big[ (Q_{\mathbf{k}}\cos(\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf r)+\widetilde Q_{\mathbf{k}}\sin(\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf r)) \cos(\omega_{\mathbf{k}}t) +(P_{\mathbf{k}}\cos(\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf r)+\widetilde P_{\mathbf{k}}\sin(\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf r)) \sin(\omega_{\mathbf{k}}t) \Big],\quad \omega_{\mathbf{k}}=\sqrt{|\mathbf{k}|^2+m^2},\quad m\approx\frac{1}{\xi} $$

Literal Reading

Phi subscript lambda of vector r and t is proportional to a sum over vector k. Each term is weighted by e raised to minus the fourth power of the magnitude of k divided by lambda, divided by the square root of twice omega subscript k. It multiplies a bracket: Q subscript k times cosine of k dot r plus Q tilde subscript k times sine of k dot r, all times cosine of omega subscript k t; plus P subscript k times cosine of k dot r plus P tilde subscript k times sine of k dot r, all times sine of omega subscript k t. Omega subscript k equals the square root of the magnitude of k squared plus m squared, and m is approximately one over xi.

Common Reading

A finite, cutoff, visualization-level sample of a free real scalar field vacuum.

Symbols

\(\phi_\Lambda(\mathbf r,t)\): the rendered cutoff field amplitude at position \(\mathbf r=(x,z)\) and time \(t\).

\(\mathbf{k}\): wave vector for a field mode.

\(\Lambda\): ultraviolet cutoff that damps short-wavelength modes for readability and stability.

\(\omega_{\mathbf{k}}\): mode angular frequency.

\(m\): mass-like parameter controlling correlation length.

\(\xi\): correlation length; larger \(\xi\) produces broader, smoother field structure.

\(Q_{\mathbf{k}},\widetilde Q_{\mathbf{k}},P_{\mathbf{k}},\widetilde P_{\mathbf{k}}\): independent standard Gaussian coefficients. They select one possible vacuum sample for the finite model.

Meaning of the Equation

A single visualization is one draw from a field distribution. The cutoff and modes control roughness, preventing the picture from pretending to show an infinite continuum literally.

Conditions and units

The rendering uses normalized coordinates, time and amplitude. With physical units restored, \(\mathbf k,\Lambda,m\) here are inverse lengths, \(\xi\) a length, and \(t\) is expressed as a length with \(c=1\); the mass-like \(m\) corresponds to physical mass times \(c/\hbar\). The proportionality suppresses the mode-measure normalization. This classical Gaussian visualization can illustrate symmetrized free-field fluctuations; it does not reproduce noncommuting quantum operators or give a literal trajectory of the vacuum.
