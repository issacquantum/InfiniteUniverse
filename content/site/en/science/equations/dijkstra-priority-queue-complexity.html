Dijkstra Priority Queue Complexity

Equation

$$ O((V+E)\log V) $$

Literal Reading

Big O of the quantity V plus E times log V.

Common Reading

Using a priority queue makes shortest-path exploration scale with graph size while paying a logarithmic cost for ordering candidates.

Symbols

\(V\): number of vertices or nodes.

\(E\): number of edges.

\(\log V\): cost of priority queue operations in common heap implementations.

Meaning of the Equation

The complexity depends on the data structure: a simple array, binary heap, or Fibonacci heap gives different bounds.

Conditions and units

This bound uses adjacency lists and a binary heap with suitable priority updates, with nonnegative edge weights. V and E count vertices and edges; the result bounds operation counts, not seconds. Logarithm bases differ only by a constant factor.

Worked example or interpretation

For a sparse family with E proportional to V, this bound has order V log V. For a dense family with E proportional to V², it has order V² log V. These are operation-count upper bounds for the stated heap implementation, not measured execution times.

Notation details

The outer O is an asymptotic upper bound and its argument is the entire product (V+E)log V. The logarithm is interpreted for nontrivial graphs; constant-size graphs are handled directly.
