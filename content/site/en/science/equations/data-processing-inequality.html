Data Processing Inequality

Equation

$$ D(\mathcal{E}(\rho)\Vert\mathcal{E}(\sigma)) \le D(\rho\Vert\sigma) $$

Literal Reading

D of E of rho relative to E of sigma is less than or equal to D of rho relative to sigma.

Common Reading

Applying the same quantum channel cannot increase quantum relative entropy between two states.

Symbols

\(D\): quantum relative entropy.

\(\mathcal{E}\): quantum channel.

\(\rho,\sigma\): density matrices being compared.

Meaning of the Equation

The channel can discard or blur distinguishability, but it cannot create extra distinguishability between \(\rho\) and \(\sigma\).

Conditions and units

\(\mathcal E\) must be completely positive and trace preserving. Relative entropy is measured in bits for base-two logs or nats for natural logs; it is infinite if the support condition fails. The statement concerns this distinguishability measure under a channel applied without conditioning on a selected outcome; postselection is not a trace-preserving channel.

Worked example or interpretation

A channel that discards its input and prepares the same fixed state maps both rho and sigma to identical outputs. Their output relative entropy is zero, which satisfies the inequality for any input pair. A reversible unitary channel instead preserves relative entropy.

Notation details

Double vertical bars separate the two arguments of relative entropy; they are not a norm here. Both state arguments are transformed by the same channel, and the inequality includes equality.

Source

John Watrous, The Theory of Quantum Information.
