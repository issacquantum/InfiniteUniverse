Gibbs State Density Matrix

Equation

$$ \rho_\beta=\frac{e^{-\beta H}}{Z},\qquad Z=\operatorname{Tr}(e^{-\beta H}),\qquad \beta=\frac{1}{k_B T} $$

Literal Reading

rho sub beta equals e to the minus beta H divided by Z, where Z equals the trace of e to the minus beta H and beta equals one over k sub B T.

Common Reading

The density matrix for a quantum system in thermal equilibrium at temperature \(T\).

Symbols

\(\rho_\beta\): thermal density matrix.

\(H\): Hamiltonian of the system.

\(\beta\): inverse temperature parameter.

\(Z\): partition function, which normalizes the state.

\(\mathrm{Tr}\): trace operation.

\(k_B\): Boltzmann constant.

\(T\): temperature.

Meaning of the Equation

The partition function \(Z\) normalizes the density matrix so its trace is one, and \(\beta\) sets the temperature scale.

Conditions and units

For the canonical ensemble, \(H\) is self-adjoint and \(e^{-\beta H}\) must have finite nonzero trace. \(H\) is in J, \(T\) in K, \(\beta\) in J⁻¹, and \(k_B=1.380649\times10^{-23}\) J/K exactly. Both \(Z\) and \(\rho_\beta\) are dimensionless. The formula assumes equilibrium at fixed temperature for the specified Hamiltonian; exchange of particles requires the appropriate chemical-potential term in a grand-canonical description.

Equivalent notation

$$\rho=\frac{e^{-\beta H}}{Z}$$Writing \(\rho\) instead of \(\rho_\beta\) leaves the temperature dependence implicit. Rho equals the exponential of minus beta H divided by Z.
