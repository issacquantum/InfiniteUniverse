Gibbs Free Energy

Equation

$$\Delta G=\Delta H-T\Delta S$$

Literal Reading

Delta G equals delta H minus T delta S.

Common Reading

This estimates whether a process is thermodynamically favored at constant temperature and pressure.

Symbols

  • \(\Delta G\): Gibbs free-energy change.
  • \(\Delta H\): enthalpy change.
  • \(T\): absolute temperature.
  • \(\Delta S\): entropy change.

Meaning of the Equation

\(\Delta G<0\) indicates spontaneity at constant temperature and pressure.

Conditions and units

Here \(\Delta\) means final minus initial and both states have the same temperature \(T\). \(G,H\) are energies in joules, \(S\) is entropy in J/K, and \(T\) is in kelvin; molar quantities instead use J/mol and J/(mol K) consistently. For a closed system at fixed temperature and pressure with only pressure-volume work, a spontaneous change lowers \(G\). This criterion gives no reaction rate and does not imply that an activation barrier is absent. If temperature changes, \(\Delta G=\Delta H-\Delta(TS)\), not generally the displayed constant-temperature form.

Worked example or interpretation

For illustrative changes at fixed temperature 300 K, ΔH=1000 J and ΔS=4 J/K give ΔG=−200 J. At fixed temperature and pressure, this negative change favours the process under the stated thermodynamic constraints; it does not determine its rate.

Source

David Tong, Statistical Physics, thermodynamic potentials.
