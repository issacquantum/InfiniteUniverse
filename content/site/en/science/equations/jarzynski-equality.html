Jarzynski Equality

Equation

$$\langle e^{-\beta W}\rangle=e^{-\beta\Delta F}$$

Literal Reading

The ensemble average of e raised to minus beta times W equals e raised to minus beta times delta F.

Common Reading

Many nonequilibrium work measurements can reveal an equilibrium free-energy difference.

Symbols

  • \(W\): work done in one trial.
  • \(\Delta F\): free-energy difference.
  • \(\beta\): inverse thermal energy, usually \(1/(k_BT)\).
  • \(\langle\cdot\rangle\): average over many trials.

Meaning of the Equation

The average is over repeated realizations of the same driving protocol, each starting in thermal equilibrium. W is work done on the system, the opposite sign to work done by the system in the first-law convention used elsewhere here. The free-energy difference refers to equilibrium states at the same temperature.

Conditions and units

Here \(F\) is Helmholtz free energy, \(\Delta F=F_{\rm final}-F_{\rm initial}\), and \(W\) and \(F\) have units J. With \(\beta=1/(k_BT)\) in J⁻¹ the exponents are dimensionless. The classical equality assumes canonical initial sampling and dynamics compatible with the equilibrium bath or Hamiltonian work protocol. Exponentially weighted averages can be dominated by rare trajectories, so a finite sample may estimate the result poorly. A quantum version needs an explicit definition of work measurement.

Reference

C. Jarzynski, A nonequilibrium equality for free energy differences.

Worked example or interpretation

For a quasistatic reversible isothermal protocol with no work fluctuations, every realization has W=ΔF and both sides equal exp(−βΔF). For a finite-rate protocol, substituting the mean work inside the exponential is generally incorrect: the exponential must be averaged over realizations.

Physical constants

kB=1.380649 × 10⁻²³ J/K exactly. NIST, CODATA 2022.
