Scalar Field Lagrangian

Equation

$$\mathcal{L}=-\frac{1}{2}\partial_\mu\phi\,\partial^\mu\phi-\frac{1}{2}m^2\phi^2$$

Literal Reading

Script L equals minus one half times partial subscript mu of phi times partial superscript mu of phi, minus one half m squared phi squared, summing over mu.

Common Reading

This is the free scalar-field Lagrangian: one term tracks spacetime variation of the field and the other gives the mass term.

Symbols

  • \(\mathcal{L}\): Lagrangian density.
  • \(\phi\): scalar field.
  • \(\partial_\mu\): spacetime derivative.
  • \(m\): mass parameter.

Meaning of the Equation

No interaction potential is shown here. Interaction terms would be added separately, for example as an additional \(-V_{\rm int}(\phi)\).

Conditions and units

This real free scalar uses \(\eta_{\mu\nu}=\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,1,1)\) and \(\hbar=c=1\). Raising a derivative index uses this metric, with \(\mu=0,1,2,3\). Consequently \(\mathcal L=\tfrac12\dot\phi^2-\tfrac12|\nabla\phi|^2-\tfrac12m^2\phi^2\), with positive time-kinetic energy. The field equation is \((\Box-m^2)\phi=0\), where \(\Box=-\partial_t^2+\nabla^2\). In four dimensions, \(\phi\) has mass dimension one, m dimension one, and \(\mathcal L\) dimension four. An interaction potential enters as \(-V_{\rm int}(\phi)\). David Tong, Quantum Field Theory, section 1.1.1 writes the equivalent positive contracted kinetic term with the opposite \((+---)\) signature; changing signature changes that sign, not the physical kinetic energy.

Worked example or interpretation

For a spatially uniform field, the gradient term vanishes and the Lagrangian density becomes one half the squared time derivative minus one half m²φ². Its Euler-Lagrange equation is an oscillator equation with angular frequency m in these natural units. This example also checks the positive sign of the time-kinetic term.
