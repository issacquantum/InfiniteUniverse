Uniform Circular Motion

Equation

$$\begin{aligned}\mathbf r(t)&=R(\cos\omega t,0,\sin\omega t),\\\mathbf v(t)&=R\omega(-\sin\omega t,0,\cos\omega t),\\\mathbf a(t)&=-\omega^2\mathbf r(t),\qquad F=m\omega^2R.\end{aligned}$$

Literal Reading

Vector r of t equals R times the components cosine omega t, zero, sine omega t. Vector v equals R omega times minus sine omega t, zero, cosine omega t. Vector a equals minus omega squared times vector r; F equals m omega squared R.

Common Reading

Constant speed around a circle still requires an inward acceleration and net force.

Symbols

  • \(R,\omega,t\): Positive radius (m), constant angular speed (rad/s), time (s). Radians are dimensionless; omega t is an angle.
  • \(\mathbf r,\mathbf v,\mathbf a\): Position from the center (m), velocity (m/s), acceleration (m/s²). Components are x, y, z.
  • \(m,F\): Constant mass (kg), inward net-force magnitude (N).

Meaning of the Equation

Differentiate position once for velocity and twice for acceleration. The y component is zero in all three vectors. Velocity is perpendicular to radius; acceleration is antiparallel to radius. Since v = omega R, acceleration magnitude is v²/R. The embedded model uses R = 1.95 scene units and omega = 0.62 per animation-time unit. Arrow lengths are illustrative, not calibrated physical magnitudes.

Scope and Limits

Prescribed circular path centered at the origin in the x-z plane. It does not solve a gravitational force law. An actual circle could be maintained by tension, contact or gravity under suitable conditions.
