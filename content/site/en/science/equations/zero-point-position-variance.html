Zero-Point Position Variance

Equation

$$ \langle 0|\hat{x}^2|0\rangle = \frac{\hbar}{2m\omega} $$

Literal Reading

The expectation value of x squared in the ground state equals h bar over two m omega.

Common Reading

Even in the harmonic oscillator ground state, position has nonzero spread: \(\langle x^2\rangle_0=\hbar/(2m\omega)\).

Symbols

\(|0\rangle\): the oscillator ground state.

\(\hat{x}\): the position operator.

\(\hbar\): reduced Planck constant.

\(m\): oscillator mass.

\(\omega\): angular frequency of the oscillator.

Meaning of the Equation

Even the lowest-energy oscillator cannot sit at one exact position. Quantum uncertainty leaves an irreducible spread, which is different from thermal motion caused by heat.

Conditions and units

This is the one-dimensional ideal oscillator with positive mass m in kg and angular frequency \(\omega\) in rad/s, centered at x = 0 so that \(\langle x\rangle=0\). The second moment is therefore the variance. \(\hbar\) has units J s and the result m². A displaced oscillator’s variance has the same form, but its second moment also contains the squared mean position. No thermal excitation is required for the ground-state spread.

Worked example or interpretation

For illustrative m=10⁻²⁶ kg and ω=10¹⁴ rad/s, the position variance is about 5.273 × 10⁻²³ m² and its square root is about 7.261 × 10⁻¹² m. The square root, not the variance itself, is the position standard deviation; ħ≈1.054571817 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s.
