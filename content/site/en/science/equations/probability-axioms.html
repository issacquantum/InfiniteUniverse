Probability Axioms

Equation

$$P(\Omega)=1,\quad P(A)\geq0,\quad P\!\left(\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}A_n\right)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}P(A_n)$$

Literal Reading

P of omega equals one; P of A is nonnegative; probability of the countable union equals the sum of probabilities.

Common Reading

Total probability is one, and disjoint alternatives add.

Symbols

Ω: sample space; A, Aₙ: measurable events; P: probability measure.

Meaning of the Equation

The final identity requires pairwise disjoint events. It does not assert uncountable additivity.

Conditions and units

The index \(n\) runs over positive integers and \(\Omega\) is the full sample space. \(P\) is defined on a sigma-algebra of measurable events, closed under complements and countable unions. These axioms apply to dimensionless probabilities and imply \(0\le P(A)\le1\). Additivity in the displayed equality applies only when distinct \(A_n\) have empty intersection.

Worked example or interpretation

For a fair die, the whole sample space has probability one and each single face has probability 1/6. The disjoint events {1,2} and {3} have probabilities 2/6 and 1/6, so their union has probability 3/6. Overlapping events cannot be added this way without accounting for their intersection.

Notation details

The normalization statement fixes the probability of the full space. Nonnegativity applies to every measurable event. The union symbol joins a countable family of pairwise disjoint events; the matching infinite sum adds their probabilities, with n from 1 upward. These three requirements jointly define a probability measure.
