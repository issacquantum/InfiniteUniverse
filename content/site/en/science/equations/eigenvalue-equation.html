Eigenvalue Equation

Equation

$$A\mathbf{v}=\lambda\mathbf{v}$$

Literal Reading

A v equals lambda v.

Common Reading

A nonzero eigenvector is mapped to a scalar multiple of itself.

Symbols

  • \(A\): the transformation matrix.
  • \(\mathbf{v}\): an eigenvector.
  • \(\lambda\): the eigenvalue, or scale factor.

Meaning of the Equation

For real eigenvectors and eigenvalues, a negative eigenvalue reverses orientation and a zero eigenvalue maps the vector to zero. In a complex vector space, the scalar can also change phase. Eigenvectors identify invariant one-dimensional subspaces, useful for studying modes and observables.

Conditions and units

\(A\) is a linear operator on a vector space and \(\mathbf v\ne0\). The eigenvalue has the operator’s units; its numerical value depends on the operator. Not every real matrix has real eigenvectors or a complete eigenbasis. For unbounded operators, the eigenvector must belong to the operator domain.

Worked example or interpretation

For the dimensionless diagonal matrix with entries 2 and 3, vector (1,0) maps to (2,0), so it is an eigenvector with eigenvalue 2. Vector (1,1) maps to (2,3), which is not a scalar multiple of (1,1), so that vector is not an eigenvector.
