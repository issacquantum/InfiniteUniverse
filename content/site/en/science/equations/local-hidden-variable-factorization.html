Local Hidden Variable Factorization

Equation

$$ P(A,B|a,b,\lambda)=P(A|a,\lambda)P(B|b,\lambda) $$

Literal Reading

P of A comma B given a comma b comma lambda equals P of A given a comma lambda times P of B given b comma lambda.

Common Reading

A local hidden-variable model factorizes distant outcomes once the local settings and shared hidden variable are fixed.

Symbols

\(A,B\): the two measurement outcomes recorded by the distant observers.

\(a,b\): the detector settings or measurement directions chosen on each side.

\(\lambda\): hidden information shared by the pair before measurement.

\(P(A,B|a,b,\lambda)\): the joint probability of getting both outcomes after fixing the settings and hidden variable.

\(P(A|a,\lambda)P(B|b,\lambda)\): the product form required by locality.

Meaning of the Equation

This factorization expresses Bell local causality conditional on a sufficiently complete shared variable \(\lambda\). Measurement independence is a separate assumption, \(\rho(\lambda\mid a,b)=\rho(\lambda)\), used with factorization when deriving Bell inequalities; it does not follow from the displayed equation. All probabilities are dimensionless and normalized over their outcomes.

Worked example or interpretation

At fixed settings and fixed λ, suppose the local probabilities for the chosen outcomes are 0.8 and 0.3. Factorization assigns joint probability 0.24. Averaging over a shared λ can still produce correlations, even though outcomes factorize conditionally.

Source

Clauser, Horne, Shimony and Holt (1969), Proposed Experiment to Test Local Hidden-Variable Theories.
