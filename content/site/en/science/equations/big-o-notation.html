Big O Notation

Equation

$$ f(n)=O(g(n)) $$

Literal Reading

f of n equals O of g of n.

Common Reading

The growth of \(f(n)\) is bounded above by a constant multiple of \(g(n)\) for sufficiently large inputs.

Symbols

\(f(n)\): resource use being analyzed, such as time or memory.

\(g(n)\): comparison growth function.

\(n\): input size.

Meaning of the Equation

Big-O is an asymptotic upper bound, not an exact runtime.

Conditions and units

Precisely, there are constants \(C>0\) and \(n_0\) such that \(|f(n)|\le C|g(n)|\) for all \(n\ge n_0\). Input size is dimensionless; cost units belong to the functions and the comparison constant. The notation alone says nothing about average versus worst-case cost.

Worked example or interpretation

For n≥1, 3n+2≤5n. Thus f(n)=3n+2 is O(n), with comparison constant 5 and threshold 1. It is also O(n²), so an upper bound need not be the tightest possible description.

Source

MIT 6.006, Introduction to Algorithms.
