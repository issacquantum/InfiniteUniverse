Universe Age Integral

Equation

$$t_0 = \int_{0}^{\infty} \frac{dz}{(1+z)H(z)}$$

Literal Reading

t subscript zero equals the integral from zero to infinity of d z divided by the product of one plus z and H of z.

Common Reading

This computes the age of an FLRW universe from its expansion history \(H(z)\).

Symbols

\(t_0\): present age of the universe.

\(z\): redshift.

\(H(z)\): Hubble parameter as a function of redshift.

\(\int_0^\infty\): integration from the present toward a vanishing scale factor in the assumed cosmological model, not merely the earliest observable epoch.

Meaning of the Equation

The result is model-dependent because it depends on the assumed expansion history and cosmological parameters.

Conditions and units

The redshift z is dimensionless, H has units s⁻¹, and the integral gives seconds. It follows from \(dt=-dz/[(1+z)H(z)]\) on a monotonically expanding FLRW branch with \(1+z=a_0/a\). Interpreting it as a finite age since a Big Bang requires the integral to converge and the model to extend to \(a\to0\). Bounces, contracting phases, or a past-eternal model require different limits or branches.

Worked example or interpretation

In the ideal spatially flat matter-only model, H(z)=H₀(1+z)^(3/2). The displayed integral then gives t₀=2/(3H₀), two thirds of the Hubble time. Adding radiation or a cosmological constant changes the expansion history and the integral.

Source

David Tong, Cosmology, The Expanding Universe.
