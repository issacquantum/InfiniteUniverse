Stationary Action Condition

Equation

$$\delta S=0$$

Literal reading

Delta S equals zero, where delta means a first variation of the action.

Conceptual reading

A physical trajectory is stationary under the allowed infinitesimal variations of the path.

Symbols

\(S\) is the action, the time integral of the Lagrangian along a path. \(\delta\) is a variation operator: it extracts the first-order change when the path is perturbed. The equality to zero requires that change to vanish for every admissible variation, not that the action itself be zero.

Values, units and domains

Action and its variation have units J·s when the variation parameter is dimensionless. Zero is not a universal action value. The set of admissible paths is fixed by the boundary conditions and constraints.

Meaning

For a regular mechanical Lagrangian and fixed endpoints, integrating the first variation by parts produces the Euler-Lagrange equations. Stationarity need not be a minimum.

Assumptions and limits

Classical variational formulation with appropriate fixed-endpoint or other stated boundary conditions. Endpoint terms must not be discarded when those conditions do not remove them.

Example

For a free particle with fixed starting and ending positions and times, the constant-velocity path has zero first variation of the action. A small allowed bend changes the action only at second order around that path.

Source

William Rowan Hamilton, On a General Method in Dynamics (1834) and Second Essay (1835).
