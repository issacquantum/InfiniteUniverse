Planck Energy Quantum

Equation

$$ E = h\nu $$

Literal Reading

E equals h times nu.

Common Reading

The energy of one quantum equals Planck's constant times the radiation frequency.

Symbols

\(E\): the energy carried by one quantum of radiation.

\(h\): Planck's constant, the basic quantum of action.

\(\nu\): the frequency of the radiation.

Meaning of the Equation

Frequency sets the size of each light quantum. This is why blue or ultraviolet photons can trigger processes that red photons cannot, even when the total brightness is large.

Conditions and units

\(h=6.62607015\times10^{-34}\) J s exactly, \(\nu\) is frequency in Hz, and \(E\) is photon energy in J in the same observation frame. With angular frequency \(\omega=2\pi\nu\), the equivalent formula is \(E=\hbar\omega\). Intensity changes the photon flux; it does not change individual photon energy at fixed frequency. Multiphoton processes lie beyond a single-photon threshold argument.

Worked example or interpretation

At frequency 5 × 10¹⁴ Hz, a photon has energy 3.313035075 × 10⁻¹⁹ J, approximately 2.068 eV. Doubling the number of photons at that frequency doubles total energy but leaves each photon’s energy unchanged.
