Language Modeling Objective

Equation

$$ L_{\rm LM}=-\sum_t \log p_\theta(x_t\mid x_{1:t-1}) $$

Literal Reading

L subscript L M equals minus the sum over t of the logarithm of p subscript theta of x subscript t, conditioned on the sequence from x subscript one through x subscript t minus one.

Common Reading

The model is trained to predict the next token from context.

Symbols

\(x_t\): token at position \(t\).

\(x_{1:t-1}\): previous context tokens.

\(p_\theta\): probability distribution produced by parameters \(\theta\).

Meaning of the Equation

The objective rewards probability distributions that anticipate text continuations. Fluent next-token prediction can still be wrong, ungrounded, or unsupported.

Conditions and units

\(L_{\rm LM}\) is the negative log-likelihood of the observed token sequence, summed over its positions \(t\). The first position uses the model’s empty or start-token context. Natural logarithms give a dimensionless loss in nats; averaging over positions gives a per-token loss. Tokens may be words, word fragments, punctuation, or other encoded units. The probabilities must be normalized over the vocabulary; a zero probability for an observed token gives infinite loss.

Worked example or interpretation

If a two-token observed sequence receives conditional probabilities 0.5 and 0.25, its probability is 0.125. The summed loss is −ln(0.5)−ln(0.25)=−ln(0.125)≈2.0794 nats, or about 1.0397 nats per token after averaging.

Notation details

The colon in the context subscript denotes a sequence slice from position 1 through t−1. The conditioning bar separates the current observed token from that preceding context. LM labels language modeling; the minus sign applies to the full sum.

Source

Goodfellow, Bengio and Courville, Deep Learning.
