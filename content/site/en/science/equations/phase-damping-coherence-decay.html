Phase Damping Coherence Decay

Equation

$$ \rho_{01}(t) = \rho_{01}(0)\, e^{-t/T_2} $$

Literal Reading

rho sub zero one of t equals rho sub zero one of zero times e to the minus t over T two.

Common Reading

A simple model of dephasing: phase information fades over time even when population probabilities do not necessarily change.

Symbols

\(\rho_{01}(t)\): the off-diagonal element of the density matrix at time \(t\).

\(\rho_{01}(0)\): the initial coherence.

\(e^{-t/T_2}\): exponential decay factor.

\(T_2\): dephasing time.

Meaning of the Equation

The form \(\rho_{01}(t)=\rho_{01}(0)e^{-t/T_2}\) is equivalent to \(e^{-\gamma t}\) with \(\gamma=1/T_2\). It describes coherence decay, not population loss.

Conditions and units

This exponential assumes a constant decay rate in a chosen basis and an interaction picture that removes coherent phase rotation. Density-matrix elements are dimensionless; \(t,T_2\) are in seconds and \(\gamma\) in s⁻¹. For pure phase damping, populations remain fixed. More generally transverse relaxation can include population relaxation, with \(1/T_2=1/(2T_1)+1/T_\phi\) in the standard Markovian qubit model; \(T_1\) is population-relaxation time and \(T_\phi\) pure-dephasing time. Not every environment produces exponential decay.

Worked example or interpretation

With initial coherence 0.5 and T₂=2 s, at t=2 s the coherence is 0.5/e≈0.1839. At t=4 s it is 0.5/e²≈0.0677. In pure phase damping the diagonal populations remain unchanged while these off-diagonal terms decay.

Notation details

The indices 01 select the row-zero, column-one coherence in the chosen qubit basis; they are not a decimal number. The exponential base e acts on the dimensionless ratio −t/T₂, with t≥0 and T₂>0.
