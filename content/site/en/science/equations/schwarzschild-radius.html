Schwarzschild Radius

Equation

$$r_s = \frac{2GM}{c^2}$$

Literal Reading

r sub s equals 2GM over c to the power of 2.

Common Reading

The Schwarzschild radius marks the horizon size for a non-rotating black hole.

Symbols

\(r_s\): Schwarzschild radius.

\(G\): Newton's gravitational constant.

\(M\): black hole mass.

\(c\): speed of light.

Meaning of the Equation

This is the horizon radius for a nonrotating, uncharged Schwarzschild black hole.

Scope and Limits

This radius applies to the ideal Schwarzschild solution: spherical, nonrotating, uncharged, and isolated. Real astrophysical black holes rotate, so this is the clean baseline rather than the whole story.

Conditions and units

With M in kg, \(G\approx6.67430\times10^{-11}\) m³ kg⁻¹ s⁻² and \(c=299792458\) m/s, \(r_s\) is in meters. It is an areal coordinate radius, whose sphere has area \(4\pi r_s^2\), not a proper radial distance. Assigning this length to a star’s mass does not imply the star already has a horizon if its material surface lies outside it.

Worked example or interpretation

For an illustrative mass of 10³⁰ kg, the radius is about 1485.23 m. Doubling that mass doubles the radius. A material object of the same mass with a much larger surface radius is not thereby a Schwarzschild black hole.

Source

Sean M. Carroll, Lecture Notes on General Relativity.
