Adam Optimizer Update

Equation

$$ \theta_{t+1}=\theta_t-\eta\frac{\hat m_t}{\sqrt{\hat v_t}+\epsilon} $$

Literal Reading

Theta subscript t plus one equals theta subscript t minus eta times m hat subscript t, divided by the sum of the square root of v hat subscript t and epsilon.

Common Reading

Adam uses running estimates of the gradient’s first moment and second raw moment, with bias corrections; the second raw moment is not the variance.

Symbols

\(\hat m_t\): bias-corrected first moment estimate of the gradient.

\(\hat v_t\): bias-corrected second moment estimate.

\(\eta\): learning rate.

\(\epsilon\): small constant preventing division by zero.

Meaning of the Equation

Adam changes each parameter with a step that reflects both recent gradient direction and the running squared gradient magnitude. That is why it can move differently across parameters instead of using one identical learning rate everywhere.

Conditions and units

\(\theta_t\) is the parameter vector at iteration \(t\); \(t\) is an integer, not physical time. Operations, including the square root and division, are componentwise. With dimensionless parameters and loss, every quantity is dimensionless. More generally, \(\epsilon\) has gradient units and \(\eta\) sets the parameter-step units. Hyperparameters and moment estimates are algorithm-dependent; the update alone does not guarantee convergence.

Worked example or interpretation

For one dimensionless parameter, take theta=1, learning rate 0.01, corrected first moment 2, corrected second moment 4, and epsilon=10⁻⁸. The update is 1−0.01×2/(2+10⁻⁸), approximately 0.99. These are illustrative current estimates, not universal hyperparameters.

Notation details

The hat marks bias correction, the square root acts on each nonnegative second-moment component, and the denominator is the sum of that root and epsilon. Index t labels the current iteration, while t+1 labels the updated parameter.

Source

Kingma and Ba, Adam: A Method for Stochastic Optimization.
