Hamilton's Equations

Equation

$$\dot q=\frac{\partial H}{\partial p},\qquad\dot p=-\frac{\partial H}{\partial q}$$

Literal Reading

Q dot equals the partial derivative of H with respect to p; p dot equals minus the partial derivative of H with respect to q.

Common Reading

The Hamiltonian determines how coordinates and canonical momenta evolve.

Symbols

  • \(q,p\): Canonical coordinate and conjugate momentum. For Cartesian position these use m and kg m/s.
  • \(H(q,p,t)\): Hamiltonian in J, obtained by the Legendre transform \(H=p\dot q-L\) when invertible.
  • \(\dot q,\dot p\): Time derivatives, in m/s and N for Cartesian coordinates.
  • \(\partial/\partial p,\partial/\partial q\): Partial derivatives with all other arguments held fixed; t is time in seconds.

Meaning of the Equation

For \(H=p^2/(2m)+kq^2/2\), with mass m (kg) and stiffness k (N/m), the equations give \(\dot q=p/m\) and \(\dot p=-kq\). Together they reproduce the spring equation. A phase-space point specifies position and momentum; its trajectory is not just a curve in ordinary space. Canonical momentum can differ from mass times velocity, for example with electromagnetic coupling. In the Legendre transform, L denotes the Lagrangian (J).

Scope and Limits

Canonical variables and a regular Hamiltonian formulation. H is conserved if it has no explicit time dependence. Its identification with total mechanical energy requires the appropriate time-independent coordinate and interaction assumptions.
