Pure State Hilbert Normalization

Equation

$$ |\psi\rangle\in\mathcal{H},\qquad \langle\psi|\psi\rangle=1 $$

Literal Reading

Ket psi belongs to Hilbert space H and the inner product of psi with itself equals one.

Common Reading

A pure quantum state is a normalized vector in Hilbert space.

Symbols

\(|\psi\rangle\): pure quantum state vector.

\(\mathcal{H}\): Hilbert space of possible states.

\(\langle\psi|\psi\rangle\): inner product of the state with itself.

\(1\): normalization condition ensuring total probability is one.

Meaning of the Equation

A pure state vector must have unit length in Hilbert space. The squared amplitudes then form a valid probability distribution for measurements.

Conditions and units

The norm and total probability are dimensionless. Probabilities are squared moduli of expansion coefficients in an orthonormal basis, not plain squares of complex amplitudes. Vectors differing only by a global phase represent the same pure physical state. Continuous position representations require their integration measure when normalizing.

Worked example or interpretation

The qubit vector with coefficients 1/√2 and i/√2 has squared norm 1/2+1/2=1. Using ordinary complex squares would incorrectly give 1/2−1/2=0; the inner product conjugates the bra coefficients and therefore uses squared moduli.

Source

John Watrous, The Theory of Quantum Information.
