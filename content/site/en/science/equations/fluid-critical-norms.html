Critical Norms and Energy Scaling

Equation

$$\|\mathbf u_\lambda\|_{L^p_tL^q_x}=\lambda^{1-2/p-3/q}\|\mathbf u\|_{L^p_tL^q_x},\qquad\|\mathbf u_\lambda(t)\|_2^2=\lambda^{-1}\|\mathbf u(\lambda^2t)\|_2^2$$

Literal Reading

The L p in time, L q in space norm of u lambda equals lambda to the power one minus two over p minus three over q, times the same norm of u. The squared L two norm of u lambda at t equals lambda to minus one times the squared L two norm of u at lambda squared t.

Common Reading

A critical norm is unchanged by Navier-Stokes rescaling, while kinetic energy decreases under concentration.

Symbols

  • \(\mathbf u_\lambda(x,t)=\lambda\mathbf u(\lambda x,\lambda^2t)\): Rescaled velocity, with scaling factor lambda greater than zero.
  • \(p,q\): Time and space integrability exponents, not pressure.
  • \(L^p_tL^q_x\): Mixed norm: take the spatial L q norm, then the temporal L p norm.
  • \(\|\cdot\|_2\): Spatial L two norm.
  • \(t\): Time in the rescaled solution.

Meaning of the Equation

The velocity contributes one power of the scale factor, time integration contributes minus two over p, and three-dimensional spatial integration contributes minus three over q. Criticality means the total exponent is zero. Thus energy can stay bounded during concentration without controlling a critical norm. The critical relation alone is not a regularity theorem; such results require additional hypotheses on the solution and the exponents.

Scope and Limits

These identities use the whole space and correspondingly rescaled time intervals. Infinite exponents are interpreted with their reciprocal equal to zero. Arbitrary rescaling is not a symmetry of a fixed unit torus.
