Time-Dependent Hubble Parameter

Equation

$$H(t) = \frac{\dot{a}(t)}{a(t)}$$

Literal Reading

H of t equals a dot of t divided by a of t.

Common Reading

The Hubble parameter is the fractional growth rate of the scale factor: how fast \(a(t)\) changes relative to its current size.

Symbols

\(H(t)\): Hubble parameter at time \(t\).

\(a(t)\): scale factor as a function of time.

\(\dot{a}(t)\): time derivative of the scale factor.

Meaning of the Equation

The Hubble parameter measures expansion per unit scale at a given cosmic time. It is not a fixed speed; it changes as the contents and geometry of the universe evolve.

Conditions and units

The overdot is a derivative with respect to cosmic proper time \(t\), in seconds. For \(a>0\), \(H\) has units s⁻¹ regardless of the scale-factor normalization: positive means expansion, negative contraction, and zero an instant of stationary scale. This kinematic definition does not determine acceleration or an expansion history without dynamics.

Worked example or interpretation

If a(t) is proportional to t^(2/3) on an expanding matter-only flat solution with t>0, differentiation gives H=2/(3t). The scale grows while its fractional growth rate decreases. The proportionality normalization cancels in the ratio.

Source

David Tong, Cosmology, The Expanding Universe.
