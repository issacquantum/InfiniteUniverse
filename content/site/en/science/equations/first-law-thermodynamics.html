First Law of Thermodynamics

Equation

$$dU=\delta Q-\delta W$$

Literal Reading

dU equals heat added minus work done by the system.

Common Reading

The internal energy changes according to heat entering the system and work leaving the system.

Symbols

  • \(dU\): change in internal energy.
  • \(\delta Q\): heat added to the system.
  • \(\delta W\): work done by the system.

Meaning of the Equation

Heat and work are two ways energy crosses a system boundary. The first law keeps the bookkeeping straight: internal energy rises or falls according to that exchange.

Conditions and units

\(U,Q,W\) have energy units (J). The differential \(dU\) is exact because internal energy is a state function; \(\delta Q,\delta W\) denote path-dependent transfers. This form describes a closed system with no net changes in bulk kinetic or potential energy. Open systems require mass-flow energy terms; work done on the system has the opposite sign convention.

Worked example or interpretation

If a closed system receives 100 J of heat and performs 40 J of work on its surroundings, its internal energy increases by 60 J. Work done on the system would have the opposite sign in this convention.

Source

David Tong, Statistical Physics, thermodynamics.
