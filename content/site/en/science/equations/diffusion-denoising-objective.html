Diffusion Denoising Objective

Equation

$$ L_{\rm simple}=\mathbb{E}_{t,x_0,\epsilon}\!\left[\|\epsilon-\epsilon_\theta(x_t,t)\|^2\right] $$

Literal Reading

L subscript simple equals the expectation over t, x subscript zero and epsilon of the squared norm of epsilon minus epsilon subscript theta evaluated at x subscript t and t.

Common Reading

A diffusion model learns to reverse a gradual noising process by predicting how to denoise each intermediate state.

Symbols

\(x_0\): original clean data.

\(x_t\): noised data at timestep \(t\).

\(\epsilon\): sampled noise.

\(\epsilon_\theta\): neural network prediction of that noise.

Meaning of the Equation

Training teaches the model which direction moves a noisy sample back toward the data distribution. The process is gradual: each step removes part of the corruption.

Conditions and units

\(\theta\) denotes network parameters, \(t\) a sampled diffusion step, and \(\mathbb E\) the average over the specified step, data and noise distributions. The norm is Euclidean. Standardized data and Gaussian noise are dimensionless, giving a dimensionless mean-squared loss. The forward noise schedule defines \(x_t\); the loss alone is not a sampler and does not guarantee exact reversal or monotonically less noise on each stochastic sample path.
