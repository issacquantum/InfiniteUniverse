Arrhenius Equation

Equation

$$k=Ae^{-E_a/(RT)}$$

Literal Reading

k equals A times e raised to minus E subscript a divided by the product R T.

Common Reading

Reaction rate usually increases with temperature because more molecules can cross the activation barrier.

Symbols

  • \(k\): rate constant.
  • \(A\): prefactor related to collision or attempt frequency.
  • \(E_a\): activation energy.
  • \(R\): gas constant.
  • \(T\): absolute temperature.

Meaning of the Equation

This is an empirical activated-process approximation; \(A\) and \(E_a\) can depend on mechanism and temperature regime.

Units and scope

\(T\) is in kelvins, \(E_a\) in J/mol and \(R\) in J/(mol K), so the exponent is dimensionless. \(A\) has the same units as \(k\); those units depend on reaction order. Neither is a universal frequency. The formula describes a fitted rate constant over a specified temperature range, not the reaction rate without concentration data.
