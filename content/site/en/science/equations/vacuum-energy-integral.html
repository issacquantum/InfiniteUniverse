Vacuum Energy Integral

Equation

$$\rho_{\mathrm{vac}}\sim \frac{1}{2}\int\frac{d^3k}{(2\pi)^3}\hbar\omega_k$$

Literal Reading

Rho subscript vacuum is formally of order one half times the integral of d cubed k divided by the cube of two pi, multiplied by h-bar times omega subscript k.

Common Reading

The formal zero point energy contribution of quantum fields.

Symbols

\(\rho_{\mathrm{vac}}\): vacuum energy density.

\(k\): wave vector; the corresponding momentum is \(\hbar k\).

\(\omega_k\): mode frequency.

\(\hbar\): reduced Planck constant.

Meaning of the Equation

The formal sum over zero-point modes diverges without a cutoff or renormalization. When gravity is included, this becomes part of the cosmological-constant problem.

Conditions and units

The expression represents one bosonic degree of freedom; field multiplicities and fermionic contributions require their own counting and signs. \(d^3k\) is wave-vector volume in m⁻³, \(\omega_k\) angular frequency in s⁻¹, and \(\hbar\omega_k\) energy in J, giving energy density J/m³. For relativistic high-frequency modes, a sharp wave-number cutoff produces a quartic divergence. That regulator-dependent bare term alone is not a prediction of the measured cosmological constant; renormalized gravitational parameters and a consistent stress tensor are needed.

Worked example or interpretation

For a massless mode with ωk=c|k| and a spherical cutoff |k|≤K, carrying out the angular integral and then the radial integral gives ħcK⁴/(16π²). K is an imposed wave-number cutoff in m⁻¹, not a measured cosmological parameter. Here ħ≈1.054571817 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s and c=299792458 m/s exactly.
