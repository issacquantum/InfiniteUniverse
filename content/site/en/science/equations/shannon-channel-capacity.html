Shannon Channel Capacity

Equation

$$ C=\max_{p(x)} I(X;Y) $$

Literal Reading

C equals the maximum, over input distributions p of x, of the mutual information I between X and Y.

Common Reading

Channel capacity is the maximum reliable information rate.

Symbols

\(C\): channel capacity, usually measured in bits per channel use.

\(p(x)\): probability distribution chosen for the channel input.

\(I(X;Y)\): mutual information between sent and received variables.

Meaning of the Equation

If written as \(C=\max_{p(x)}I(X;Y)\), this is the capacity of a general discrete memoryless channel. The formula \(B\log_2(1+\mathrm{SNR})\) is for an AWGN channel.

Conditions and units

For a finite-alphabet discrete memoryless channel, the conditional law \(p(y\mid x)\) is fixed while \(p(x)\) is optimized. X is the input and Y the output. Base-two logarithms give bits per channel use, achievable asymptotically with arbitrarily small error at rates below capacity, not necessarily in one use. In the separate bandwidth formula, B is bandwidth in Hz, SNR a dimensionless signal-to-noise power ratio, and capacity is in bits/s under additive white Gaussian noise and a power constraint.
