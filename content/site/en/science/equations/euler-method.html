Euler Method

Equation

$$ y_{n+1}=y_n+h f(t_n,y_n) $$

Literal Reading

y sub n plus 1 equals y sub n plus h f of t sub n comma y sub n.

Common Reading

This advances a system forward using a local derivative as an approximation to the next state.

Symbols

\(y_n\): current state.

\(y_{n+1}\): next state.

\(h\): step size.

\(f(t_n,y_n)\): derivative or slope at the current point.

Meaning of the Equation

Euler assumes the derivative stays nearly constant during one small step. Accuracy depends on making the step short enough that the true curve does not bend too much.

Conditions and units

\(n\) is a step index, \(t_n=t_0+nh\), and \(f\) is the right-hand side of \(dy/dt=f(t,y)\). The product \(hf\) has the same units as \(y\). This explicit method has first-order global accuracy under suitable regularity and stability assumptions; stiff equations can demand very small steps for stability even when the solution looks smooth.

Worked example or interpretation

For y′=−y in normalized units, current y=2 and step h=0.1 give next value 2+0.1×(−2)=1.8. The exact value after that step is 2 exp(−0.1)≈1.80967, showing that the numerical update is an approximation.

Source

Driscoll and Braun, Fundamentals of Numerical Computation, initial-value problems.
