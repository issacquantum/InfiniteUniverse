Nonlinear Simple Pendulum Equation

Equation

$$m\ell^2\ddot\theta=-mg\ell\sin\theta$$

Literal reading

M times ell squared times theta double dot equals minus m g ell times sine of theta.

Conceptual reading

Gravitational torque produces angular acceleration without assuming a small angle.

Symbols

\(m\) is bob mass, \(\ell\) pendulum length, \(g\) local gravitational acceleration magnitude and \(\theta\) angle from the downward vertical. Two dots mean two derivatives with respect to time. Sine acts on theta; the minus sign makes the torque restoring near zero.

Values, units and domains

m is in kg, ell in m, g in m/s² and theta in radians. Angular acceleration is in rad/s²; both sides are torques in N·m. g is location-dependent, not universal; 9.81 m/s² is illustrative.

Meaning

The sine makes the motion nonlinear at finite amplitude. The restoring torque vanishes at the vertical positions but the upward position is unstable.

Assumptions and limits

Point bob, massless rigid support of fixed length, frictionless pivot, uniform gravity and planar motion. No small-angle approximation has yet been made.

Example

With ell = 1 m, g = 9.81 m/s² and theta = π/6 radians, angular acceleration is −9.81 × 0.5 = −4.905 rad/s².
