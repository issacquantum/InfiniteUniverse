Coulomb Potential

Equation

$$ V(r) = -\frac{e^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0 r} $$

Literal Reading

V of r equals minus e squared divided by the product four pi epsilon naught r.

Common Reading

The attractive electrostatic potential energy between the electron and the proton.

Symbols

\(V(r)\): the potential energy as a function of distance.

\(e\): the elementary charge.

\(\epsilon_0\): the permittivity of free space.

\(r\): the distance from the nucleus.

negative sign: the interaction is attractive, not repulsive.

Meaning of the Equation

The inverse-distance attraction is the binding term in the hydrogen problem. It is what lets the electron form discrete bound states around the proton.

Units and scope

\(V\) is potential energy in joules, not electric potential in volts. \(e\) is in coulombs, \(\epsilon_0\) in F/m, and \(r>0\) in meters. The energy zero is chosen at infinite separation. This is the nonrelativistic point-charge interaction in vacuum; nuclear structure, screening, and radiative corrections are omitted.

Sources

MIT, Quantum Physics I, lecture notes.

Worked example or interpretation

At the Bohr radius a₀≈5.29177210544 × 10⁻¹¹ m, this potential energy is approximately −27.2114 eV. It is not the ground-state total energy, which also includes kinetic energy. Use e=1.602176634 × 10⁻¹⁹ C exactly and ε₀≈8.8541878188 × 10⁻¹² F/m.
