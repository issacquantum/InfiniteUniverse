Cross Entropy Loss

Equation

$$ L=-\sum_i y_i\log(\hat{y}_i) $$

Literal Reading

L equals minus the sum over i of y subscript i times the logarithm of y hat subscript i.

Common Reading

Cross-entropy loss is the negative log-likelihood of the target labels under the model’s predicted probability distribution.

Symbols

\(L\): loss value.

\(y_i\): true label indicator or target probability.

\(\hat{y}_i\): predicted probability for class \(i\).

Meaning of the Equation

It penalizes the model when it assigns low probability to the correct target, rather than being generic thermodynamic entropy.

Conditions and units

\(i\) labels classes; both probability vectors are normalized and dimensionless. Log means natural log here, giving loss in nats. Terms with \(y_i=0\) contribute zero by convention; a zero prediction with \(y_i>0\) gives infinite loss. For one-hot targets this is a negative log-likelihood; for soft targets it is the expected negative log-likelihood under the target distribution.

Worked example or interpretation

For a one-hot target in class 1 and predicted probabilities (0.8,0.2), only the first term contributes. The loss is −ln(0.8)≈0.2231 nats. Assigning probability 0.1 to that same correct class would give about 2.3026 nats, a larger penalty.

Notation details

The hat marks a predicted value. The minus sign applies to the complete sum; the logarithm acts on the predicted probability before multiplication by the target weight.

Source

David J. C. MacKay, Information Theory, Inference, and Learning Algorithms.
