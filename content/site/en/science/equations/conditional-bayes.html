Conditional Probability and Bayes

Equation

$$P(A\mid B)=\frac{P(A\cap B)}{P(B)}=\frac{P(B\mid A)P(A)}{P(B)}$$

Literal Reading

P of A given B equals the intersection probability divided by P of B, also equal to P of B given A times P of A divided by P of B.

Common Reading

Update a probability using observed evidence.

Symbols

A, B: events; P(A): prior; P(B|A): likelihood; P(A|B): posterior.

Meaning of the Equation

P(B) must be positive; the expression P(B|A) also requires P(A)>0. Generalizations use densities or conditional distributions.

Conditions and units

\(\cap\) denotes intersection and \(P(A\cap B)\) the joint probability; \(P(B)\) is the evidence probability. All probabilities are dimensionless. This is an exact identity on one probability space, not a guarantee that an empirical likelihood model is correct.
