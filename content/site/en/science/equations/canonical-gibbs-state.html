Canonical Gibbs State

Equation

$$\rho_\beta=\frac{e^{-\beta H}}{Z}$$

Literal reading

Rho subscript beta equals the exponential of minus beta times H, divided by Z.

Conceptual reading

Thermal equilibrium weights energy eigenstates exponentially according to their energies.

Symbols

\(\rho_\beta\) is the density operator at inverse temperature \(\beta\); \(H\) is the Hamiltonian. \(e\) is the base of natural logarithms; its operator exponential acts on the spectrum of H. \(Z\) is the trace of that exponential, the partition function. The subscript records temperature dependence; division by Z normalizes the full operator.

Values, units and domains

H is in J; beta is in J⁻¹ and equals the reciprocal of Boltzmann constant times absolute temperature. The Boltzmann constant is exactly 1.380649 × 10⁻²³ J/K. The exponent, Z and finite-dimensional density entries are dimensionless. Require a finite nonzero Z.

Meaning

At positive temperature, higher energy states have smaller individual Boltzmann weights. Degeneracy still affects the total weight of an energy level. Normalization gives total probability one.

Assumptions and limits

Canonical equilibrium for a specified self-adjoint Hamiltonian and temperature. Particle exchange needs a grand-canonical expression with chemical potential. Non-equilibrium states need not have this form.

Example

For two nondegenerate levels of energies zero and a positive gap Δ with beta times Δ = ln 2, their weights are 1 and 1/2. Dividing by Z = 3/2 gives probabilities 2/3 and 1/3.

Source

David Tong, Statistical Physics; NIST, CODATA 2022.
