Cutoff Gaussian Field Sample

Equation

$$\phi_\Lambda(\mathbf r,t)\propto \sum_{\mathbf{k}} \frac{e^{-(|\mathbf{k}|/\Lambda)^4}}{\sqrt{2\omega_{\mathbf{k}}}} \Big[ (Q_{\mathbf{k}}\cos(\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf r)+\widetilde Q_{\mathbf{k}}\sin(\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf r)) \cos(\omega_{\mathbf{k}}t) +(P_{\mathbf{k}}\cos(\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf r)+\widetilde P_{\mathbf{k}}\sin(\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf r)) \sin(\omega_{\mathbf{k}}t) \Big]$$

Literal reading

Phi subscript capital lambda at vector r and t is proportional to a sum over wave vectors k. The weight is exponential of minus the fourth power of the ratio of the magnitude of k to capital lambda, divided by the square root of twice omega subscript k. It multiplies a bracket with two terms: the Q cosine plus Q-tilde sine spatial combination times cosine of omega t; plus the P cosine plus P-tilde sine spatial combination times sine of omega t.

Conceptual reading

A finite collection of weighted random oscillations produces a real field image.

Symbols

\(\phi_\Lambda\) is the rendered scalar amplitude, \(\mathbf r=(x,z)\) a position and \(t\) time. \(\mathbf k\) labels the retained wave vectors; \(\sum_{\mathbf k}\) sums them. \(\Lambda>0\) is a cutoff wave number and \(\omega_{\mathbf k}>0\) each mode’s angular frequency. \(Q_{\mathbf k},\widetilde Q_{\mathbf k},P_{\mathbf k},\widetilde P_{\mathbf k}\) are independent standard normal random coefficients; tildes label distinct coefficients. Bars take vector magnitude, the dot is a Euclidean inner product, e is the exponential base, and sine and cosine act on the parenthesized phases. \(\propto\) omits an overall normalization.

Values, units and domains

The visualization uses normalized coordinates, time and amplitude. In physical length units with c=1, k, Lambda and omega have inverse-length units and t has length units. Every trigonometric or exponential argument is dimensionless. The omitted mode measure and normalization determine the physical field amplitude; no SI amplitude is specified by proportionality.

Meaning

The cutoff suppresses short wavelengths. The finite mode set and random coefficients choose a sample, rather than representing an infinite continuum or a measured quantum-field history.

Assumptions and limits

This classical Gaussian model illustrates symmetrized free-field fluctuations. It does not reproduce noncommuting quantum operators. Frequencies and normalization must follow the chosen model; a massless zero mode needs separate treatment because the displayed weight would divide by zero.

Example

At t=0, the terms multiplied by sine of omega t vanish. The Q and Q-tilde coefficients then specify the initial spatial sample. At wave-number magnitude Lambda, the ultraviolet damping factor is e⁻¹ ≈ 0.368; at twice Lambda it is e⁻¹⁶ ≈ 1.13 × 10⁻⁷.
