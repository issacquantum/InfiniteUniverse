Work-Energy Theorem

Equation

$$W_{\rm net}=\int_A^B\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r=\Delta K$$

Literal reading

Net work equals the line integral from A to B of vector F dotted with differential displacement vector r, and equals the change in kinetic energy.

Conceptual reading

The work of the net force changes a particle’s kinetic energy.

Symbols

\(W_{\rm net}\) is net work; \(\mathbf F\) net force; \(d\mathbf r\) infinitesimal displacement along the actual path. The dot is a scalar product. A and B label endpoints, not vectors to multiply. \(K\) is kinetic energy and \(\Delta K\) its final minus initial value. The integral follows the path.

Values, units and domains

Force is in N, displacement in m, and work and energy in J = N·m. Work and energy are scalars; the integral may be negative.

Meaning

Only the force component along displacement contributes to work. A perpendicular force can turn a trajectory without changing speed.

Assumptions and limits

Newtonian particle of constant mass in an inertial frame. For an extended deformable body, centre-of-mass work alone does not account for all internal energy transfers.

Example

A 2 N net force acting along a 3 m displacement does 6 J of work, so kinetic energy increases by 6 J.
