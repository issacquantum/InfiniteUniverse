Sobolev Norm

Equation

$$\|u\|_{W^{k,p}(\Omega)}=\left(\sum_{|\alpha|\leq k}\|D^\alpha u\|_{L^p(\Omega)}^p\right)^{1/p}$$

Literal Reading

The W k p norm is the pth root of the sum of the pth powers of L p norms of derivatives through order k.

Common Reading

Control the function and its weak derivatives together.

Symbols

k: nonnegative integer; 1≤p<∞; α: multi-index; Dᵅ: weak derivative.

Meaning of the Equation

This is an integer-order Sobolev norm. Fractional order and p=∞ require other definitions. Embedding conclusions depend on dimension and domain.

Conditions and units

\(\Omega\) is an open domain in \(\mathbb R^d\), u a function with weak derivatives through order k in \(L^p\), and \(\alpha=(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_d)\) a tuple of nonnegative integers with \(|\alpha|=\sum_j\alpha_j\). Order zero includes u itself. The \(L^p\) norm integrates \(|D^\alpha u|^p\) over \(\Omega\) before taking its pth root. This standard unweighted norm uses nondimensional coordinates or a fixed mathematical unit convention; physical derivatives of different orders have different units and require reference-length weights before being combined dimensionally.

Worked example or interpretation

On the dimensionless interval (0,1), take u(x)=x, k=1 and p=2. The squared L two norm of u is 1/3 and that of its weak derivative 1 is 1. The displayed Sobolev norm is therefore √(1/3+1)=2/√3. Both the function and its derivative contribute.

Source

John K. Hunter, Notes on Partial Differential Equations, Sobolev spaces.
