Bayes' Theorem

Equation

$$P(A\mid B)=\frac{P(B\mid A)P(A)}{P(B)}$$

Literal Reading

P of A vertical bar B equals P of B vertical bar A P of A over P of B.

Common Reading

The probability of a hypothesis after evidence equals the likelihood times the prior, normalized by the evidence probability.

Symbols

\(P(A\mid B)\): updated probability of \(A\) after knowing \(B\).

\(P(B\mid A)\): likelihood of seeing \(B\) if \(A\) is true.

\(P(A)\): prior probability.

\(P(B)\): evidence normalization.

Meaning of the Equation

Evidence reshapes a prior by favoring hypotheses that made the evidence likely and reducing hypotheses that did not. The denominator keeps the updated probabilities normalized.

Conditions and units

\(A,B\) are events in a common probability space. The probabilities are dimensionless. The displayed conditional probabilities require \(P(B)>0\) and, for the likelihood as written, \(P(A)>0\). It is an exact probability identity; a posterior still depends on the chosen prior and likelihood model.

Worked example or interpretation

Choose consistent illustrative probabilities P(A)=0.2, P(B|A)=0.6 and P(B)=0.3. Then P(A|B)=0.6×0.2/0.3=0.4. The evidence doubles the probability of A in this example; Bayes’s rule does not always increase a prior.

Notation details

The vertical bar reads given or conditional on. Juxtaposition in the numerator multiplies the likelihood by the prior, and the entire product is divided by the evidence probability.
