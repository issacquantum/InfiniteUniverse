Relativistic Time Dilation

Equation

$$\Delta t' = \gamma \Delta t$$

Literal Reading

Delta t prime equals gamma times delta t.

Common Reading

Moving clocks being measured to run slow relative to the observer's frame.

Symbols

\(\Delta t\): time interval measured in the clock's rest frame.

\(\Delta t'\): time interval measured from the relatively moving frame.

\(\gamma\): Lorentz factor.

Meaning of the Equation

This relation applies to uniform relative motion between inertial frames, with the unprimed interval measured in the clock’s rest frame. Accelerated clocks in flat spacetime can still be treated within special relativity by integrating proper time along their paths. Curved spacetime requires the corresponding general-relativistic metric.

Conditions and units

The two events occur at the same position in the unprimed clock-rest frame, so \(\Delta t\) is proper elapsed time. Both intervals are in seconds and \(\gamma=(1-v^2/c^2)^{-1/2}\ge1\), with constant relative speed \(|v|<c\). Without that same-position condition, a general Lorentz transformation includes a spatial-separation term.

Worked example or interpretation

At relative speed 0.6c, one second between ticks in the clock’s rest frame corresponds to 1.25 s in the other inertial frame. The events occur at the same position only in the clock-rest frame. Here c=299792458 m/s exactly.

Source

Sean M. Carroll, Lecture Notes on General Relativity.
