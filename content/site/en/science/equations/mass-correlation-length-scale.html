Mass Parameter and Correlation-Length Scale

Equation

$$m\approx\frac1\xi$$

Literal reading

m is approximately one divided by xi.

Conceptual reading

A larger mass scale corresponds to a shorter characteristic correlation length.

Symbols

\(m\) is the model’s positive inverse-length mass parameter and \(\xi\) its characteristic correlation length. \(\approx\) denotes an approximate identification, not exact equality. The numerator one is dimensionless.

Values, units and domains

Xi is positive and has length units; m has reciprocal-length units. In physical terms m is mass times c divided by ħ. The rendering uses normalized lengths, so its parameter is not a universal particle mass.

Meaning

The relation links the model’s scale controls: reducing the inverse-length parameter allows correlations over larger distances. It does not specify the complete correlation function or remove the ultraviolet cutoff.

Assumptions and limits

This is a free massive-field scale relation used approximately in the visualization. Correlators can include power-law prefactors, and finite mode sets, boundaries and cutoff affect the image. The massless limit does not give a finite correlation length through this formula.

Example

In a consistent normalized length unit, xi=2 corresponds approximately to m=0.5. Doubling xi to 4 halves m to 0.25; this is a scale comparison, not a measurement of a fundamental constant.
