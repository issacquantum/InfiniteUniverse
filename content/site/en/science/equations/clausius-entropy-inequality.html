Clausius Entropy Inequality

Equation

$$dS\ge\frac{\delta Q}{T}$$

Literal reading

The differential of entropy S is greater than or equal to heat increment delta Q divided by absolute temperature T.

Conceptual reading

Entropy change includes transferred entropy and any nonnegative internal production.

Symbols

\(S\) is thermodynamic entropy, \(dS\) its state-function differential, \(\delta Q\) heat entering the system and \(T\) the absolute temperature at which that heat is exchanged. Delta marks a path-dependent heat increment, not a state-function differential. \(\ge\) allows equality for a reversible process.

Values, units and domains

Heat is in J, temperature in positive K and entropy in J/K. The sign convention takes heat into the system as positive. Multiple boundary temperatures require separate heat-transfer contributions.

Meaning

The inequality distinguishes entropy transfer from entropy production. A system’s entropy can decrease by exporting enough heat; the second law constrains total production, not every subsystem’s change.

Assumptions and limits

Closed-system entropy balance for appropriately specified heat exchange and equilibrium-state entropy changes. Equality holds for a reversible process. A single T cannot represent unrelated reservoirs at different temperatures.

Example

For reversible absorption of an illustrative 600 J at constant 300 K, entropy rises by 2 J/K. Irreversible production adds to the corresponding entropy balance.

Source

David Tong, Statistical Physics; NIST, CODATA 2022.
