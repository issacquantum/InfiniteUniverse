Photoelectric Equation

Equation

$$ K_{\max} = h\nu - \phi $$

Literal Reading

K max equals h times nu minus phi.

Common Reading

The maximum kinetic energy of the emitted electron equals the photon energy minus the work function.

Symbols

\(K_{\max}\): the maximum kinetic energy of the emitted electron.

\(h\nu\): the energy of the incoming photon.

\(\phi\): the work function, the minimum energy required to free the electron from the material.

Meaning of the Equation

The threshold shows that light energy arrives in quanta. Brighter low-frequency light can send more photons, but if each photon lacks enough energy, electrons are not ejected.

Conditions and units

\(h=6.62607015\times10^{-34}\) J s exactly and \(\nu\) is ordinary frequency in Hz, not angular frequency. \(K_{\max}\) and the material-dependent work function \(\phi\) use J or a consistent electronvolt convention. The single-photon result applies above threshold \(h\nu\ge\phi\); a negative right side means no emission by that mechanism, not negative kinetic energy. Intense fields can support multiphoton emission, outside this approximation.

Worked example or interpretation

For illustrative photon energy 5 eV and material work function 2 eV, the maximum emitted-electron kinetic energy is 3 eV. A photon energy of 1 eV is below threshold for that material in the single-photon model. One eV is exactly 1.602176634 × 10⁻¹⁹ J.
