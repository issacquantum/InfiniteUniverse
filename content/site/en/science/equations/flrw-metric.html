FLRW (Friedmann-Lemaitre-Robertson-Walker) Metric

Equation

$$ds^2 = -c^2dt^2 + a^2(t)\left[\frac{dr^2}{1-kr^2}+r^2d\Omega^2\right]$$

Literal Reading

d s squared equals minus c squared d t squared, plus a of t squared times the bracket d r squared divided by one minus k r squared, plus r squared d capital omega squared.

Common Reading

The standard metric for a homogeneous and isotropic universe.

Symbols

\(a(t)\): scale factor.

\(k\): spatial curvature parameter.

\(d\Omega^2\): angular part of the spatial metric.

Meaning of the Equation

The metric encodes the idealized large-scale geometry of a universe that is homogeneous and isotropic. The scale factor carries the expansion, while the curvature parameter sets the spatial shape. It is not a general metric for every spacetime; it is the symmetric cosmological model used after those assumptions are made.

Conditions and units

Use signature \((-+++)\), cosmic time \(t\) in seconds, \(c\) in m/s, \(a(t)\) in meters, and dimensionless comoving radius \(r\) and \(k=0,\pm1\). Then \(ds^2\) is in m². \(d\Omega^2=d\theta^2+\sin^2\theta\,d\phi^2\), with polar angles \(\theta,\phi\), is the unit-sphere angular line element. A dot on \(a\) determines expansion or contraction; the metric alone does not supply its dynamics.
