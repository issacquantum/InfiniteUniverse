Black Hole Evaporation Time

Equation

$$\tau = \frac{5120\pi G^2M^3}{\hbar c^4}$$

Literal Reading

tau equals 5120 pi G squared M cubed over h bar c to the fourth.

Common Reading

The approximate lifetime of a nonrotating, uncharged Schwarzschild black hole evaporating through Hawking radiation.

Symbols

\(\tau\): evaporation time.

\(M\): black hole mass.

\(G\): gravitational constant.

\(\hbar\): reduced Planck constant.

\(c\): speed of light.

Meaning of the Equation

This is an approximate lifetime for an isolated, nonrotating, uncharged Schwarzschild black hole; accretion, spin, charge, and the final quantum-gravity phase are excluded.

Scope and Limits

This estimate assumes an isolated Schwarzschild black hole and a semiclassical evaporation calculation. It ignores accretion, spin, charge, and the unknown final quantum-gravity phase.

Conditions and units

In SI, \(\tau\) is in seconds and \(M\) is the initial mass in kg. \(G\) has units m³ kg⁻¹ s⁻², \(\hbar\) J s, and \(c\) m/s. The coefficient 5120π comes from an idealized blackbody estimate using the horizon area. Greybody factors and the available particle species change the numerical lifetime; this is not the full Hawking emission calculation.

Worked example or interpretation

Within this approximate model, doubling the initial mass multiplies the lifetime by eight. For an illustrative initial mass of 10¹² kg, substitution gives about 8.41 × 10¹⁹ s. Particle emission channels and greybody corrections alter this estimate, especially as the mass decreases.
