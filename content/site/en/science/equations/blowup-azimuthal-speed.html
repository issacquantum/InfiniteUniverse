Construction: Azimuthal Core Speed

Equation

$$U_{\theta}\asymp\tau^{-1/2-h}$$

Literal reading

U subscript theta is comparable to tau raised to minus one half minus h.

Conceptual reading

The characteristic azimuthal speed increases as the singular time approaches.

Symbols

\(U_{\theta}\) denotes a characteristic magnitude of the leading azimuthal velocity across the core, not every pointwise component. \(\tau\) is remaining normalized time and \(h\) a fixed positive exponent. \(\asymp\) denotes two-sided positive bounds independent of tau.

Values, units and domains

These are normalized construction variables with singular time 1. Here \(\tau=1-t>0\) is remaining time and \(0<h<1/100\) a fixed dimensionless exponent. Physical lengths, times and velocities require reference scales; a power of normalized time is not an SI dimensional equality.

Meaning

The negative exponent permits unbounded characteristic speed in a shrinking region. It does not imply that velocity diverges at every spatial point; the azimuthal component in particular vanishes on the axis.

Assumptions and limits

This summarizes a leading-core estimate in the cited forced construction, not a universal flow law or an independent verification of the proof. The full result also requires the residual cancellations and smooth forcing. The similarity-defined core is not a transported material parcel.

Example

For h=0.005, the reference power is tau^(−0.505), which diverges as tau tends to zero. Comparability specifies its order, not an exact velocity profile.
