Relativistic Stellar Equilibrium

Equation

$$\frac{dP}{dr}=-\frac{G(\rho_E+P/c^2)(m+4\pi r^3P/c^2)}{r^2\left(1-2Gm/(rc^2)\right)}$$

Literal reading

The pressure gradient includes energy density, pressure contributions to gravity, and a spacetime-curvature factor.

Symbols and units

P: Pa; r: areal radius, m; rhoE: total local energy density divided by c², kg/m³, not merely rest-mass density; m(r): enclosed gravitational mass, kg; G: m³ kg⁻¹ s⁻²; c: m/s; pi: dimensionless. The gradient is Pa/m.

Physical meaning

Pressure and energy gravitate, and curvature changes radial balance in a compact star. An equation of state and the relativistic enclosed-energy mass relation close the model.

Derivation

Use a static spherical metric in Einstein’s equations and local stress-energy conservation for an isotropic perfect fluid. Eliminating the metric gradient gives this TOV equation; it is not obtained by merely replacing Newtonian mass with rest energy.

Assumptions and limits

Static spherical general relativity with isotropic pressure, negligible rotation, and no cosmological term. Equilibrium does not prove stability; rapid rotation and anisotropic stresses require extensions.

Worked example

When pressure divided by c² is much smaller than density and compactness is small, the additional factors approach their Newtonian limits and ordinary hydrostatic balance is recovered.

Source

Oppenheimer & Volkoff (1939)
