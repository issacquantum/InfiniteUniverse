Renormalization Group Equation

Equation

$$\mu\frac{dg}{d\mu}=\beta(g)$$

Literal Reading

mu d g over d mu equals beta of g.

Common Reading

A coupling changes with measurement scale; \(\beta(g)\) gives the running.

Symbols

  • \(\mu\): renormalization energy scale.
  • \(g\): coupling parameter.
  • \(\beta(g)\): function telling how the coupling runs with scale.

Meaning of the Equation

Here \(\mu\) is the renormalization energy scale. The beta function describes variation with its logarithm. Using a length scale inversely proportional to this energy scale instead reverses the sign of the logarithmic derivative.

Conditions and units

The renormalized coupling \(g(\mu)\) is taken dimensionless, so \(\beta\) is dimensionless; \(\mu\) uses energy units and is positive. The derivative compares descriptions of the same physics at different renormalization scales, not time evolution. The beta function depends on the theory and renormalization scheme, and several running couplings generally require a coupled set of equations. Physical observables should not depend on an arbitrary scale when treated consistently.

Worked example or interpretation

At a fixed point g*, β(g*)=0 and the coupling does not run with the renormalization scale. As an illustrative mathematical flow, β(g)=bg has solution g(μ)=g(μ₀)(μ/μ₀)^b for constant dimensionless b and positive reference scale μ₀. This example does not specify the beta function of any particular physical theory.
