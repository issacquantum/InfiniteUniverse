Kohn-Sham Equations

Equation

$$\left[-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\nabla^2+V_{\mathrm{eff}}(\mathbf{r})\right]\psi_i=\epsilon_i\psi_i$$

Literal Reading

open bracket minus h-bar squared over two m times nabla squared plus V effective of r close bracket psi sub i equals epsilon sub i psi sub i.

Common Reading

A hard many-electron problem is replaced by easier one-electron-like equations with an effective potential.

Symbols

  • \(\psi_i\): Kohn-Sham orbital.
  • \(V_{\mathrm{eff}}\): effective potential.
  • \(\epsilon_i\): orbital energy parameter.
  • \(\nabla^2\): Laplacian / spatial second-derivative operator.

Meaning of the Equation

Kohn-Sham orbitals are auxiliary orbitals used to reproduce the electron density; they are not generally the true many-body electron wavefunction.

Conditions and units

Here \(m\) is the electron mass, \(\hbar\) the reduced Planck constant, \(\mathbf r\) position, and \(i\) an orbital index. In SI, \(V_{\rm eff}\) and \(\epsilon_i\) are in J, \(\nabla^2\) has units m⁻², and a normalized three-dimensional orbital has units m⁻³ᐟ². The potential includes the external, Hartree, and exchange-correlation contributions and depends on the density, so the equations are solved self-consistently. The displayed scalar local-potential form omits spin and relativistic extensions. Practical exchange-correlation functionals are approximations, and orbital energies are not generally electron-removal or excitation energies.

Worked example or interpretation

For a spin-unpolarized closed shell with one normalized spatial orbital occupied by two electrons, that orbital contributes 2|ψ|² to the density. A self-consistent solution must reproduce the density used to construct its effective potential. The electron mass is approximately 9.1093837139 × 10⁻³¹ kg and ħ≈1.054571817 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s.

Source

Kohn and Sham (1965), Self-Consistent Equations Including Exchange and Correlation Effects.
