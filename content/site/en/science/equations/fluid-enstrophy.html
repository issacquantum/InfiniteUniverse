Enstrophy Balance

Equation

$$\frac12\frac{d}{dt}\|\boldsymbol\omega\|_2^2+\nu\|\nabla\boldsymbol\omega\|_2^2=\int_\Omega\boldsymbol\omega\cdot S\boldsymbol\omega\,dx+\int_\Omega(\nabla\times\mathbf f)\cdot\boldsymbol\omega\,dx$$

Literal Reading

One half the time derivative of the squared L two norm of omega, plus nu times the squared L two norm of the gradient of omega, equals the integral over Omega of omega dot S omega, plus the integral over Omega of the curl of f dotted with omega.

Common Reading

Vortex stretching and rotational forcing compete with viscous dissipation in the evolution of total squared vorticity.

Symbols

  • \(\boldsymbol\omega=\nabla\times\mathbf u\): Vorticity of the velocity field.
  • \(S=(\nabla\mathbf u+(\nabla\mathbf u)^{\mathsf T})/2\): Symmetric strain tensor; the superscript denotes transpose.
  • \(\frac12\|\boldsymbol\omega\|_2^2\): Enstrophy, the integral of half the squared vorticity magnitude.
  • \(\|\nabla\boldsymbol\omega\|_2^2\): Integral of the squared spatial derivatives of vorticity.
  • \(\nu,\mathbf f\): Positive kinematic viscosity and external force per unit mass.
  • \(\Omega,dx,t\): Spatial domain, volume element, and time.

Meaning of the Equation

Taking the inner product of the vorticity equation with vorticity and integrating removes the transport term by incompressibility. Integration by parts turns the Laplacian into a nonnegative dissipation term on the left. The antisymmetric part of the velocity gradient contributes nothing to the quadratic stretching term, leaving the strain tensor. Stretching has no fixed sign in three dimensions; in a genuinely planar two-dimensional flow it vanishes.

Scope and Limits

This equality requires sufficient smoothness, finite integrals, and periodicity or decay that eliminates boundary terms. It is not an enstrophy equality for arbitrary three-dimensional weak solutions.
