Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle

Equation

$$ \Delta x \, \Delta p \geq \frac{\hbar}{2} $$

Literal Reading

Delta x times delta p is greater than or equal to h-bar divided by two.

Common Reading

The product of state spreads for noncommuting observables has a lower bound; this is not merely measurement-device disturbance.

Symbols

\(\Delta x\): the uncertainty or spread in position.

\(\Delta p\): the uncertainty or spread in momentum.

\(\hbar/2\): the minimum quantum bound for the product of those spreads.

Meaning of the Equation

The limit is built into wave structure itself. A state squeezed tightly in position needs many momentum components, so momentum becomes less sharply defined even with perfect instruments.

Units and scope

\(\Delta x\) and \(\Delta p\) are standard deviations in the same quantum state, for position and its conjugate momentum component. Their units are m and kg m/s; their product and \(\hbar\) have units J s. The bound applies to states with well-defined finite variances on the appropriate operator domains. It is not a claim about simultaneous instrument errors, and equality is not attained by every state.

MIT, Quantum Physics I, lecture notes (publication page).

Worked example or interpretation

For a position standard deviation of 1 nm, the momentum standard deviation must be at least about 5.273 × 10⁻²⁶ kg·m/s, using ħ≈1.054571817 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s. A minimum-uncertainty Gaussian can attain equality; a general state need not.
