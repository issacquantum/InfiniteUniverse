Incompressible Navier-Stokes Momentum

Equation

$$\partial_t\mathbf u+(\mathbf u\cdot\nabla)\mathbf u=-\nabla p+\nu\Delta\mathbf u+\mathbf f$$

Literal Reading

The time derivative of u plus u dot gradient applied to u equals minus the gradient of p plus nu times the Laplacian of u plus f.

Common Reading

A moving fluid element accelerates under pressure, viscous diffusion, and external force per unit mass.

Symbols

  • \(x,t\): Spatial position and time.
  • \(\mathbf u(x,t)\): Velocity vector field.
  • \(\partial_t\mathbf u\): Change at a fixed spatial position.
  • \((\mathbf u\cdot\nabla)\mathbf u\): Nonlinear advection, with component \(\sum_j u_j\partial_{x_j}u_i\).
  • \(p,\nabla p\): Pressure divided by constant density, and its spatial gradient.
  • \(\nu>0\): Kinematic viscosity, with units of length squared per time.
  • \(\Delta\): Spatial Laplacian \(\sum_{i=1}^3\partial_{x_i}^2\), acting on each velocity component.
  • \(\mathbf f\): External force per unit mass.

Meaning of the Equation

Local acceleration measures change at a fixed point; nonlinear advection accounts for motion through a spatially varying velocity field. Their sum is the acceleration following a fluid particle. The negative pressure gradient supplies a force per unit mass, the Laplacian diffuses momentum, and the external force adds the prescribed acceleration. Incompressibility couples pressure to the velocity field rather than allowing pressure to evolve independently.

This equation must be solved together with \(\nabla\cdot\mathbf u=0\), initial data, and domain conditions. Pressure enforces the divergence constraint; viscosity transports momentum diffusively. Each term has dimensions of acceleration. Nonlinearity comes from the unknown velocity multiplying its own gradient, not from an additional force law.

Scope and Limits

The system here assumes a Newtonian fluid with constant density and viscosity. Smooth solutions require compatible divergence-free initial data and domain conditions. The continuum model does not describe molecular motion or all compressible flows.

Conditions and units

In SI, \(x\) is in m, \(t\) in s, \(\mathbf u\) in m/s, \(p\) in m²/s², \(\nu\) in m²/s, and \(\mathbf f\) in m/s². The indices \(i,j\) range over the three Cartesian components. The physical pressure in pascals is \(\rho_0p\), where \(\rho_0\) is the constant mass density.

Worked example or interpretation

For a steady shear u=(Ay,0,0) with constant A, both the advective term and the Laplacian vanish. Constant pressure and zero body force then satisfy the interior momentum equation. Moving boundaries can sustain this shear; the example does not describe a finite-energy solution over all of space.
