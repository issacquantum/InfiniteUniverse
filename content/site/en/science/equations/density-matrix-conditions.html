Density Matrix Conditions

Equation

$$ \rho\succeq 0,\qquad \mathrm{Tr}(\rho)=1 $$

Literal Reading

Rho is positive semidefinite and its trace equals one.

Common Reading

A valid quantum state must assign nonnegative probabilities to measurements and must have total probability one.

Symbols

\(\rho\): density matrix representing a quantum state.

\(\succeq 0\): positive semidefinite condition.

\(\mathrm{Tr}(\rho)\): trace of the density matrix.

\(1\): total probability normalization.

Meaning of the Equation

These conditions state what makes a density matrix physically valid: probabilities are nonnegative, total probability is one, and the matrix is compatible with quantum inner products.

Conditions and units

These conditions include pure states as well as mixed states. Positivity includes Hermiticity; in infinite dimensions \(\rho\) must be trace class. Its eigenvalues are dimensionless nonnegative numbers summing to one. Pure states additionally satisfy \(\rho^2=\rho\); a mixed-state ensemble decomposition is not unique.

Worked example or interpretation

The diagonal matrix with entries 0.3 and 0.7 satisfies positivity because both eigenvalues are nonnegative, and normalization because their sum is one. Entries 1.2 and −0.2 have the same trace but violate positivity. The two conditions are both necessary.

Notation details

Positive semidefinite means every vector has a nonnegative quadratic form with rho. Trace is the sum of diagonal entries in an orthonormal basis. The symbol succeq compares operators by positivity, not each matrix entry separately.

Source
