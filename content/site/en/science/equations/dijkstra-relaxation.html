Dijkstra Relaxation

Equation

$$ \text{if }d[v]>d[u]+w(u,v),\quad d[v]\leftarrow d[u]+w(u,v) $$

Literal Reading

if d of v is greater than d of u plus w of u comma v, then d of v is updated to d of u plus w of u comma v.

Common Reading

If passing through \(u\) gives a shorter route to \(v\), the algorithm updates the best known distance.

Symbols

\(d[v]\): best known distance to node \(v\).

\(d[u]\): best known distance to node \(u\).

\(w(u,v)\): weight of the edge from \(u\) to \(v\).

Meaning of the Equation

Dijkstra’s algorithm assumes nonnegative edge weights.

Conditions and units

The labels u and v denote vertices. Distances are from the chosen source; distances and edge weights share cost units, such as meters or seconds. The arrow is assignment, not equality. Relaxation itself can be used in other algorithms with negative weights; Dijkstra’s greedy finalization is the step that requires nonnegative weights.

Worked example or interpretation

If the current route to v costs 12 s, the route to u costs 5 s and edge (u,v) costs 3 s, the candidate through u costs 8 s. Since 12>8, replace d[v] with 8 s. If the candidate were 14 s, the existing value would remain.
