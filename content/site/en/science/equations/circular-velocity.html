Velocity in Uniform Circular Motion

Equation

$$\mathbf v(t)=R\omega(-\sin\omega t,0,\cos\omega t)$$

Literal reading

Vector v of t equals R omega times the coordinate triple minus sine omega t, zero, cosine omega t.

Conceptual reading

The velocity is tangent to the circle and has constant magnitude.

Symbols

\(\mathbf v(t)\) is velocity; \(R\) positive radius; \(\omega\) constant angular rate; \(t\) time. The tuple lists x, y and z components. The minus sign applies to sine; the common scalar R omega multiplies all components.

Values, units and domains

Radius is in m, time in s, angular rate in rad/s and velocity in m/s. The phase is dimensionless. Speed is R times the absolute angular rate.

Meaning

Differentiating the circular position changes radial direction into tangential direction. Constant speed does not mean a constant velocity vector.

Assumptions and limits

For the x-z parametrization starting on positive x, with constant R and omega. Variable radius or angular rate adds terms.

Example

At t = 0 with R = 2 m and omega = 3 rad/s, velocity is (0, 0, 6) m/s.
