Divergence-Free Weak Formulation

Equation

$$\begin{aligned}&\int_0^T\!\!\int_\Omega\!\Big[-\mathbf u\cdot\partial_t\boldsymbol\varphi-(\mathbf u\otimes\mathbf u):\nabla\boldsymbol\varphi\Big]dx\,dt\\ &\quad+\nu\int_0^T\!\!\int_\Omega\nabla\mathbf u:\nabla\boldsymbol\varphi\,dx\,dt\\ &=\int_\Omega\mathbf u_0\cdot\boldsymbol\varphi(x,0)\,dx+\int_0^T\!\!\int_\Omega\mathbf f\cdot\boldsymbol\varphi\,dx\,dt\end{aligned}$$

Literal Reading

The integral from zero to T and over Omega of minus u dot the time derivative of phi minus u tensor u contracted with the gradient of phi, with measure dx dt, plus nu times the integral over the same region of the gradient of u contracted with the gradient of phi, equals the integral over Omega of u subscript zero dot phi at x and zero, plus the integral from zero to T and over Omega of f dot phi.

Common Reading

Momentum is tested against smooth divergence-free fields, moving derivatives away from the possibly rough velocity.

Symbols

  • \(\Omega,T\): Spatial domain and finite final time.
  • \(\boldsymbol\varphi\): Smooth divergence-free vector test field, zero near \(T\), compactly supported in space or periodic.
  • \(\mathbf u,\mathbf u_0,\mathbf f\): Velocity, initial velocity, and force per unit mass.
  • \(\mathbf u\otimes\mathbf u\): Tensor with entries \(u_i u_j\).
  • \(A:B\): Full contraction \(\sum_{i,j}A_{ij}B_{ij}\), with gradient convention \((\nabla v)_{ij}=\partial_jv_i\).
  • \(\nu,dx\,dt\): Kinematic viscosity and space-time integration measure.

Meaning of the Equation

The signs follow from integration by parts in momentum. Test fields vanish near the final time but may be nonzero initially, producing the initial-data term. Pressure vanishes against divergence-free tests. This identity alone is not the full definition of a Leray-Hopf solution: distributional zero divergence, initial attainment, weak continuity, energy-class membership, and the energy inequality are also required.

Conditions and units

The indices \(i,j\) range from 1 to 3. With a dimensionless test field and SI coordinates, all displayed integrals have units m⁴/s. Other test-field units multiply every term equally. Spatial boundary terms must vanish through the stated support, periodicity, or compatible boundary conditions.

Worked example or interpretation

For zero initial velocity, zero velocity at all times and zero force, every integral vanishes for every admissible test field. For a nonzero smooth solution, multiplying momentum by a test field and integrating gives the displayed identity; its usefulness is that the identity can remain meaningful when classical second derivatives of velocity do not exist.
