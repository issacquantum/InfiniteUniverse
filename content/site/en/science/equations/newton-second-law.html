Newton's Second Law

Equation

$$\mathbf{F}=m\mathbf{a}$$

Literal Reading

Vector F equals m times vector a.

Common Reading

Net force determines acceleration, not velocity.

Symbols

  • \(\mathbf F\): Net external force, the vector sum of all external forces, in newtons (N).
  • \(m\): Constant inertial mass in kilograms (kg).
  • \(\mathbf a=d\mathbf v/dt\): Acceleration in metres per second squared (m/s²); \(\mathbf v\) is velocity in m/s and \(t\) is time in seconds. The fraction \(d\mathbf v/dt\) denotes the derivative of velocity with respect to time, not the ratio of two finite values.

Bold type identifies vectors. Juxtaposition in \(m\mathbf a\) multiplies a scalar by a vector; equality holds component by component. Read the acceleration definition as “the derivative of vector v with respect to t.”

How a Physicist Interprets It

Choose the body or system and add its external forces in an inertial frame. Net force and mass determine acceleration. Predicting later position also requires initial position and velocity and how the forces depend on time or motion.

Values

There is no universal constant in this equation. Mass is measured; forces come from measurements or interaction models. The example below treats its inputs as exact for illustration: 10 N minus 2 N gives 8 N; dividing by 2 kg gives 4 m/s². A newton is a kilogram metre per second squared, so the units agree.

Meaning of the Equation

Choose the body and draw only forces acting on it. Resolve the vector equation along fixed axes. For a 2 kg cart pulled horizontally by 10 N against 2 N of resistance, the net force is 8 N and acceleration is 4 m/s² in the pulling direction. A zero resultant means constant velocity, which need not be zero. For a constant-total-mass system, the same relation uses its center-of-mass acceleration.

Scope and Limits

Inertial frame, constant mass and Newtonian speeds. A rocket exchanging mass requires a momentum balance with the outgoing material. In a rotating or accelerating frame, inertial forces must be included.
