Normalization Condition

Equation

$$ \int_{\mathbb R^3} |\Psi(\mathbf{r}, t)|^2 \, d^3r = 1 $$

Literal Reading

The integral over three-dimensional real space of the squared modulus of capital psi of vector r and t, with volume element d cubed r, equals one.

Common Reading

The total probability of finding the particle somewhere in space must equal one.

Symbols

\(|\Psi|^2\): the probability density associated with the wavefunction.

\(d^3r\): the infinitesimal volume element in three-dimensional space.

\(1\): certainty that the particle exists somewhere in space.

Meaning of the Equation

Normalization says the whole probability distribution adds to one. It makes the wavefunction usable for predictions through the Born rule.

Conditions and units

\(\Psi\) is a square-integrable one-particle spatial wavefunction at a fixed time \(t\). Position \(\mathbf r\) is in m, time in s, \(d^3r=dx\,dy\,dz\) in m³, and \(\Psi\) in m⁻³ᐟ², so the integral is dimensionless. The superscript 3 denotes a three-dimensional volume element, not the cube of a one-dimensional differential. Spin components, if present, require summing their probability densities. Infinite plane waves require generalized or box normalization instead.

Worked example or interpretation

A constant amplitude 1/√V inside a box of volume V and zero outside has total probability V×(1/V)=1. This illustrates normalization only; discontinuous box edges do not automatically define a finite-energy state for a chosen Hamiltonian.
