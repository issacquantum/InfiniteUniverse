Vorticity Transport and Stretching

Equation

$$\begin{aligned}\boldsymbol\omega&=\nabla\times\mathbf u,\\ \partial_t\boldsymbol\omega+(\mathbf u\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol\omega&=(\boldsymbol\omega\cdot\nabla)\mathbf u\\ &\quad+\nu\Delta\boldsymbol\omega+\nabla\times\mathbf f\end{aligned}$$

Literal Reading

The time derivative of omega plus its advection equals omega dot gradient applied to u, plus viscous diffusion of omega, plus the curl of f. Omega is the curl of u.

Common Reading

Rotation is carried by the flow, stretched or tilted by velocity gradients, diffused by viscosity, and affected by rotational forcing.

Symbols

  • \(\boldsymbol\omega=\nabla\times\mathbf u\): Vorticity; curl of the velocity, with units of inverse time.
  • \(\mathbf u\): Velocity transporting the vorticity.
  • \((\boldsymbol\omega\cdot\nabla)\mathbf u\): Vortex stretching and tilting term.
  • \(\nu\Delta\boldsymbol\omega\): Viscous diffusion, with positive kinematic viscosity.
  • \(\nabla\times\mathbf f\): Curl of external force per unit mass.
  • \(\partial_t,\nabla,\Delta\): Time derivative, spatial gradient operator, and spatial Laplacian.

Meaning of the Equation

Taking curl of incompressible momentum removes pressure. Stretching has no definite sign in the enstrophy balance and can amplify vorticity in 3D. For a genuinely planar 2D velocity independent of the third coordinate, vorticity points in that third direction and the stretching term vanishes. This structural difference is central to regularity estimates.
