Calibration Condition

Equation

$$ P(\mathrm{correct}\mid \mathrm{confidence}=0.8)\approx 0.8 $$

Literal Reading

The probability of being correct given confidence equals 0.8 is approximately 0.8.

Common Reading

When the model says 80 percent confidence many times, it should be right about 80 percent of those times.

Symbols

\(P(\mathrm{correct}\mid \mathrm{confidence}=0.8)\): the observed correctness rate among cases where the model reported 0.8 confidence.

\(\approx\): approximately equal, because calibration is measured statistically over many examples.

Meaning of the Equation

Calibration is a population/statistical property over many predictions, not a guarantee for a single prediction.

Conditions and units

Confidence and probability are dimensionless. With continuously varying scores, calibration is expressed by a conditional expectation or estimated in score bins; the event of exactly 0.8 can have probability zero. Finite-sample uncertainty and the evaluation population matter, and calibration need not persist under distribution shift.
