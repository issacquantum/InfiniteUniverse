Construction: Axial Core Length

Equation

$$\ell_z\asymp\tau^{1/2-h}$$

Literal reading

Ell subscript z is comparable to tau raised to one half minus h. The exponent includes the subtraction.

Conceptual reading

The axial scale contracts more slowly than the radial square-root scale.

Symbols

\(\ell_z\) is characteristic core length along the z axis. \(\tau\) is remaining normalized time and \(h\) the fixed exponent adjustment. \(\asymp\) denotes two-sided positive bounds independent of tau.

Values, units and domains

These are normalized construction variables with singular time 1. Here \(\tau=1-t>0\) is remaining time and \(0<h<1/100\) a fixed dimensionless exponent. Physical lengths, times and velocities require reference scales; a power of normalized time is not an SI dimensional equality.

Meaning

Since one half minus h is positive, the axial length also tends to zero. Relative elongation does not mean that the absolute core length grows.

Assumptions and limits

This summarizes a leading-core estimate in the cited forced construction, not a universal flow law or an independent verification of the proof. The full result also requires the residual cancellations and smooth forcing. The similarity-defined core is not a transported material parcel.

Example

For the illustrative permitted value h=0.005, the exponent is 0.495. Tau^0.495 tends to zero as tau does, but its ratio to √tau tends to infinity.
