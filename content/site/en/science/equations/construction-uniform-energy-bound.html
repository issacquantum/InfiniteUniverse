Construction: Uniform Finite-Energy Bound

Equation

$$\sup_{0\le t<1}\|\mathbf u(t)\|_{L^2(\mathbb R^3)}<\infty$$

Literal reading

The supremum for t from zero inclusive to one exclusive of the L two norm over real three-dimensional space of u at t is finite.

Conceptual reading

The square-integrated velocity stays uniformly bounded up to the singular time.

Symbols

\(\mathbf u(t)\) is the spatial velocity field at time t. \(\|\cdot\|_{L^2(\mathbb R^3)}\) is the square root of the integral over all three-dimensional space of squared velocity magnitude. \(\sup\) takes the least upper bound over all indicated times. \(\infty\) denotes unboundedness, and the strict inequality states that a finite common bound exists.

Values, units and domains

Time is normalized to [0,1). With SI coordinates the norm has units m⁵ᐟ²/s. Its square times half the constant mass density gives kinetic energy in J. The finite bound depends on the constructed solution; it is not a universal constant.

Meaning

A finite L two bound constrains total kinetic energy, not peak velocity. Spatial concentration can increase the maximum while occupying sufficiently little volume.

Assumptions and limits

This is one property of the forced construction reported by OpenAI, with positive viscosity, zero initial velocity and a smooth force compactly supported in space and time. It is not a claim about every flow or the unforced problem, and this property alone does not prove that the momentum and incompressibility equations hold.

Example

A smooth bump multiplied by n and rescaled to width 1/n in three dimensions has squared L two norm proportional to n²/n³=1/n, while its maximum is proportional to n. This family illustrates why an integral bound alone cannot bound the maximum; it is not asserted to solve Navier-Stokes.
