Velocity Verlet Integration

Equation

$$\begin{aligned}x_{n+1}&=x_n+v_n h+\tfrac12a_nh^2,\\a_{n+1}&=a(x_{n+1}),\\v_{n+1}&=v_n+\tfrac12(a_n+a_{n+1})h.\end{aligned}$$

Literal Reading

X sub n plus one equals x sub n plus v sub n times h plus one half a sub n times h squared. A sub n plus one equals a evaluated at x sub n plus one. V sub n plus one equals v sub n plus one half of the sum of a sub n and a sub n plus one, times h.

Common Reading

Advance position, recalculate acceleration, then update velocity using the mean endpoint acceleration.

Symbols

  • \(n,n+1\): Integer timestep indices, not exponents.
  • \(x_n,v_n,a_n\): Numerical position (m), velocity (m/s), acceleration (m/s²) at step n.
  • \(h,a(x)\): Positive fixed timestep (s) and position-dependent acceleration function.

Meaning of the Equation

For smooth conservative position-dependent forces this is a second-order method: halving h typically reduces global error by about four over a fixed interval. In a harmonic oscillator, fixed-step Verlet is stable for h omega zero strictly below 2. Check phase error as well as energy: a plausible orbit can gradually lose timing accuracy. Symplecticity preserves Hamiltonian geometry, not exact physical energy at every step.

A reproducible refinement check uses \(m=1\) kg, \(k=1\) N/m, \(x(0)=1\) m and \(v(0)=0\). The exact result is \(x(t)=\cos(t)\) m with time in seconds. At \(t=1\) s, velocity Verlet gives approximately 0.538893 m for \(h=0.2\) s and 0.539951 m for \(h=0.1\) s, compared with 0.540302 m exactly. The position error falls from 0.001409 to 0.000351 m, close to a factor of four. Its numerical frequency is \(2\arcsin(h\omega_0/2)/h\); at \(h=0.2\) s it is about 1.001674 rad/s. After 1000 s the accumulated phase lead is about 1.674 radians, even though the relative energy error stays within 1% in exact arithmetic for this example. Phase error is a timing error within the oscillation, not necessarily a large amplitude error.

Scope and Limits

Fixed step and a separable Hamiltonian with constant mass. Velocity-dependent drag, adaptive timesteps and impacts need appropriate modifications; this formula is not a universal integrator.
