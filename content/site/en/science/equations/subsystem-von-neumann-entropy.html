Entropy of a Quantum Subsystem

Equation

$$S_A=-\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_A\log\rho_A)$$

Literal reading

S subscript A equals minus the trace of rho subscript A multiplied by the logarithm of rho subscript A.

Conceptual reading

The spectrum of a subsystem’s reduced state determines its von Neumann entropy.

Symbols

\(S_A\) is the entropy of subsystem A; \(\rho_A\) its normalized density operator. A is a subsystem label. The operator logarithm applies to rho’s eigenvalues; the product is operator multiplication, and the trace sums diagonal entries. The leading minus sign makes entropy nonnegative. Zero eigenvalues contribute zero by continuity.

Values, units and domains

With natural logarithm, entropy is dimensionless in nats; with base two it is in bits. This display uses log without fixing a base, which must be chosen consistently. Thermodynamic entropy adds a Boltzmann-constant factor when using natural logarithms.

Meaning

For a pure bipartite total state, subsystem entropy quantifies entanglement. For a mixed total state it also includes classical uncertainty and is not alone an entanglement measure.

Assumptions and limits

Finite-dimensional normalized positive state, or infinite-dimensional state with defined finite entropy. The logarithm is evaluated on the support.

Example

A subsystem with eigenvalues 1/2 and 1/2 has entropy ln 2 nats, or 1 bit if base two is chosen. Eigenvalues 1 and 0 give zero entropy.

Source

John Watrous, The Theory of Quantum Information (2018).
