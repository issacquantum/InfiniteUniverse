Luminosity, Radius, and Effective Temperature

Equation

$$L=4\pi R^2\sigma T_{\rm eff}^4$$

Literal reading

Luminosity equals surface area times the Stefan-Boltzmann flux at effective temperature.

Symbols and units

L: W; R: photospheric radius, m; sigma: Stefan-Boltzmann constant, W m⁻² K⁻⁴; Teff: effective temperature, K; pi: dimensionless.

Physical meaning

A cool star can be very luminous if its emitting area is large.

Derivation

Define effective temperature by total emergent surface flux and multiply by the area of a sphere. This definition does not require every wavelength to match a blackbody spectrum.

Assumptions and limits

Spherical star with a useful single effective temperature and radius. Distortion, spots, gravity darkening, and extended atmospheres complicate the observational inference.

Worked example

At half the effective temperature, a star needs four times the radius to keep the same luminosity.
