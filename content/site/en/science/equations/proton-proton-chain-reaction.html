Proton-Proton Chain Reaction

Equation

$$ 4p\rightarrow{}^4\mathrm{He}+2e^++2\nu_e+\text{energy} $$

Literal Reading

Four protons produce helium four, two positrons, two electron neutrinos, and energy.

Common Reading

This is the net reaction of the proton-proton chain in stars.

Symbols

\(p\): proton or hydrogen nucleus.

\({}^{4}\mathrm{He}\): helium-4 nucleus.

\(e^+\): positron.

\(\nu_e\): electron neutrino.

energy: released binding energy.

Meaning of the Equation

Intermediate photons and steps occur inside the full chain; this net equation summarizes the overall conversion.

Conditions and units

The superscript 4 is helium’s mass number, the plus sign on \(e^+\) denotes positive charge, and the subscript e identifies electron-flavor neutrinos. This is a net reaction, not a single four-proton collision or a rate law. Released energy is shared among particle kinetic energies and radiation; escaping neutrinos do not all heat the star. Any numerical energy budget must specify whether subsequent positron annihilation with ambient electrons is included.

Worked example or interpretation

Charge is balanced: four initial protons carry +4e; the helium nucleus carries +2e and the two positrons together carry +2e. The two electron neutrinos balance the positrons’ electron-lepton number. This accounting checks the net reaction without assigning a common energy to every neutrino.

Source

Adelberger et al. (2011), Solar fusion cross sections II: the pp chain and CNO cycles.
