Equal Subsystem Entropies of a Pure Bipartite State

Equation

$$S(\rho_A)=S(\rho_B)$$

Literal reading

S of rho subscript A equals S of rho subscript B.

Conceptual reading

The two reduced states of a pure bipartite state have equal entropy because their nonzero spectra coincide.

Symbols

\(S\) is von Neumann entropy; \(\rho_A\) and \(\rho_B\) are normalized reduced density operators obtained by tracing out the opposite subsystem. A and B label subsystems. Parentheses apply the entropy function to the entire operator.

Values, units and domains

Use the same logarithm base on both sides: bits for base two or nats for natural logarithms. Entropies are dimensionless; matrix entries are dimensionless in normalized finite-dimensional bases.

Meaning

Schmidt coefficients give the same nonzero density-operator eigenvalues on both sides, even when subsystem dimensions differ. Those eigenvalues fix the equal entropies.

Assumptions and limits

The joint AB state must be pure. The equality is not generally true for mixed bipartite states. Entropies must be well defined; finite dimension avoids divergence.

Example

A maximally entangled pair of qubits has one bit of entropy on each side. A product of two pure states has zero on each side.

Source

John Watrous, The Theory of Quantum Information (2018).
