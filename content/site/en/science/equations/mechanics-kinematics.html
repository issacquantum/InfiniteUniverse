Position, Velocity and Acceleration

Equation

$$\mathbf v=\frac{d\mathbf r}{dt},\quad\mathbf a=\frac{d\mathbf v}{dt},\qquad x=x_0+v_0t+\tfrac12at^2$$

Literal Reading

Vector v equals the time derivative of vector r; vector a equals the time derivative of vector v. X equals x zero plus v zero times t plus one half a times t squared.

Common Reading

Velocity measures position change; acceleration measures velocity change. Constant acceleration gives a quadratic position function.

Symbols

  • \(\mathbf r,\mathbf v,\mathbf a\): Position (m), velocity (m/s), acceleration (m/s²); bold symbols are vectors.
  • \(x,x_0,v_0,a\): One-axis position, initial position, initial velocity and constant acceleration; signs follow the chosen positive axis.
  • \(t,d/dt\): Elapsed time since initial conditions (s) and time derivative.

Meaning of the Equation

Starting at x zero = 0, v zero = 3 m/s and a = 2 m/s², after 4 s the position is 12 + 16 = 28 m and velocity is 11 m/s. Speed is the magnitude of velocity, not a signed component. A curved path can have acceleration even at constant speed.

Scope and Limits

The derivative definitions are general for smooth motion. The last expression requires constant acceleration and t = 0 at the initial state.
