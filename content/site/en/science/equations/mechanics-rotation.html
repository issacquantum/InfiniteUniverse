Torque and Rotation

Equation

$$\boldsymbol\tau=\mathbf r\times\mathbf F,\quad\frac{d\mathbf L}{dt}=\boldsymbol\tau_{\rm ext},\qquad\tau_z=I_z\alpha$$

Literal Reading

Vector tau equals vector r cross vector F. The time derivative of vector L equals the external torque vector. Tau sub z equals I sub z times alpha.

Common Reading

Torque changes angular momentum; fixed-axis rigid rotation has an inertia-dependent angular acceleration.

Symbols

  • \(\mathbf r,\mathbf F,\times\): Position from a fixed inertial origin (m), force (N), cross product with right-hand-rule direction.
  • \(\boldsymbol\tau,\mathbf L\): Torque (N m) and angular momentum (kg m²/s). L here is not the Lagrangian.
  • \(\tau_z,I_z,\alpha\): Torque component about the fixed z axis, moment of inertia (kg m²), angular acceleration (rad/s²). The subscript ext means external, t is time (s), and d/dt is a time derivative.

Meaning of the Equation

A perpendicular 20 N push 0.30 m from a hinge supplies 6 N m of torque. Applied to a fixed-axis body with I = 2 kg m², it gives 3 rad/s². A rigid body at rest is in static equilibrium when both net force and net torque vanish. A force through the pivot has no torque about it even if the force is large.

Scope and Limits

The angular-momentum balance uses a fixed inertial origin and appropriate internal-force cancellation. The scalar last equation requires a rigid body rotating about a fixed axis with constant I. General 3D rotation requires an inertia tensor; L need not be parallel to angular velocity.
