Vorticity as the Curl of Velocity

Equation

$$\boldsymbol\omega=\nabla\times\mathbf u$$

Literal reading

Bold omega equals nabla cross bold u. Nabla cross means the curl operator acting on the velocity field, not an ordinary cross product of two measured vectors.

Conceptual reading

The curl measures the local rotational part of the velocity gradient.

Symbols

\(\boldsymbol\omega\) is the vorticity vector and \(\mathbf u(\mathbf x,t)\) the velocity vector at position \(\mathbf x\) and time \(t\). \(\nabla\times\) is the spatial curl in a right-handed coordinate system. Bold symbols denote vectors; equality defines vorticity.

Values, units and domains

Velocity has units m/s, spatial differentiation contributes m⁻¹, and vorticity has units s⁻¹. A differentiable velocity field is required for this pointwise definition. No universal numerical value is assigned to either field.

Meaning

Vorticity is twice the angular velocity of the local rigid-rotation part of the motion. It does not describe the entire deformation or trajectory of a fluid parcel.

Assumptions and limits

The definition applies to smooth three-dimensional velocity fields, whether compressible or incompressible. For planar motion the only possible nonzero component is normal to the plane. Distributional curls can extend the definition to less regular fields.

Example

For rigid rotation \(\mathbf u=(-\Omega y,\Omega x,0)\), the z component of curl is \(\partial_x(\Omega x)-\partial_y(-\Omega y)=2\Omega\), and the other components vanish. With constant angular speed 3 s⁻¹, vorticity is 6 s⁻¹ along the rotation axis.

Source

David Tong, Lectures on Fluid Mechanics.
