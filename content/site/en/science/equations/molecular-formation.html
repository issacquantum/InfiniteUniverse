Molecular Formation

Equation

$$\mathrm{H}+\mathrm{H}\rightarrow \mathrm{H}_2\quad\text{(schematic)}$$

Literal Reading

H plus H goes to H two, schematically.

Common Reading

Hydrogen atoms can form molecular hydrogen, shown here as a simplified net picture.

Symbols

\(\mathrm{H}\): atomic hydrogen.

\(\mathrm{H}_2\): molecular hydrogen.

schematic: the displayed reaction suppresses intermediate steps.

Meaning of the Equation

This is schematic; actual primordial \(H_2\) formation proceeds mainly through intermediate ions such as \(H^-\) or \(H_2^+\).

Conditions and units

The subscript 2 counts nuclei in the molecule; it is not a power. Stable formation must release binding energy and conserve momentum through radiation, another collision partner, or intermediate reactions. In primordial low-density gas the ion-mediated pathways supply that mechanism; direct attachment of two neutral atoms without an energy-removal mechanism is not what the arrow asserts.

General schematic

$$\text{atoms}\rightarrow\text{molecules}$$ This schematic arrow means atoms combine into molecules under suitable chemical conditions. It is not a balanced reaction or a universal timeline; the hydrogen reaction above is one example.

Worked example or interpretation

In an illustrative primordial pathway, a hydrogen atom first captures an electron while emitting a photon, forming H⁻. That ion reacts with another hydrogen atom to form H₂ and release the electron. The net drawing omits these intermediate steps and the photon that carries energy away.

Source

Galli and Palla (1998), The Chemistry of the Early Universe.
