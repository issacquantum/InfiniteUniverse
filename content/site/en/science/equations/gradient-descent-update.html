Gradient Descent Update

Equation

$$ \theta_{t+1}=\theta_t-\eta\nabla_\theta L(\theta_t) $$

Literal Reading

Theta subscript t plus one equals theta subscript t minus eta times the gradient with respect to theta of L, evaluated at theta subscript t.

Common Reading

Parameters are updated by moving opposite the gradient of the loss.

Symbols

\(\theta_t\): parameters at training step \(t\).

\(\eta\): learning rate.

\(L\): loss function being minimized.

\(\nabla_\theta L\): gradient of the loss with respect to the parameters.

Meaning of the Equation

The update changes parameters in the direction that most quickly lowers the loss locally. It is an optimization rule, not a guarantee that the model understands the data.

Conditions and units

The index \(t\) counts iterations, not physical time, and \(\eta>0\). Steepest descent refers to the Euclidean parameter norm and a differentiable loss. A finite step can increase the loss if the learning rate is too large; nonconvex problems need not reach a global minimum. With dimensionless parameters and loss, \(\eta\) is dimensionless; otherwise its units must convert a gradient into a parameter increment.
