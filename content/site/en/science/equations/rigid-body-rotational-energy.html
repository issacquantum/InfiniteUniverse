Rigid-Body Rotational Kinetic Energy

Equation

$$K_{\mathrm{rot}}=\tfrac12I\omega^2$$

Literal reading

K subscript rot equals one half I times omega squared.

Conceptual reading

Rotation stores kinetic energy according to the mass distribution and angular speed.

Symbols

\(K_{\mathrm{rot}}\) is rotational kinetic energy; rot is a descriptive subscript. \(I\) is the moment of inertia about the rotation axis and \(\omega\) angular speed. The square applies to omega; one half is dimensionless.

Values, units and domains

I is in kg·m², angular speed in rad/s and energy in J. Radians are dimensionless in SI. Values depend on the body and motion.

Meaning

For fixed I, doubling angular speed quadruples rotational energy. This energy is distinct from translation of the centre of mass.

Assumptions and limits

Rigid-body rotation about a specified axis. The general three-dimensional formula uses the inertia tensor; deformation can change I.

Example

With I = 2 kg·m² and angular speed 3 rad/s, rotational energy is 0.5 × 2 × 9 = 9 J.
