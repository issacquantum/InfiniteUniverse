Mass-Energy Equivalence

Equation

$$E = mc^2$$

Literal Reading

E equals m c to the power of 2.

Common Reading

Rest mass corresponds to rest energy.

Symbols

\(E\): energy.

\(m\): mass.

\(c\): speed of light in vacuum.

Meaning of the Equation

Changes in invariant mass or binding energy can appear as kinetic energy or radiation.

Conditions and units

Here \(E\) means rest energy and \(m\) invariant mass, in kg; \(c=299792458\) m/s gives \(E\) in J. For a moving particle, total energy instead satisfies \(E_{\rm total}^2=p^2c^2+m^2c^4\), where \(p\) is the magnitude of its momentum. A system’s rest mass includes its internal energy and binding energy, not just the sum of constituent rest masses.

Worked example or interpretation

An illustrative invariant mass of 1 kg corresponds to rest energy 8.987551787368176 × 10¹⁶ J. This conversion does not say that a practical process can release all of that energy; accessible reactions and conservation laws determine what can be extracted.

Source

Sean M. Carroll, Lecture Notes on General Relativity.
