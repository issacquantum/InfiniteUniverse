Galilean Transformations

Equation

$$x' = x - vt,\qquad t' = t$$

Literal Reading

x prime equals x minus v times t; t prime equals t.

Common Reading

The classical transformation between two inertial frames moving at constant relative velocity.

Symbols

\(x\): position measured in the original frame.

\(x'\): position measured in the moving frame.

\(v\): relative velocity between frames.

\(t\) and \(t'\): time measured in each frame.

Meaning of the Equation

Valid for speeds much smaller than \(c\); at relativistic speeds, Lorentz transformations replace Galilean transformations.

Conditions and units

The primed frame moves at constant signed velocity \(v\) along the unprimed x axis; parallel axes and coincident origins at \(t=0\) are assumed. Positions have units m, time s, and \(v\) m/s. Transverse coordinates are unchanged. This is an exact symmetry of Newtonian mechanics and a low-speed approximation to relativistic coordinate transformations.
