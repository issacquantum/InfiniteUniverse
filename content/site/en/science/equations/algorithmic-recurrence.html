Algorithmic Recurrence

Equation

$$ T(n)=aT(n/b)+f(n) $$

Literal Reading

T of n equals a times T evaluated at n divided by b, plus f of n.

Common Reading

This describes divide and conquer algorithms, where a problem is split into smaller pieces and then combined.

Symbols

\(T(n)\): running time for size \(n\).

\(a\): number of subproblems.

\(n/b\): size of each subproblem.

\(f(n)\): work outside the recursive calls.

Meaning of the Equation

The equation turns a recursive procedure into a growth calculation. By tracking how many smaller subproblems are created and how much extra work is added, it predicts the total running time.

Conditions and units

\(n\) is a dimensionless input size, \(a\ge1\) an integer number of recursive calls, and \(b>1\) the dimensionless size-reduction factor. \(T\) and \(f\) must use the same cost units, such as operation counts. A base case and rounding rule are needed when \(n/b\) is not integral. This recurrence models equal-sized subproblems; it is not a formula for every recursive algorithm.

Worked example or interpretation

For two half-size recursive calls, one unit of combining work per input element and base cost T(1)=1, take a=2, b=2 and f(n)=n. Then T(2)=4, T(4)=12 and T(8)=32 cost units. For powers of two this gives T(n)=n log₂ n+n.
