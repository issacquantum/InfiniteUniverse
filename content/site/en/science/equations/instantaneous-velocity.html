Instantaneous Velocity

Equation

$$\mathbf v=\frac{d\mathbf r}{dt}$$

Literal reading

Vector v equals d vector r divided by dt: the derivative of position with respect to time.

Conceptual reading

Velocity is the instantaneous rate and direction of position change.

Symbols

\(\mathbf r\) is the position vector, \(\mathbf v\) the velocity vector and \(t\) time. Bold type denotes vectors. The operator \(d/dt\) differentiates each component; the equals sign relates the vectors.

Values, units and domains

Position is in m, time in s and velocity in m/s. Values depend on the motion and reference frame; there is no universal velocity.

Meaning

The derivative gives a tangent vector to a smooth trajectory. Its magnitude is speed; its components may be negative.

Assumptions and limits

The trajectory must be differentiable at the time considered. An average displacement divided by a finite time interval is not generally the instantaneous velocity.

Example

For motion along x with position 3t metres when t is in seconds, the velocity is 3 m/s in the positive x direction.
