Strong Subadditivity

Equation

$$ S(ABC)+S(B)\le S(AB)+S(BC) $$

Literal Reading

S of A B C plus S of B is less than or equal to S of A B plus S of B C.

Common Reading

Strong subadditivity is a fundamental consistency condition for quantum entropy across overlapping subsystems.

Symbols

\(A\), \(B\), \(C\): three subsystems.

\(S(ABC)\): entropy of the combined system.

\(S(AB)\), \(S(BC)\): entropy of paired subsystems.

Meaning of the Equation

This is a consistency law for how quantum correlations can be shared. It prevents entropy assignments that would make overlapping subsystems carry impossible amounts of shared information.

Conditions and units

All entropies are von Neumann entropies of marginals of the same normalized tripartite state; \(S(B)\) is the entropy of B alone. A common logarithm base gives bits or nats. In finite dimensions this is an exact theorem and is equivalent to nonnegative conditional mutual information \(I(A:C\mid B)\). It is stronger than merely demanding nonnegative individual entropies. Infinite-dimensional divergent terms require a suitable extended formulation.

Reference

John Watrous, The Theory of Quantum Information.

Worked example or interpretation

For three independent fair classical bits encoded in orthogonal quantum states, S(ABC)=3, S(B)=1, S(AB)=2 and S(BC)=2 bits. Both sides equal 4 bits. This example saturates the inequality; correlations can make it strict.
