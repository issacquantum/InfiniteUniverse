Classical Action Functional

Equation

$$S[q]=\int_{t_1}^{t_2}L(q,\dot q,t)\,dt$$

Literal reading

S of the path q equals the integral from t one to t two of L of q, q dot and t, with respect to time.

Conceptual reading

Action assigns a number to an entire candidate history of a mechanical system.

Symbols

\(S[q]\) is the action functional; square brackets indicate dependence on a whole path. \(q\) denotes generalized coordinates; \(\dot q\) their time derivatives. \(L\) is the Lagrangian, a scalar function of coordinates, velocities and time. \(t_1,t_2\) are endpoint times; \(dt\) is the time differential and the integral accumulates L along the path.

Values, units and domains

L has units J and S units J·s. Time is in s. Coordinate units depend on the coordinate: lengths in m or angles in radians, for example. Action is not generally energy.

Meaning

A path can be assigned an action even if it does not satisfy the equations of motion. Comparing nearby paths leads to the separate stationarity principle.

Assumptions and limits

A classical Lagrangian description with a differentiable admissible path and a well-defined integral. Choice of L encodes the model and constraints; this definition alone does not select a physical trajectory.

Example

If L stays at an illustrative constant 3 J over a 2 s path interval, its action is 6 J·s.

Source

William Rowan Hamilton, On a General Method in Dynamics (1834) and Second Essay (1835).
