Kinetic Friction Magnitude

Equation

$$|f_k|=\mu_k N$$

Literal reading

The absolute value of f subscript k equals mu subscript k times N.

Conceptual reading

The dry-sliding model makes friction magnitude proportional to the normal load.

Symbols

\(f_k\) is a signed tangential friction component; bars give magnitude. \(\mu_k\) is the kinetic friction coefficient and \(N\) the nonnegative normal-force magnitude. Subscript k labels kinetic friction, not spring stiffness.

Values, units and domains

Both forces are in N. The dimensionless coefficient depends on the contacting surfaces and conditions; it has no universal value.

Meaning

This specifies magnitude. The force direction opposes relative sliding at the contact, which is not necessarily opposite the object’s velocity in an arbitrary frame.

Assumptions and limits

Empirical approximation for dry sliding. Actual friction can depend on speed, temperature and surface history.

Example

With μₖ = 0.3 and normal force 20 N, friction has magnitude 6 N, directed against relative sliding.
