Hydrogen Orbital Probability Element

Equation

$$dP=|\psi_{320}|^2r^2\sin\theta\,dr\,d\theta\,d\phi$$

Literal reading

d P equals the squared modulus of psi subscript three two zero, times r squared, times sine theta, times d r d theta d phi. The subscripts label quantum numbers, not an exponent.

Conceptual reading

Position probability is density times the spherical volume element.

Symbols

\(P\) is position probability and \(dP\) its infinitesimal contribution. \(\psi_{320}\) is the hydrogen wavefunction with principal quantum number 3, orbital angular-momentum number 2 and magnetic quantum number 0. Bars take its complex modulus and the superscript 2 squares it. \(r\) is radial distance, \(\theta\) polar angle, \(\phi\) azimuth, \(\sin\) the sine function, and \(dr,d\theta,d\phi\) integration differentials. The factor \(r^2\sin\theta\) is the spherical-coordinate Jacobian.

Values, units and domains

In SI, r is in m, the normalized wavefunction has units m⁻³ᐟ², and its squared modulus has units m⁻³. Angles are dimensionless radians, with r ≥ 0, 0 ≤ theta ≤ pi and 0 ≤ phi < 2pi. The volume element is in m³ and probability is dimensionless. In the article’s atomic-unit orbital formulas, r is the numerical radius in Bohr radii and the wavefunction uses matching normalization.

Meaning

Integrating this measure over a region gives its position probability. Sampling r and both angles uniformly would omit the orbital density and the spherical volume factor. The points represent a distribution, not an electron trajectory.

Assumptions and limits

The Born rule is applied to a normalized stationary orbital in the nonrelativistic Coulomb model. The expression is not a classical orbit or a time sequence. Atomic-unit wavefunctions cannot be inserted unchanged as SI densities.

Example

Integration over all space gives one by normalization. If a chosen region has integral 0.2, a fraction near 0.2 of a large independent sample is expected there. A finite sample fluctuates; the probability does not prescribe the exact number of points.
