Structure Formation

Equation

$$\begin{gathered}\text{initial density fluctuations}\\ \downarrow\\ \text{gravitational growth and dark-matter structure}\\ \downarrow\\ \text{gas accumulation and cooling}\\ \downarrow\\ \text{stars and, later, planetary systems}\end{gathered}$$

Literal Reading

Initial density fluctuations, followed by gravitational growth and dark-matter structure, gas accumulation and cooling, and stars with planetary systems forming later.

Common Reading

Initial density contrasts grow through gravity; dark-matter structures collect gas that can cool and form stars. Planetary systems form later in circumstellar disks.

Symbols

Initial density fluctuations: small departures from the average cosmic density.

stars, planets, and matter structures: larger cosmic structures assembled over time.

Meaning of the Equation

Gravity amplifies tiny initial density contrasts over cosmic time. That growth turns small early differences into galaxies, clusters, filaments, and the cosmic web.

Conditions and units

The arrows summarize processes in the standard cosmological picture, not a balanced reaction, a rate equation, or a single sequence followed everywhere. Dark matter supports gravitational structure but does not need to form molecules. Gas cooling and collapse depend on environment and chemical composition. Molecular hydrogen helps cool primordial gas in suitable early halos; atoms, ions, metals, molecules, and dust contribute differently in later environments. Molecules therefore aid some stages of star formation rather than initiating the cosmic web.

Reference

David Tong, Cosmology: Structure Formation.

Worked example or interpretation

A dark-matter overdensity can grow gravitationally before its gas has cooled enough to form stars. Gas cooling can later permit denser collapse within that structure. The arrows summarize these dependencies; they do not assign every object the same formation date or imply that dark matter must first become molecular.
