Neural Scaling Law

Equation

$$ L(N,D)\approx L_\infty+aN^{-\alpha}+bD^{-\beta} $$

Literal Reading

L of N comma D is approximately L infinity plus a N to the minus alpha plus b D to the minus beta.

Common Reading

For many language-model regimes, performance changes predictably as parameters, data, and compute scale.

Symbols

\(N\): model parameter count.

\(D\): dataset size.

\(L_\infty\): irreducible loss floor.

\(a,b\): empirical coefficients that set the size of each scaling term.

\(\alpha,\beta\): empirical scaling exponents.

Meaning of the Equation

This is an empirical scaling ansatz, not a fundamental law; constants depend on architecture, data, compute, and training regime.

Conditions and units

\(L\) is the specified evaluation loss, often cross-entropy in nats per token; \(N\) counts parameters and \(D\) training tokens in the chosen convention. Positive fitted exponents describe diminishing error contributions over the studied regime. Coefficients \(a,b,L_\infty\) use compatible loss units. The extrapolated floor is a fit parameter, not a demonstrated universal limit. Compute does not appear independently in this formula, and extrapolation outside the training and data regime requires validation.

Worked example or interpretation

If the fitted exponent alpha were 1/2, multiplying N by four would halve only the term aN^(−alpha). The data-dependent term and fitted floor remain unchanged when D is fixed. This algebraic example uses an illustrative exponent, not a universal measured value.

Notation details

The infinity subscript labels an extrapolated loss floor rather than substituting infinity into an arithmetic operation. Negative exponents indicate reciprocal powers, and the approximation sign identifies a fitted relation.

Source

Hoffmann et al., Training Compute-Optimal Large Language Models.
