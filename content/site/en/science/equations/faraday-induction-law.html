Faraday’s Law of Induction

Equation

$$\nabla\times\mathbf E=-\frac{\partial\mathbf B}{\partial t}$$

Literal reading

Nabla cross bold E equals minus the partial derivative of bold B with respect to t. The minus sign multiplies the entire time derivative.

Conceptual reading

A changing magnetic field produces circulation of the electric field.

Symbols

\(\mathbf E\) is electric field, \(\mathbf B\) magnetic flux density and \(t\) time. \(\nabla\times\) is the spatial curl in a right-handed coordinate system. \(\partial/\partial t\) differentiates at fixed position. Both sides are vectors, with their signs tied to the same orientation convention.

Values, units and domains

E is in V/m, B in T and t in s. Curl E is in V/m², equal to T/s. No universal rate of magnetic-field change is specified.

Meaning

For a fixed loop, electric circulation equals the negative rate of change of magnetic flux through its spanning surface. The sign expresses opposition to the flux change when circuit induction is interpreted with a consistent orientation.

Assumptions and limits

This local Maxwell equation describes the electric field. A moving conducting loop also experiences the magnetic part of the Lorentz force; a motional emf is not obtained by ignoring that force. Smooth fields allow the usual pointwise derivatives.

Example

If B=(0,0,b t), a compatible local field is E=(b y/2,−b x/2,0). Its curl is (0,0,−b), exactly the negative time derivative of B. Here b is a constant in T/s; boundary conditions can add a curl-free electric field.

Source

David Tong, Lectures on Electromagnetism; NIST, 2022 CODATA constants.
