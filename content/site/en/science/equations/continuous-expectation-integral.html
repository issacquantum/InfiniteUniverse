Expectation under a Probability Density

Equation

$$\mu=\mathbb E_p[f(X)]=\int f(x)\,p(x)\,dx$$

Literal reading

Mu equals the expectation under p of f of X, which equals the integral of f of x times p of x with respect to x. The brackets delimit the quantity averaged.

Conceptual reading

The integral averages an observable over its probability distribution.

Symbols

\(\mu\) is the scalar expectation, \(\mathbb E_p\) the expectation operator for density p, \(X\) a random variable and \(x\) a possible value. \(f\) is the observable function and \(p(x)\) the nonnegative normalized density. \(\int\) integrates over its full support, and \(dx\) denotes the integration measure, including volume in multiple dimensions.

Values, units and domains

The density has reciprocal units to dx, so probability is dimensionless. Mu and f have the same units, set by the observable. The expectation is finite when the absolute value of f is integrable against p. There are no universal physical constants here.

Meaning

This is the target average, not a finite sample estimate. Sampling from a different distribution without correcting the weights generally estimates a different quantity.

Assumptions and limits

The written density form assumes an absolutely continuous distribution with respect to dx. Discrete distributions instead use sums, and more general distributions use their probability measure. A finite expectation does not imply finite variance.

Example

For a dimensionless uniform variable on [0,1] and f(x)=x, p(x)=1 on that interval. Integrating x gives one half. This exact average does not say that every finite random sample has average one half.

Source
