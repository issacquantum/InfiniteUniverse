Quantum Circuit Complexity

Equation

$$ C_{\epsilon}(U)=\min\left\{m:\left\|U-G_m\cdots G_2G_1\right\|\le\epsilon\right\} $$

Literal Reading

C subscript epsilon of U is the minimum number m of gates for which the norm of the difference between U and the product G subscript m through G subscript one is at most epsilon.

Common Reading

Circuit complexity is the shortest gate recipe for building a target operation to a chosen accuracy.

Symbols

\(U\): the target unitary operation.

\(G_1,\ldots,G_m\): elementary gates from an allowed gate set.

\(\epsilon\): tolerated approximation error.

\(\|\cdot\|\): a norm measuring distance between operators.

Meaning of the Equation

This quantity minimizes the number of allowed gates needed to approximate the entire target unitary in the specified operator norm. It is not state-preparation complexity, which asks only for the desired action on a chosen initial state. The gate set, norm, and tolerance are part of the definition.

Conditions and units

The minimum ranges over all allowed gate sequences of nonnegative integer length \(m\), with \(G_1\) applied first. Take the norm to be the spectral operator norm; the unitaries and error \(\epsilon\ge0\) are dimensionless. The identity is the empty sequence. If no allowed sequence meets the tolerance the complexity is infinite. The displayed definition compares phases literally; identifying global phases would require minimizing over an additional phase.
