Landauer's Principle

Equation

$$ E_{\min} = k_B T \ln 2 $$

Literal Reading

E sub min equals k sub B T natural log 2.

Common Reading

Logically irreversible erasure of one bit at temperature T requires at least \(k_B T\ln2\) of heat dissipation in the ideal limit.

Symbols

\(E_{\min}\): the minimum energy dissipated as heat.

\(k_B\): Boltzmann's constant.

\(T\): the temperature of the environment.

\(\ln 2\): the natural logarithm of two, appearing because one classical bit has two possible values.

Meaning of the Equation

The displayed minimum applies to resetting an initially unbiased, uncorrelated classical bit in contact with a thermal reservoir, with an ideal reversible implementation of the reset. Bias, side information, and the memory’s physical free-energy changes require a more general accounting. This is not an energy cost for every logical operation.

Conditions and units

With \(k_B=1.380649\times10^{-23}\) J/K exactly and reservoir temperature \(T\) in K, \(E_{\min}\) is in joules. For example, at 300 K it is approximately \(2.87\times10^{-21}\) J per unbiased bit erased. The reset is logically irreversible even when its ideal physical implementation approaches thermodynamic reversibility.

Source

Rolf Landauer (1961), Irreversibility and Heat Generation in the Computing Process.
