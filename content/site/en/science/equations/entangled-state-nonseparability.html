Entangled State Non-Separability

Equation

$$ |\Psi_{AB}\rangle \neq |\psi_A\rangle \otimes |\psi_B\rangle $$

Literal Reading

Ket psi subscript A B is not equal to the tensor product of ket psi subscript A and ket psi subscript B.

Common Reading

The joint state of A and B cannot be written as a product of one state for A and one state for B.

Symbols

\(|\Psi_{AB}\rangle\): the full quantum state of the two-particle system taken together.

\(|\psi_A\rangle\): a state that would belong only to subsystem A.

\(|\psi_B\rangle\): a state that would belong only to subsystem B.

\(\otimes\): the tensor product, used to build a joint state from two independent subsystem states.

\(\neq\): not equal, meaning this factorization is impossible for an entangled state.

Meaning of the Equation

The subsystems do not have complete independent states of their own. Predictions require the joint state, which is why entanglement produces correlations beyond product probabilities.

Conditions and units

The inequality means that no pair of local kets gives this factorization, not merely that one chosen pair fails. Kets are dimensionless normalized vectors. Each subsystem still has a valid reduced density operator. For mixed joint states, entanglement means failure to express the density operator as a convex mixture of product states; failure to be a single product is not enough.

Example: why a Bell pair needs a joint state

Can two separate pure states describe a pair whose measurement results always agree? Take two spins, A and B. The ket labels 0 and 1 denote the two possible spin results along one chosen axis. In the Bell state (ket 00 plus ket 11)/√2, the first label refers to A and the second to B. Measuring that axis gives 00 or 11, each with probability one half, and never 01 or 10.

Suppose instead that the joint pure state were a product. Write the amplitudes for A as a and b, and those for B as c and d. Expanding the product gives amplitudes ac, ad, bc and bd for 00, 01, 10 and 11. The Bell state requires ac and bd to be nonzero, so all four factors must be nonzero. But then ad and bc cannot both vanish. No product can give those amplitudes.

In matrix language, put those four amplitudes into a two-by-two coefficient matrix. Its rank is the number of linearly independent columns. A product has rank one because its columns are proportional; the Bell state's diagonal matrix has rank two. This is another way to establish the same failure of factorization.

The argument assumes the specified joint pure state. Perfect agreement along one axis alone does not demonstrate entanglement: a classical mixture of 00 and 11 gives the same probabilities on that axis. Measurements in other bases test the coherence that distinguishes the Bell state from that mixture.
