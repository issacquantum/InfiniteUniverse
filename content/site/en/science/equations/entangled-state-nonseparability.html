Entangled State Non-Separability

Equation

$$ |\Psi_{AB}\rangle \neq |\psi_A\rangle \otimes |\psi_B\rangle $$

Literal Reading

Ket psi subscript A B is not equal to the tensor product of ket psi subscript A and ket psi subscript B.

Common Reading

The joint state of A and B cannot be written as a product of one state for A and one state for B.

Symbols

\(|\Psi_{AB}\rangle\): the full quantum state of the two-particle system taken together.

\(|\psi_A\rangle\): a state that would belong only to subsystem A.

\(|\psi_B\rangle\): a state that would belong only to subsystem B.

\(\otimes\): the tensor product, used to build a joint state from two independent subsystem states.

\(\neq\): not equal, meaning this factorization is impossible for an entangled state.

Meaning of the Equation

The subsystems do not have complete independent states of their own. Predictions require the joint state, which is why entanglement produces correlations beyond product probabilities.

Conditions and units

The inequality means that no pair of local kets gives this factorization, not merely that one chosen pair fails. Kets are dimensionless normalized vectors. Each subsystem still has a valid reduced density operator. For mixed joint states, entanglement means failure to express the density operator as a convex mixture of product states; failure to be a single product is not enough.

Worked example or interpretation

The coefficient matrix of the Bell state (ket 00 plus ket 11)/√2 is diagonal with two nonzero entries, so it has rank two. A product of two local kets has a rank-one coefficient matrix. The rank difference proves that this Bell state cannot factor into local pure states.

Source

John Watrous, The Theory of Quantum Information.
