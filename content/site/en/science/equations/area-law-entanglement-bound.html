Area-Law Entanglement Bound

Equation

$$ S(A)=O(|\partial A|) $$

Literal Reading

S of A is big O of the size of the boundary of A.

Common Reading

Entanglement scales with the boundary, not the full volume.

Symbols

\(S(A)\): entanglement entropy of region \(A\).

\(\partial A\): the boundary of region \(A\).

\(|\partial A|\): size or area of that boundary.

\(O(\cdot)\): scaling up to constant factors.

Meaning of the Equation

Area laws often apply to ground states of local gapped systems, not to all quantum states.

Conditions and units

\(A\) is a subsystem and \(S(A)\) the entropy of its reduced state, in bits or nats according to the logarithm. Boundary size is counted in lattice units or with a stated ultraviolet cutoff; a continuum area alone cannot set a dimensionless entropy. Big O is an upper bound, not necessarily proportionality. A spectral gap implies an area law in one-dimensional local systems under the theorem’s assumptions; a general higher-dimensional area-law theorem does not follow merely from having a gap.

Worked example or interpretation

In a one-dimensional chain, a contiguous interior interval has two boundary cuts regardless of its length. An area-law bound can therefore keep its entropy bounded while the interval grows. A volume-law state would instead have entropy proportional to the number of sites and would not satisfy that uniform bound.

Notation details

The boundary symbol is a set boundary, not differentiation here; bars count its size under the chosen lattice or cutoff convention. Big O means an upper bound by a constant independent of region size, not an equality of entropy and geometric area.

Source

M. B. Hastings, An Area Law for One Dimensional Quantum Systems.
