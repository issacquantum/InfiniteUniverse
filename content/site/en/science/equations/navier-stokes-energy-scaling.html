Scaling of the Squared Spatial Velocity Norm

Equation

$$\|\mathbf u_\lambda(t)\|_2^2=\lambda^{-1}\|\mathbf u(\lambda^2t)\|_2^2$$

Literal reading

The squared L two norm of u subscript lambda at t equals lambda to minus one times the squared L two norm of u at lambda squared t.

Conceptual reading

Three-dimensional parabolic concentration decreases the spatial kinetic-energy norm.

Symbols

\(\mathbf u\) is velocity; \(\mathbf u_\lambda(x,t)=\lambda\mathbf u(\lambda x,\lambda^2t)\) is rescaled velocity. \(\lambda>0\) is scale factor and \(t\) time. \(\|\cdot\|_2^2\) is the integral over all three-dimensional space of the squared velocity magnitude. The exponent −1 is a reciprocal factor; the time argument on the right is rescaled, not the same original-flow time.

Values, units and domains

Lambda is dimensionless. With velocity in m/s and space in m, the squared norm has units m⁵/s². Multiplying it by half the constant mass density gives kinetic energy in J.

Meaning

Two powers of velocity contribute lambda squared, while the volume change contributes lambda to minus three. A bounded energy norm can therefore coexist with concentration at smaller scales.

Assumptions and limits

Whole space, square-integrable velocity and corresponding time arguments. This is an identity for a rescaled field, not an assertion that an unchanged physical flow loses energy between identical times. A fixed periodic domain needs a separate treatment.

Example

For lambda = 2, the squared norm at rescaled time t is half the original squared norm at time 4t. It is not necessarily half the original norm at t.

Source

Charles L. Fefferman, Existence and Smoothness of the Navier-Stokes Equation.
