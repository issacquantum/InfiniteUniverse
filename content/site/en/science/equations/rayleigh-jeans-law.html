Rayleigh-Jeans Law

Equation

$$ B_\nu(\nu,T)=\frac{2\nu^2}{c^2}k_BT $$

Literal Reading

B sub nu of nu and T equals two nu squared over c squared times k sub B T.

Common Reading

This is the frequency form of the Rayleigh-Jeans law for blackbody spectral radiance.

Symbols

\(B_\nu(\nu,T)\): spectral radiance per unit frequency.

\(\nu\): the frequency of the radiation.

\(T\): the absolute temperature of the emitting body.

\(c\): the speed of light in vacuum.

\(k_B\): Boltzmann's constant, which connects temperature to energy.

Meaning of the Equation

The frequency label \(B_\nu\) matters: this is not the wavelength form \(B_\lambda\).

Conditions and units

This is the low-frequency limit \(h\nu\ll k_BT\) of Planck’s law for equilibrium blackbody radiation. \(T\) is in K, \(\nu\) in Hz, \(k_B\) in J/K, \(c\) in m/s, and \(B_\nu\) in W m⁻² sr⁻¹ Hz⁻¹. Extrapolating the formula to arbitrarily high frequencies produces the ultraviolet divergence and is outside its validity. Here \(h\) is Planck’s constant, in J s.

Worked example or interpretation

At fixed sufficiently low frequency, doubling temperature doubles this approximate radiance. At hν/(kBT)=0.01, Planck’s occupation factor is about 99.501 while the classical factor is 100, an overestimate of about 0.50%. The constants are h=6.62607015 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s, kB=1.380649 × 10⁻²³ J/K and c=299792458 m/s.
