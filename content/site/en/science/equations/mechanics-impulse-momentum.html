Impulse and Momentum

Equation

$$\mathbf p=m\mathbf v,\qquad\Delta\mathbf p=\int_{t_1}^{t_2}\mathbf F_{\rm ext}\,dt$$

Literal Reading

Vector p equals m times vector v. Delta vector p equals the integral from t one to t two of the external force vector with respect to time.

Common Reading

External impulse equals the change in momentum.

Symbols

  • \(\mathbf p,m,\mathbf v\): Momentum (kg m/s), mass (kg) and velocity (m/s).
  • \(\Delta\mathbf p\): Final minus initial momentum, component by component.
  • \(\mathbf F_{\rm ext},t_1,t_2,dt\): Net external force (N), initial and final times and integration interval (s); impulse has units N s.

Meaning of the Equation

Choose the system before a collision. A 1 kg cart moving at 4 m/s sticks to a stationary 3 kg cart. With negligible external impulse, 4 kg m/s divided by 4 kg gives 1 m/s for the combined carts. Kinetic energy falls from 8 J to 2 J; the remaining 6 J becomes deformation, heat and sound, not lost total energy. An elastic collision additionally conserves kinetic energy.

Scope and Limits

For a system use the sum of particle momenta. Conservation requires negligible net external impulse; it does not require kinetic-energy conservation. Mass exchange across the chosen boundary adds momentum flux.
