Leray–Hopf Energy Inequality

Equation

$$\begin{aligned}\frac12\|\mathbf u(t)\|_2^2+\nu\int_s^t\|\nabla\mathbf u(a)\|_2^2\,da\\ \leq\frac12\|\mathbf u(s)\|_2^2+\int_s^t\int_\Omega\mathbf f\cdot\mathbf u\,dx\,da\end{aligned}$$

Literal Reading

One half the squared L two norm of u at t, plus nu times the integral from s to t of the squared L two norm of the gradient of u at a, with respect to a, is less than or equal to one half the squared L two norm of u at s, plus the integral from s to t and over Omega of f dot u, with volume element dx and time element da.

Common Reading

Energy remaining plus viscous dissipation cannot exceed starting energy plus external energy input.

Symbols

  • \(\Omega\): The whole space or periodic domain.
  • \(s,t,a\): Starting time, later time, and integration time.
  • \(\|\mathbf u(t)\|_2^2\): \(\int_\Omega|\mathbf u(x,t)|^2dx\); twice the kinetic energy at unit density.
  • \(\|\nabla\mathbf u\|_2^2\): \(\int_\Omega\sum_{i,j}|\partial_j u_i|^2dx\); squared gradient norm.
  • \(\nu\): Positive kinematic viscosity.
  • \(\mathbf f\cdot\mathbf u\): External power density when mass density is normalized to one.

Meaning of the Equation

For smooth solutions with appropriate decay or periodicity this is an equality. Leray–Hopf weak solutions satisfy the inequality from \(s=0\) and almost every starting time \(s\), for subsequent times with the usual representative. It controls \(L^\infty_tL^2_x\) and \(L^2_tH^1_x\) on finite intervals, not all derivatives or peak speed. Pressure and transport disappear only after integration using zero divergence and the domain conditions.
