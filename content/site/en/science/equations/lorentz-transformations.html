Lorentz Transformations

Equation

$$x' = \gamma(x - vt),\qquad t' = \gamma\left(t - \frac{vx}{c^2}\right)$$

Literal Reading

x' equals gamma times the quantity x minus vt comma t' equals gamma times the quantity t minus vx over c to the power of 2.

Common Reading

Space and time coordinates transform between inertial frames while preserving the speed of light.

Symbols

\(x,t\): position and time in one inertial frame.

\(x',t'\): position and time in another inertial frame.

\(v\): relative velocity between the frames.

\(\gamma\): Lorentz factor.

\(c\): speed of light.

Meaning of the Equation

These transformations mix space and time so every inertial observer measures the same light speed and spacetime interval. They replace Galilean absolute time with spacetime geometry.

Conditions and units

The primed frame moves at constant signed velocity \(v\) along the unprimed x axis, with parallel axes and coincident origins at time zero. \(\gamma=(1-v^2/c^2)^{-1/2}\) is dimensionless, \(|v|<c\), positions are in m, and times in s. The transverse coordinates are unchanged. The term \(vx/c^2\) has time units. These are global inertial transformations in flat spacetime, not a general coordinate transformation in a curved geometry.

Worked example or interpretation

Take v=0.6c and an event with x=0 and t=1 s. Then γ=1.25, x′=−0.75c·s and t′=1.25 s. Substitution preserves the interval −c²·s². The two transformed coordinates describe the same event, not a new event or a changed physical trajectory; c=299792458 m/s.

Source

Sean M. Carroll, Lecture Notes on General Relativity.
