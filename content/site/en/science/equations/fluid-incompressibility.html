Incompressibility

Equation

$$\nabla\cdot\mathbf u=\sum_{i=1}^{3}\partial_{x_i}u_i=0$$

Literal Reading

The divergence of u, the sum of each velocity component’s derivative in its corresponding coordinate, equals zero.

Common Reading

A material fluid volume has no local rate of expansion or contraction.

Symbols

  • \(u_i\): The ith velocity component.
  • \(x_i\): The corresponding Cartesian coordinate.
  • \(\partial_{x_i}\): Partial derivative with respect to that coordinate.
  • \(\nabla\cdot\mathbf u\): Divergence, a scalar local volume-change rate.
  • \(\sum_{i=1}^3\): Sum over the three spatial coordinates.

Meaning of the Equation

For the flow map \(X(a,t)\), zero divergence keeps \(\det(\partial X/\partial a)\) constant, as long as the smooth flow exists; \(a\) labels an initial particle position. Fluid elements can still stretch in one direction and contract in another. A core defined by similarity coordinates can shrink because particles cross its boundary. Confusing that core with a transported parcel would contradict incompressibility.

Conditions and units

With position in meters and velocity in m/s, each divergence term has units s⁻¹. For a smooth material volume \(V(t)\), its fractional volume-change rate is the divergence. Constant density follows along particle paths from mass conservation; zero divergence alone does not require the density to be identical at different positions.

Worked example or interpretation

The local linear field u=(−ax,−ay,2az), with constant a in s⁻¹, has divergence −a−a+2a=0. It contracts in two directions and stretches in the third while preserving material volume. On the whole space it is only a local illustration, not a finite-energy velocity field.

Source
