Smooth Kinetic-Energy Balance

Equation

$$\frac{dE}{dt}+\nu\|\nabla\mathbf u\|_2^2=\int_\Omega\mathbf f\cdot\mathbf u\,dx$$

Literal Reading

The derivative of E with respect to t, plus nu times the squared L two norm of the gradient of u, equals the integral over Omega of f dot u.

Common Reading

The rate of change of kinetic energy equals the power supplied by the force minus viscous dissipation.

Symbols

  • \(E(t)=\frac12\int_\Omega|\mathbf u(x,t)|^2\,dx\): Kinetic energy with constant mass density normalized to one.
  • \(\mathbf u,\mathbf f\): Velocity and external force per unit mass.
  • \(\nu\): Positive kinematic viscosity.
  • \(\|\nabla\mathbf u\|_2^2\): Integral of the sum of squared velocity gradients.
  • \(\Omega,dx,t\): Spatial domain, volume element, and time.

Meaning of the Equation

Dotting momentum with velocity and integrating gives this balance. Incompressibility and the domain conditions remove the integrated advection and pressure terms; viscosity produces the squared-gradient loss. Forcing can add or remove energy according to its alignment with velocity. Without forcing, kinetic energy cannot increase. This integral control does not bound the peak velocity or every derivative.

Scope and Limits

The differential equality applies to smooth solutions with periodicity or sufficient decay and integrability. For Leray–Hopf weak solutions the corresponding integrated statement is generally an inequality.
