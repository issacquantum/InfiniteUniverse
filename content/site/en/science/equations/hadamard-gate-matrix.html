Hadamard Gate Matrix

Equation

$$ H = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & -1 \end{pmatrix} $$

Literal Reading

H equals one divided by the square root of two times a matrix with first row one, one and second row one, minus one.

Common Reading

The Hadamard gate turns basis states into balanced superpositions.

Symbols

\(H\): the Hadamard gate.

\(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\): the normalization factor that keeps the operation unitary.

\(\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & -1 \end{pmatrix}\): the matrix form of the transformation in the \(|0\rangle, |1\rangle\) basis.

Meaning of the Equation

Hadamard sends basis states into balanced superpositions with signs that matter for interference. It is useful because later gates can turn those signs into amplified or cancelled measurement outcomes.

Conditions and units

The matrix and state amplitudes are dimensionless. Columns give the images of the ordered computational basis states: \(H|0\rangle=(|0\rangle+|1\rangle)/\sqrt2\) and \(H|1\rangle=(|0\rangle-|1\rangle)/\sqrt2\). Here \(H\) names a gate, not a Hamiltonian; \(H^\dagger H=I\). An arbitrary input need not produce equal measurement probabilities.

Worked example or interpretation

Applying the matrix to the column (1,0) selects its first column, (1,1)/√2. Applying it again gives (1,0), since the sum in the first component is 2/2 and the difference in the second is zero. Thus this input returns to its initial state after two Hadamard gates.

Source

John Watrous, The Theory of Quantum Information.
