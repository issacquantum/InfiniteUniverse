Hawking Temperature

Equation

$$T_H = \frac{\hbar \kappa}{2\pi c k_B} = \frac{\hbar c^3}{8\pi G M k_B}$$

Literal Reading

T subscript H equals h-bar times kappa divided by the product two pi c k subscript B, which for Schwarzschild equals h-bar times c cubed divided by the product eight pi G M k subscript B.

Common Reading

For a Schwarzschild black hole, the Hawking temperature is inversely proportional to its mass.

Symbols

\(T_H\): Hawking temperature.

\(\hbar\): reduced Planck constant.

\(\kappa\): surface gravity.

\(k_B\): Boltzmann constant.

\(M\): black hole mass.

Meaning of the Equation

The second equality is for a Schwarzschild black hole; rotation and charge change the stationary surface gravity. A dynamical horizon does not generally have a unique equilibrium temperature.

Scope and Limits

The second equality is the Schwarzschild case. Rotation and charge change the stationary surface gravity. This equilibrium formula does not generally apply to dynamical horizons or describe the final Planck-scale endpoint of evaporation.

Conditions and units

In this SI convention, \(\kappa\) is an acceleration in m/s², normalized by the stationary time at infinity; \(T_H\) is in K and \(M\) in kg. \(G\) is in m³ kg⁻¹ s⁻², \(c\) in m/s, \(\hbar\) in J s, and \(k_B\) in J/K. For Schwarzschild, \(\kappa=c^4/(4GM)\). The formula describes the temperature referred to infinity for a stationary asymptotically flat black hole; local static measurements include gravitational redshift.
