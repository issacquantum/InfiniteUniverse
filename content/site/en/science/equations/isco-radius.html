ISCO (innermost stable circular orbit) Radius

Equation

$$r_{\text{ISCO}} = \frac{6GM}{c^2}$$

Literal Reading

r sub ISCO equals 6GM over c to the power of 2.

Common Reading

The innermost stable circular orbit for matter around a non rotating black hole.

Symbols

\(r_{\text{ISCO}}\): innermost stable circular orbit radius.

\(M\): black hole mass.

\(G\): gravitational constant.

\(c\): speed of light.

Meaning of the Equation

For Schwarzschild only, \(r_{\rm ISCO}=6GM/c^2\). Kerr ISCO depends on spin and on whether the orbit is prograde or retrograde.

Scope and Limits

The value \(6GM/c^2\) is the Schwarzschild result. Around rotating Kerr black holes, the innermost stable orbit depends on spin and on whether the orbit moves with or against the rotation.

Conditions and units

The radius is the Schwarzschild areal coordinate in meters, not a proper radial distance. With \(M\) in kg, \(G\) in m³ kg⁻¹ s⁻² and \(c\) in m/s, the right side has units m. This is the marginally stable timelike circular geodesic of a test particle; strictly stable circular orbits lie outside it. Pressure, magnetic forces, or self-gravity can affect a real accretion flow.

Worked example or interpretation

If GM/c² is 1 km for an illustrative Schwarzschild mass, the marginally stable circular orbit is at areal radius 6 km and the horizon is at 2 km. A massive test particle on a circular orbit just below 6 km has no radial stability.

Source

Sean M. Carroll, Lecture Notes on General Relativity.
