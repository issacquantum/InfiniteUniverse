Hubble's Law

Equation

$$v = H_0 d$$

Literal Reading

v equals H0 d.

Common Reading

Recession velocity equals the Hubble constant times distance.

Symbols

\(v\): recessional velocity.

\(H_0\): the present-day Hubble constant.

\(d\): distance to the galaxy or source.

Meaning of the Equation

For comoving objects in FLRW spacetime, \(v=H_0d\) relates the present rate of change of proper distance to that distance on a common cosmic-time slice. Using measured redshift as \(v\approx cz\) is only a low-redshift approximation; arbitrary distance definitions do not obey the same relation.

Conditions and units

With \(d\) in m and \(H_0\) in s⁻¹, \(v\) is in m/s; km/s and Mpc may also be paired consistently. Peculiar velocities relative to the comoving flow are not included. Recession defined this way is not a local special-relativistic relative velocity and can exceed \(c\) at large proper distances without local faster-than-light motion.

Worked example or interpretation

With illustrative H₀=70 km/s/Mpc and present proper distance 10 Mpc, the comoving recession rate is 700 km/s. An observed galaxy can have an additional peculiar velocity, so its measured redshift is not an exact distance reading from this multiplication alone.

Source

David Tong, Cosmology, The Expanding Universe.
