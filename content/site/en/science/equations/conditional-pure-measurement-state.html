Conditional Pure State after a Measurement

Equation

$$|\psi_m\rangle=\frac{M_m|\psi\rangle}{\sqrt{P(m)}}$$

Literal reading

Ket psi subscript m equals M subscript m applied to ket psi, divided by the square root of P of m.

Conceptual reading

Conditioning on an observed outcome normalizes the state transformed by its measurement operator.

Symbols

\(|\psi\rangle\) is the normalized input ket; \(M_m\) is the operator for outcome \(m\); \(P(m)\) is that outcome’s Born probability, equal to the squared norm of the numerator. \(|\psi_m\rangle\) is the normalized conditional ket. The root covers the probability; the denominator divides every component.

Values, units and domains

State amplitudes and probabilities are dimensionless in normalized finite-dimensional bases. Require P(m) greater than zero. The denominator is positive and the resulting ket has unit norm.

Meaning

The denominator removes the probability weight from the unnormalized transformed vector. Conditioning is distinct from merely discarding an unobserved outcome.

Assumptions and limits

Pure input and a single measurement operator for the recorded outcome. A coarse-grained outcome with several operators can yield a mixed state. Conditioning on zero-probability outcomes is undefined.

Example

For input (|0⟩ + |1⟩)/√2 and the projector onto |0⟩, the numerator is |0⟩/√2 and P(0) = 1/2. Dividing gives normalized state |0⟩.

Source

John Watrous, The Theory of Quantum Information (2018).
