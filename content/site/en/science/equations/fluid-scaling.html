Navier-Stokes Scaling

Equation

$$\begin{aligned}\mathbf u_\lambda(x,t)&=\lambda\mathbf u(\lambda x,\lambda^2t),\\ p_\lambda(x,t)&=\lambda^2p(\lambda x,\lambda^2t),\\ \mathbf f_\lambda(x,t)&=\lambda^3\mathbf f(\lambda x,\lambda^2t)\end{aligned}$$

Literal Reading

u subscript lambda at x and t equals lambda times u at lambda x and lambda squared t. p subscript lambda at x and t equals lambda squared times p at lambda x and lambda squared t. f subscript lambda at x and t equals lambda cubed times f at lambda x and lambda squared t.

Common Reading

Spatial concentration, faster time, and increased velocity can be rescaled together without changing viscosity.

Symbols

  • \(\lambda>0\): Dimensionless scaling factor.
  • \(x,t\): Coordinates of the rescaled solution.
  • \(\mathbf u_\lambda,p_\lambda,\mathbf f_\lambda\): Rescaled velocity, pressure, and forcing.
  • \(\mathbf u,p,\mathbf f\): Original solution fields and force.

Meaning of the Equation

On \(\mathbb R^3\), every momentum term acquires the factor \(\lambda^3\), and zero divergence is preserved. Initial data and the time interval also rescale. A mixed norm gains \(\lambda^{1-2/p-3/q}\); zero exponent defines criticality. The kinetic-energy integral gains \(\lambda^{-1}\), showing why energy bounds do not exclude fine-scale concentration. On a fixed unit torus arbitrary rescaling changes periodicity, so it is not a global fixed-domain symmetry.

Conditions and units

The mixed norm is \(L^p_tL^q_x\), with time integrability exponent \(p\) and spatial exponent \(q\); this \(p\) is not the pressure field. Its scaling compares corresponding rescaled time intervals. Velocity, pressure per unit density, and force per unit mass retain their respective physical units because \(\lambda\) is dimensionless.

Worked example or interpretation

For λ=2, the transformed velocity at (x,t) is twice the original velocity at (2x,4t); pressure is multiplied by four and forcing by eight at that same transformed point. The linked transformation of all three fields is essential: changing only the velocity amplitude is not this symmetry.
