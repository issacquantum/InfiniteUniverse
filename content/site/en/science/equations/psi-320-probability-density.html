Hydrogen 3d₍z²₎ Probability Density

Equation

$$ |\psi_{320}(r,\theta,\phi)|^2 = \frac{1}{39366\pi}\, r^4\, e^{-2r/3}\,(3\cos^2\theta - 1)^2 $$

Literal Reading

The squared magnitude of psi subscript three two zero of r, theta, phi equals one divided by thirty-nine thousand three hundred sixty-six pi, times r to the fourth, times e raised to minus two r over three, times the square of the quantity three cosine squared theta minus one.

Common Reading

Squaring the wavefunction gives the spatial probability density for finding the electron.

Symbols

\(|\psi_{320}|^2\): the probability density, not the wavefunction itself.

\(r^4 e^{-2r/3}\): the radial probability weighting.

\((3\cos^2\theta - 1)^2\): the angular probability factor that preserves the orbital shape while removing sign information.

\(1/(39366\pi)\): the normalization constant for the density.

Meaning of the Equation

Squaring the wavefunction gives where detections are more or less likely for the 3d z-squared orbital. The lobes and torus are probability structure, not a literal electron path.

Conditions and units

Here \(r\) is the dimensionless radius in Bohr radii, \(\theta\) the polar angle, and \(\phi\) the azimuth; the m = 0 state has no azimuthal dependence. The labels 3, 2, 0 are the principal, orbital, and magnetic quantum numbers. The formula assumes the nonrelativistic fixed-proton Coulomb model in atomic units. To recover an SI wavefunction multiply the amplitude by \(a_0^{-3/2}\) and use \(r=r_{\rm SI}/a_0\), where \(a_0\) is the Bohr radius; density then gains \(a_0^{-3}\). Normalization integrates the density with \(r^2\sin\theta\,dr\,d\theta\,d\phi\). The radial density factor alone is not the radial probability distribution, which includes the volume Jacobian.

Worked example or interpretation

At equal nonzero radius, the density on the axis is four times the equatorial density because the angular factors squared are 4 and 1. On the nodal cones both are replaced by zero. A probability for a finite region still requires integrating this density with the spherical volume element. The Bohr radius is approximately 5.29177210544 × 10⁻¹¹ m.
