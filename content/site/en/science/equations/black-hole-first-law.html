Black Hole First Law

The energy balance between nearby stationary black holes.

Equation

$$d(Mc^2) = \frac{\kappa c^2}{8\pi G}dA + \Omega_H dJ + \Phi_H dQ$$

Literal Reading

The differential of M c squared equals kappa c squared divided by eight pi G, times d A, plus omega subscript H times d J, plus phi subscript H times d Q.

Symbols and SI units

\(M\): mass (kg); \(c\): speed of light (m/s); \(G\): gravitational constant (m³ kg⁻¹ s⁻²); \(\kappa\): surface gravity normalized to time at infinity (m/s²); \(A\): horizon area (m²); \(\Omega_H\): horizon angular velocity (rad/s); \(J\): angular momentum (kg m²/s); \(\Phi_H\): horizon electric potential relative to infinity (V); \(Q\): electric charge (C). The symbol \(d\) denotes an infinitesimal change between stationary solutions. Every term has energy units (J).

Physical interpretation

At fixed constants, the change in total energy equals the area contribution plus rotational and electrical work terms. The area coefficient is evaluated on the unperturbed stationary solution; the horizon properties depend on the black hole, not on universal numerical values.

Scope and limits

This form applies to neighboring stationary, asymptotically flat Einstein-Maxwell black-hole solutions with fixed gravitational coupling. It is not an evolution equation for a merger and does not determine the microscopic origin of entropy.

Sources

Jacob D. Bekenstein, Bekenstein-Hawking entropy.

Worked example or interpretation

For neighboring uncharged nonrotating solutions, the angular-momentum and charge terms vanish. A positive area change then corresponds to a positive mass-energy change because surface gravity is positive. Adding rotation or charge introduces the respective work term rather than changing its physical units.

Source

Sean M. Carroll, Lecture Notes on General Relativity.

Physical constants

G≈6.67430 × 10⁻¹¹ m³ kg⁻¹ s⁻²; c=299792458 m/s exactly. NIST, CODATA 2022.
