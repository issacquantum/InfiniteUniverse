Effective Stiffness at a Potential Minimum

Equation

$$k_{\mathrm{eff}}=U^{\prime\prime}(x_0)>0$$

Literal reading

K subscript eff equals U double prime at x zero and is greater than zero.

Conceptual reading

Positive potential curvature acts as an effective spring stiffness near equilibrium.

Symbols

\(k_{\mathrm{eff}}\) is effective stiffness; eff means effective. \(U\) is potential energy, \(x_0\) equilibrium position and two primes denote two position derivatives evaluated there. The greater-than sign states strict positivity.

Values, units and domains

U is in J, x₀ in m and stiffness in N/m. The equilibrium also requires vanishing first derivative. Stiffness is a property of the particular potential, not a universal constant.

Meaning

The definition connects local geometry of the potential to the linear restoring response. Positive curvature resists small displacement.

Assumptions and limits

Smooth one-dimensional potential at a nondegenerate minimum. In several dimensions, stability depends on the Hessian on the allowed directions, not one scalar curvature.

Example

For a potential whose local quadratic term is 4x² J when x is in m, its second derivative is 8 N/m, so the effective stiffness is 8 N/m.
