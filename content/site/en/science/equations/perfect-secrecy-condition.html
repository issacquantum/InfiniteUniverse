Perfect Secrecy Condition

Equation

$$ P(M=m\mid C=c)=P(M=m) $$

Literal Reading

The probability that M equals m, given that C equals c, equals the probability that M equals m.

Common Reading

The ciphertext carries no usable information about the message.

Symbols

\(M\): the message random variable.

\(C\): the ciphertext random variable.

\(\mid\): conditioning on what an observer has seen.

Meaning of the Equation

Perfect secrecy can be stated as \(P(M=m\mid C=c)=P(M=m)\) or equivalently \(I(M;C)=0\): the ciphertext gives no information about the message.

Conditions and units

The equality must hold for every message value \(m\) and every ciphertext value \(c\) of positive probability under the encryption model. Lowercase symbols are realizations of the uppercase random variables. All probabilities are dimensionless; the equivalent mutual information is zero. This is an information-theoretic condition, stronger than computational difficulty, and it gives neither message authentication nor protection against omitted side channels.

Worked example or interpretation

For one bit encrypted by XOR with an independent uniformly random key bit used once, either ciphertext occurs with probability 1/2 for either message. Observing the ciphertext therefore leaves the original message probabilities unchanged. Reusing the same key for several messages does not retain this guarantee.

Source

Claude Shannon (1949), Communication Theory of Secrecy Systems.
