Amplitude-Encoded Classical Vector

Equation

$$ |x\rangle=\frac{1}{\|x\|}\sum_i x_i|i\rangle $$

Literal Reading

Ket x equals one over the norm of x, times the sum over i of x subscript i times ket i.

Common Reading

Encoding a classical vector into quantum amplitudes.

Symbols

\(|x\rangle\): the normalized quantum state representing the classical vector \(x\).

\(x_i\): the \(i\)-th entry of the classical vector.

\(|i\rangle\): the computational-basis state labeled by index \(i\).

\(\sum_i\): the sum over all vector indices.

\(\|x\|\): the vector norm, included so the quantum state has total probability one.

Meaning of the Equation

State preparation has a cost that depends on the input representation and the available operations.

Conditions and units

The vector must be nonzero, with a finite Euclidean norm \(\|x\|_2=\sqrt{\sum_i|x_i|^2}\). The basis states are orthonormal and dimensionless; any common units of the entries cancel in normalization. General vectors may require costly preparation. Special structured inputs can be prepared efficiently without QRAM; efficient access is an assumption to analyze, not a consequence of this identity.

Worked example or interpretation

For the real vector (3,4), the Euclidean norm is 5. Its encoded state is 0.6 times ket 0 plus 0.8 times ket 1. A computational-basis measurement gives probabilities 0.36 and 0.64, whose sum is one. The measurement returns one index, not both original entries.

Notation details

The sum includes each represented vector component once. The ket labels a vector in a complex Hilbert space; i is a basis index, not the imaginary unit in this expression.

Source

Ewin Tang, A quantum-inspired classical algorithm for recommendation systems.
