Position with Constant Acceleration

Equation

$$x=x_0+v_0t+\tfrac12at^2$$

Literal reading

X equals x subscript zero plus v subscript zero times t plus one half times a times t squared.

Conceptual reading

Position combines the initial position, the contribution of initial velocity and the accumulated effect of constant acceleration.

Symbols

\(x\) is the position coordinate; \(x_0\) and \(v_0\) are position and velocity at t = 0; \(a\) is the constant acceleration component; \(t\) is elapsed time. Subscript 0 denotes initial data; exponent 2 squares time. The factor one half is dimensionless.

Values, units and domains

Positions are in m, velocity in m/s, acceleration in m/s² and time in s. All terms on the right have units m. The signed quantities follow one chosen axis.

Meaning

Integrating constant acceleration twice gives a quadratic position function, with initial data fixing its two integration constants.

Assumptions and limits

The expression applies to a fixed axis with constant acceleration over the interval and t = 0 at the initial state. It cannot simply use the final acceleration if acceleration varies.

Example

With x₀ = 0 m, v₀ = 3 m/s, a = 2 m/s² and t = 4 s, x = 0 + 12 + 16 = 28 m.
