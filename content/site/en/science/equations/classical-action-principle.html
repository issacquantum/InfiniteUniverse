Classical Action Principle

Equation

$$S[q]=\int_{t_1}^{t_2}L(q,\dot q,t)\,dt,\qquad \delta S=0$$

Literal Reading

S of the path q equals the integral from t one to t two of L of q, q dot, and t, with respect to t; delta S equals zero.

Common Reading

The physical path makes the first-order change of action vanish among allowed nearby paths with fixed endpoints.

Symbols

  • \(S[q]\): Action, a functional of the entire path, in joule seconds (J s).
  • \(q,\dot q\): Generalized coordinate and its time derivative; their units depend on the coordinate, such as metres and m/s.
  • \(L(q,\dot q,t)\): Lagrangian in joules (J); T minus U for a standard conservative mechanical system.
  • \(t_1,t_2,dt\): Fixed initial and final times and infinitesimal time interval, in seconds (s).
  • \(\delta S\): First variation under a change of path, not an ordinary time derivative.

Meaning of the Equation

The integral assigns a number to each trial path. Stationarity means that small allowed changes have no first-order effect on that number. It need not be a minimum. Integrating by parts gives the Euler-Lagrange equation when endpoint variations vanish. For a spring, \(L=\tfrac12m\dot q^2-\tfrac12kq^2\) gives the oscillator equation, with mass m (kg) and stiffness k (N/m); the body does not consciously compare future paths.

Scope and Limits

Smooth paths and Lagrangian, fixed endpoint positions and times, and ideal holonomic constraints treated with independent coordinates. Friction is not automatically included by writing T minus U.
