Pressure Profile in a Uniform-Density Sphere

Equation

$$P(r)=\frac{2\pi G}{3}\rho_0^2\left(R^2-r^2\right)$$

Literal reading

Pressure equals two pi G over three times density squared times outer radius squared minus local radius squared.

Symbols and units

P(r): pressure in Pa; r: local radius in m; R: fixed outer radius in m; rho₀: constant density in kg/m³; G: gravitational constant in m³ kg⁻¹ s⁻²; pi: dimensionless. The product has units kg m⁻¹ s⁻², or Pa.

Physical meaning

Pressure is greatest at the center and falls to the imposed zero surface pressure. The center supports the accumulated weight of overlying layers even though the local gravitational acceleration vanishes there.

Derivation

Integrate the uniform-density pressure gradient from r to R. The integral of radius is half the difference of the squared limits. Apply P(R)=0 and reverse the sign to obtain the positive interior pressure. Differentiating the result recovers the local gradient, while evaluating it at R checks the surface condition.

Assumptions and limits

Uniform density, Newtonian spherical hydrostatic balance and zero external pressure. A nonzero imposed surface pressure would be added everywhere. This mechanical model does not solve temperature, opacity, fusion, transport or stability and is not a self-consistent model of a real gas star.

Worked example

For rho₀=1,000 kg/m³ and R=1.0 × 10⁹ m, central pressure is about 1.398 × 10¹⁴ Pa. At half the radius it is three quarters of that value, about 1.048 × 10¹⁴ Pa. The surface value is zero by construction, not because every real photosphere has zero pressure.
