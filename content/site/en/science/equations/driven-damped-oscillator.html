Driven Damped Harmonic Oscillator

Equation

$$m\ddot x+b\dot x+kx=F_0\cos(\Omega t)$$

Literal reading

M times x double dot plus b times x dot plus k times x equals F subscript zero times cosine of capital omega times t.

Conceptual reading

Inertia, viscous damping and a linear restoring force respond to a sinusoidal drive.

Symbols

\(x\) is displacement, \(t\) time, dots its time derivatives, \(m\) mass, \(b\) damping coefficient and \(k\) spring stiffness. \(F_0\) is driving-force amplitude and \(\Omega\) its angular frequency. Cosine acts on the entire phase \(\Omega t\); subscript 0 labels amplitude.

Values, units and domains

x is in m; t in s; mass in kg; b in kg/s; k in N/m; F₀ in N; Ω in rad/s. Require m and k positive and passive b nonnegative. Every term has units N; the cosine argument is dimensionless.

Meaning

The drive adds energy, damping dissipates it and the spring stores it. Initial conditions determine the transient part of the motion; the sustained response depends on drive frequency.

Assumptions and limits

Linear spring, damping proportional to velocity and a prescribed sinusoidal force. It excludes nonlinear stiffness and dry friction. The driving frequency need not equal the undamped natural frequency.

Example

Without drive or damping, a 1 kg mass on a 4 N/m spring at x = 0.1 m has acceleration −0.4 m/s².
