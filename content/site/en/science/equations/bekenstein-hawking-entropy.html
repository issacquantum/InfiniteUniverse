Bekenstein Hawking Entropy

Equation

$$S_{BH} = \frac{k_B c^3 A}{4G\hbar}$$

Literal Reading

S subscript B H equals k subscript B times c cubed times A, all divided by four times G times h-bar.

Common Reading

Black-hole entropy is proportional to horizon area, not volume.

Symbols

\(S_{BH}\): black hole entropy.

\(A\): horizon area.

\(k_B\): Boltzmann constant.

\(\hbar\): reduced Planck constant.

Meaning of the Equation

This is a semiclassical black-hole thermodynamics result and is not the same as ordinary gas entropy.

Scope and Limits

The formula gives the thermodynamic entropy associated with a stationary horizon. It does not directly tell us which microscopic degrees of freedom store the information, which is why it remains central to quantum gravity.

Units and scope

\(S_{BH}\) is entropy in J/K; \(k_B\) is the Boltzmann constant in J/K, \(c\) is light speed in m/s, \(A\) is horizon area in m², \(G\) is the gravitational constant in m³ kg⁻¹ s⁻², and \(\hbar\) is the reduced Planck constant in J s. The area ratio multiplying \(k_B\) is dimensionless. This leading semiclassical area law applies in Einstein gravity; quantum corrections and other gravitational theories can modify it.

Sources

Jacob D. Bekenstein, Bekenstein-Hawking entropy.

Worked example or interpretation

Doubling horizon area doubles the leading entropy. For a Schwarzschild family, doubling mass quadruples area and therefore entropy. Entropy divided by kB is dimensionless; converting it to bits requires a further division by ln 2.

Source

Sean M. Carroll, Lecture Notes on General Relativity.
