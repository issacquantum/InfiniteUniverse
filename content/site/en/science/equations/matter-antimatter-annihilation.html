Matter-Antimatter Annihilation

Equation

$$\text{particle} + \text{antiparticle} \rightarrow \gamma + \gamma$$

Literal Reading

A particle and an antiparticle produce two gamma photons.

Common Reading

A particle and its antiparticle annihilate into photons.

Symbols

particle: a matter particle.

antiparticle: the corresponding antimatter partner.

\(\gamma\): photon.

Meaning of the Equation

When a particle meets its antiparticle, their rest energy and kinetic energy can convert into photons or other allowed products. The reaction must still conserve quantum numbers.

Conditions and units

The arrow is a schematic allowed reaction channel, not a universal fate for every particle-antiparticle pair. Electron-positron annihilation into two photons is one example when the initial quantum numbers permit it. Energy, momentum, angular momentum, and charge must all be conserved; other channels can occur. \(\gamma\) denotes a photon, not the Lorentz factor, and the drawing supplies no reaction rate or photon energy without the initial state.
