Wormhole Flare-Out Condition

Equation

$$ b(r_0)=r_0,\qquad b'(r_0)<1 $$

Literal Reading

b of r subscript zero equals r subscript zero, and the derivative of b evaluated at r subscript zero is less than one.

Common Reading

This says the spatial geometry opens outward at the throat instead of pinching shut.

Symbols

\(b(r)\): shape function controlling the wormhole's radial geometry.

\(r_0\): throat radius, the narrowest allowed radius.

\(b'(r_0)\): slope of the shape function at the throat.

Meaning of the Equation

At the throat, the shape function satisfies \(b(r_0)=r_0\) and the flare-out condition requires \(b\prime(r_0)<1\).

Conditions and units

The prime is \(db/dr\), so b and \(r_0\) are lengths and \(b^{\prime}(r_0)\) is dimensionless. This is a local condition on a static spherical Morris-Thorne throat with \(r_0>0\), together with a finite redshift function. It does not establish global traversability or a realizable matter source. Classical Einstein equations connect the flare-out to null-energy-condition violation at the throat.

Worked example or interpretation

The choice b(r)=r₀²/r gives b(r₀)=r₀ and derivative −1 at the throat, which is less than one. Both statements concern the same local throat geometry; neither establishes that the required matter or a globally traversable passage exists.

Source

Morris and Thorne (1988), Wormholes in spacetime and their use for interstellar travel.
