Logistic Growth

Equation

$$\frac{dN}{dt}=rN\left(1-\frac{N}{K}\right)$$

Literal Reading

The derivative of N with respect to t equals r times N times the quantity one minus N divided by K.

Common Reading

A population grows quickly when small, then slows as it approaches a carrying capacity.

Symbols

  • \(N\): population size.
  • \(r\): growth rate.
  • \(K\): carrying capacity.
  • \(dN/dt\): rate of population change.

Meaning of the Equation

This assumes constant carrying capacity, no age structure, no spatial structure, and simple density-dependent growth.

Conditions and units

In the usual growth model \(r>0\), \(K>0\), and \(N\ge0\). \(N\) and \(K\) share population-count units, while \(r\) is inverse time. The per-capita growth rate falls as N increases; the total growth rate peaks at \(N=K/2\). Populations above \(K\) decline toward it. The continuous deterministic approximation does not resolve individual births, deaths, or fluctuations.

Worked example or interpretation

With illustrative intrinsic rate r=0.1 day⁻¹, carrying capacity K=1000 individuals and N=200, the model gives dN/dt=16 individuals/day. At N=K the rate vanishes. These parameters describe the chosen population and environment; they are not universal biological constants.

Source

Pearl and Reed (1920), On the Rate of Growth of the Population of the United States since 1790 and its Mathematical Representation (publication page).
