Construction: Unbounded Peak Speed

Equation

$$\limsup_{t\uparrow1}\|\mathbf u(t)\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb R^3)}=\infty$$

Literal reading

The limit superior as t approaches one from below of the L infinity norm over real three-dimensional space of u at t equals infinity.

Conceptual reading

Peak speed becomes arbitrarily large along times approaching the singular endpoint.

Symbols

\(\mathbf u(t)\) is the velocity field. \(\|\cdot\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb R^3)}\) is its spatial essential supremum in magnitude, disregarding changes on sets of measure zero. \(\limsup\) takes the limiting upper value and \(t\uparrow1\) specifies approach from below. \(\infty\) states that no finite limiting upper bound exists.

Values, units and domains

The construction uses normalized time with t<1. In physical units the speed norm is in m/s. Infinity here is the failure of any finite speed bound in the mathematical model, not a measured physical speed.

Meaning

The limit superior condition need not assert monotone growth. It identifies a breakdown of bounded velocity despite possible control of an integral energy norm.

Assumptions and limits

This is one property of the forced construction reported by OpenAI, with positive viscosity, zero initial velocity and a smooth force compactly supported in space and time. It is not a claim about every flow or the unforced problem, and this property alone does not prove that the momentum and incompressibility equations hold.

Example

The scalar reference function (1−t)⁻¹ᐟ² is finite at every t<1 and exceeds any fixed threshold sufficiently near 1. It illustrates finite-time divergence only; it is not the complete constructed velocity field.
