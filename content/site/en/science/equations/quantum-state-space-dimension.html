Quantum State-Space Dimension

Equation

$$ \dim \mathcal{H}_n = 2^n $$

Literal Reading

The dimension of Hilbert space script H subscript n equals two to the power n.

Common Reading

Each extra qubit doubles the dimension of the state space.

Symbols

\(\mathcal{H}_n\): the Hilbert space of an \(n\)-qubit system.

\(\dim\): dimension, meaning the number of independent basis directions.

\(2^n\): exponential growth from \(n\) two-level systems.

Meaning of the Equation

A system of n qubits needs 2^n basis amplitudes for a general state. That exponential growth is why large quantum states are hard to store directly on classical computers.

Conditions and units

\(n\) is the nonnegative integer number of two-level subsystems and \(\mathcal H_n=(\mathbb C^2)^{\otimes n}\). Dimension is a unitless basis count. It does not mean that measuring n qubits reveals \(2^n\) independent classical numbers; normalization, global phase, measurement limits, and possible special state structure matter.

Worked example or interpretation

Three qubits have 2³=8 computational-basis vectors, labelled 000 through 111. Four qubits have 16. This basis count doubles even though a single measurement of three qubits produces only one three-bit outcome.

Source

John Watrous, The Theory of Quantum Information.
