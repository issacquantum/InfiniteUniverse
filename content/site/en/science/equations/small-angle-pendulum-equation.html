Small-Angle Pendulum Equation

Equation

$$\ddot\theta+\frac{g}{\ell}\theta\approx0$$

Literal reading

Theta double dot plus g divided by ell times theta is approximately zero.

Conceptual reading

Near the downward equilibrium, a simple pendulum behaves approximately as a harmonic oscillator.

Symbols

\(\theta\) is angular displacement from the downward vertical, two dots its second time derivative, \(g\) local gravitational acceleration magnitude and \(\ell\) pendulum length. The fraction multiplies theta; \(\approx\) signals approximation, not exact equality for finite angles.

Values, units and domains

Theta is in radians, g in m/s² and positive ell in m. Both terms have units s⁻² since radians are dimensionless. g varies by location.

Meaning

Replacing sine of the angle by the angle makes the equation linear. Its oscillation rate depends on gravity and length, not bob mass.

Assumptions and limits

Requires small angular amplitude in radians and the ideal frictionless simple-pendulum assumptions. The neglected sine correction is cubic in angle, so the relative error grows with amplitude squared.

Example

At theta = 0.1 rad, sin(theta) ≈ 0.0998334, a relative linearization error of about 0.167%. For ell = 1 m and g = 9.81 m/s², the approximate period is 2π√(ell/g) ≈ 2.006 s.
