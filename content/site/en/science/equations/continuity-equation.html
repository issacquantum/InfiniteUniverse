Continuity Equation

Equation

$$ \frac{\partial |\Psi|^2}{\partial t} + \nabla \cdot \mathbf{J} = 0 $$

Literal Reading

The partial time derivative of the squared modulus of psi, plus the divergence of J, equals zero.

Common Reading

The time change of probability density plus the divergence of probability current equals zero.

Symbols

\(\partial |\Psi|^2 / \partial t\): the local rate of change of probability density.

\(\nabla \cdot \mathbf{J}\): the divergence of the probability current.

\(0\): the statement that probability is neither created nor destroyed locally.

Meaning of the Equation

Nothing is created or destroyed locally. Any increase inside a region must be explained by current flowing in, and any decrease by current flowing out.

Conditions and units

For a three-dimensional normalized state, \(\Psi\) has units m⁻³⁄², \(|\Psi|^2\) m⁻³, \(t\) s, and the probability-current density \(\mathbf J\) m⁻² s⁻¹. The divergence differentiates spatially, so both terms have units m⁻³ s⁻¹. Local conservation follows for the appropriate Hermitian dynamics and current. Conservation of total probability additionally requires vanishing net boundary flux; an open region may gain or lose probability.

Worked example or interpretation

If net outward probability flux through a region’s boundary is 0.02 s⁻¹, its contained probability decreases at that instantaneous rate. The density can change locally even though total probability over a closed system is conserved.

Notation details

Psi is the complex wavefunction, t is time, and J is the probability-current vector field; the modulus squared is a nonnegative scalar. The partial derivative holds spatial position fixed. Divergence sums the spatial derivative of each component along its own coordinate.
