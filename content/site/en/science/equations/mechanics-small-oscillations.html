Potential Curvature and Small Oscillations

Equation

$$\begin{aligned}U(x)&\approx U(x_0)+\tfrac12U^{\prime\prime}(x_0)(x-x_0)^2,\\k_{\mathrm{eff}}&=U^{\prime\prime}(x_0)>0,\quad\omega_0=\sqrt{k_{\mathrm{eff}}/m}.\end{aligned}$$

Literal Reading

U of x is approximately U at x zero plus one half U double prime at x zero times displacement squared. Effective stiffness equals positive U double prime. Natural angular frequency is the square root of stiffness over mass.

Common Reading

Positive potential curvature produces a linear restoring force to leading order.

Symbols

  • \(U,x,x_0,m\): potential (J), position and equilibrium position (m), mass (kg). Primes denote position derivatives. \(k_{\mathrm{eff}},\omega_0\): effective stiffness (N/m), natural angular frequency (rad/s). The expansion assumes \(U^\prime(x_0)=0\); higher displacement powers are neglected.

Meaning of the Equation

The potential-minimum argument requires more than smoothness: at equilibrium \(U'(x_0)=0\), and a nondegenerate stable minimum has \(U''(x_0)>0\). For displacement \(y=x-x_0\), Taylor expansion gives \(U(x)\approx U(x_0)+\tfrac12U''(x_0)y^2\). Primes mean derivatives with respect to position. The resulting force is approximately \(-k_{\mathrm{eff}}y\), with effective stiffness \(k_{\mathrm{eff}}=U''(x_0)\) in N/m and \(\omega_0=\sqrt{k_{\mathrm{eff}}/m}\). Higher terms must remain small over the motion. For \(U=\lambda x^4\), \(\lambda>0\) in J/m⁴, the minimum is stable but degenerate: its second derivative vanishes at zero and the leading force is \(-4\lambda x^3\). There is no nonzero amplitude-independent harmonic frequency from the quadratic approximation.

Scope and Limits

One conservative coordinate, constant positive mass, smooth nondegenerate minimum. A quartic minimum does not satisfy the positive-curvature assumption.
