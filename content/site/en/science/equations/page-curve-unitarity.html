Page Curve and Unitarity

Equation

$$S_{\rm rad}(t)\uparrow\ \text{until}\ t_{\rm Page},\qquad S_{\rm rad}(t)\downarrow\ \text{after}$$

Literal Reading

S sub rad of t rises until the Page time and decreases afterward in a unitary evaporation scenario for an initially pure state.

Common Reading

In a unitary evaporation scenario for an initially pure state, radiation entropy rises at first and decreases after the Page time.

Symbols

\(S_{\rm rad}\): entanglement entropy of Hawking radiation.

\(t_{\rm Page}\): the turnover time when the radiation and remaining black hole have comparable effective entropy capacities in the idealized Page argument.

Meaning of the Equation

For an initially pure closed system that evaporates unitarily and completely into the radiation being counted, the final fine-grained radiation entropy is zero. A remnant, omitted degrees of freedom, or an initially mixed state changes that conclusion.

Scope and Limits

This is a schematic entropy curve, not a local emission formula. Its force comes from unitarity and entanglement accounting, while the detailed mechanism for information recovery depends on quantum gravity.

Conditions and units

\(t\) and \(t_{\rm Page}\) use the same time coordinate and units. The arrows indicate a schematic trend, not a differential equation or a claim of strict monotonicity for every finite system. \(S_{\rm rad}\) is the von Neumann entropy of the reduced radiation state, in bits or nats according to the logarithm, or J/K if multiplied by \(k_B\). It is not simply the accumulated coarse-grained thermal entropy emitted.
