Gibbs Hamiltonian Sample Complexity

Equation

$$ S=\mathcal{O}\!\left(\frac{\log N}{(\beta\varepsilon)^2}\right) $$

Literal Reading

S is big O of the logarithm of N divided by the square of beta times epsilon.

Common Reading

A high-temperature Hamiltonian-learning sample bound, with logarithmic system-size dependence and inverse-square dependence on inverse temperature and target error.

Symbols

\(S\): number of samples or copies required.

\(\mathcal{O}\): asymptotic scaling notation.

\(N\): system-size parameter.

\(\beta\): inverse temperature.

\(\varepsilon\): target accuracy.

Meaning of the Equation

The bound concerns learning the coefficients of a local Hamiltonian from copies of its Gibbs state under the theorem’s locality, bounded-interaction, high-temperature, and success-probability assumptions. Smaller \(\varepsilon\) or smaller inverse temperature \(\beta\) requires more samples in this regime. Smaller \(\beta\) means higher temperature. The bound must not be extrapolated to arbitrary low temperatures.

Conditions and units

Here \(N\) counts qubits, \(\varepsilon\) bounds the error of each Hamiltonian coefficient in a specified normalized local-operator basis, and \(\beta=1/(k_BT)\) has inverse-energy units. Thus \(\beta\varepsilon\) is dimensionless. The bound suppresses constants depending on bounded locality and interaction degree and takes a fixed success probability. It is not a bound for arbitrary Hamiltonians or for reconstructing every matrix entry. Haah, Kothari and Tang, Optimal learning of quantum Hamiltonians from high-temperature Gibbs states.

Worked example or interpretation

Within the theorem’s regime and at fixed inverse temperature, halving the target coefficient error multiplies the displayed bound’s error-dependent factor by four. Halving beta at fixed error also multiplies that factor by four. Big O hides constants and therefore does not yield an exact sample count from these ratios.

Notation details

The full product beta epsilon is squared in the denominator. Logarithm bases only change constant factors here. The sample count S is distinct from an entropy despite the shared letter.
