Quantum Relative Entropy

Equation

$$ D(\rho \,\|\, \sigma) = \operatorname{Tr}(\rho \log_2 \rho) - \operatorname{Tr}(\rho \log_2 \sigma) $$

Literal Reading

D of rho relative to sigma equals trace of rho log base two rho minus trace of rho log base two sigma.

Common Reading

Quantum relative entropy is a divergence or distinguishability measure between quantum states.

Symbols

\(D\): quantum relative entropy.

\(\rho\): the state being compared.

\(\sigma\): the reference state.

\(\operatorname{Tr}\): trace operation.

\(\log_2\): logarithm base two.

Meaning of the Equation

It is not symmetric and is not a metric, so it should not be described as an ordinary distance.

Conditions and units

Both operators are positive with trace one and the logarithms are operator functions. The finite expression requires the support of \(\rho\) to lie within that of \(\sigma\); otherwise the relative entropy is infinite. The convention \(0\log0=0\) handles zero eigenvalues in the first term. The result is nonnegative and in bits for the displayed base-two logarithms.

Reference

John Watrous, The Theory of Quantum Information.

Worked example or interpretation

For rho the projector onto ket 0 and sigma half the qubit identity, the first trace is zero on rho’s support. The second is −1 because log₂(1/2)=−1. Their difference is 1 bit. Identical states give zero instead.
