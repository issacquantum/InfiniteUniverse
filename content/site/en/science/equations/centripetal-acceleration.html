Centripetal Acceleration

Equation

$$\mathbf a(t)=-\omega^2\mathbf r(t)$$

Literal reading

Vector a of t equals minus omega squared times vector r of t.

Conceptual reading

Uniform circular motion requires acceleration toward the centre.

Symbols

\(\mathbf a(t)\) is acceleration, \(\mathbf r(t)\) displacement from the circle centre, \(t\) time and \(\omega\) constant angular rate. The square applies to omega; the minus sign reverses the radial direction.

Values, units and domains

Position is in m, time in s, angular rate in rad/s and acceleration in m/s². Radians are dimensionless.

Meaning

The acceleration changes the direction of velocity while leaving speed constant. It is perpendicular to the tangential velocity.

Assumptions and limits

Constant-radius, constant-angular-rate circular motion. Nonuniform circular motion also has tangential acceleration.

Example

At position (2, 0, 0) m and angular rate 3 rad/s, acceleration is (−18, 0, 0) m/s².
