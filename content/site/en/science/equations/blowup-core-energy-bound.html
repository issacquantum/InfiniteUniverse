Construction: Core Energy Bound

Equation

$$E_{\mathrm{core}}=O(\tau^{1/2-3h})$$

Literal reading

E subscript core is big O of tau to the power one half minus three h.

Conceptual reading

Shrinking core volume can outweigh growing squared velocity in the energy integral.

Symbols

\(E_{\mathrm{core}}\) is kinetic energy integrated over the similarity-defined core; the subscript distinguishes it from total-fluid energy. \(\tau\) is remaining normalized time and \(h\) a fixed exponent. \(O\) is an asymptotic upper bound by a constant multiple, and the full exponent is one half minus three times h.

Values, units and domains

These are normalized construction variables with singular time 1. Here \(\tau=1-t>0\) is remaining time and \(0<h<1/100\) a fixed dimensionless exponent. Physical lengths, times and velocities require reference scales; a power of normalized time is not an SI dimensional equality.

Meaning

Core volume has scale tau^(3/2−h); squared dominant speed has scale tau^(−1−2h). Adding exponents yields one half minus three h. Its positivity makes this upper bound tend to zero despite increasing peak speed.

Assumptions and limits

This summarizes a leading-core estimate in the cited forced construction, not a universal flow law or an independent verification of the proof. The full result also requires the residual cancellations and smooth forcing. The similarity-defined core is not a transported material parcel.

Example

With h=0.005, the exponent is 0.5−0.015=0.485>0. Thus the bounded nonnegative core energy tends to zero. This says nothing about energy outside the core.
