Instantaneous Acceleration

Equation

$$\mathbf a=\frac{d\mathbf v}{dt}$$

Literal reading

Vector a equals d vector v divided by dt: differentiate velocity with respect to time.

Conceptual reading

Acceleration measures change in velocity, including changes of direction at constant speed.

Symbols

\(\mathbf a\) is acceleration, \(\mathbf v\) velocity and \(t\) time. Bold symbols are vectors. \(d/dt\) is the componentwise time derivative; equality relates the resulting vectors.

Values, units and domains

Velocity has units m/s, time s and acceleration m/s². Components depend on the trajectory and reference frame.

Meaning

Both increasing speed and turning can produce acceleration. Zero instantaneous speed does not imply zero acceleration.

Assumptions and limits

The velocity must be differentiable. Ideal instantaneous collisions require an impulse description rather than a finite ordinary acceleration at the discontinuity.

Example

A velocity component increasing by 2 m/s every second has constant acceleration 2 m/s² along that axis.
