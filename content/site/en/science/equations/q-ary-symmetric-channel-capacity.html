Symmetric Noisy Channel Capacity

Equation

$$ C=\log_2 q+(1-p)\log_2(1-p)+p\log_2\frac{p}{q-1} $$

Literal Reading

C equals the base-two logarithm of q, plus one minus p times the base-two logarithm of one minus p, plus p times the base-two logarithm of p divided by q minus one.

Common Reading

The maximum reliable information rate for a q-ary symmetric noisy channel.

Symbols

\(C\): channel capacity in bits per channel use.

\(q\): alphabet size, meaning the number of possible symbols the channel can send.

\(p\): total symbol error probability, meaning the chance that the received symbol is not the one sent.

\(\log_2\): logarithm base 2, so the result is measured in bits.

Meaning of the Equation

For \(q\ge2\) and \(0\le p\le1\), with \(0\log 0=0\), capacity decreases to zero at \(p=(q-1)/q\), where the output is independent of the input. It increases again above that point because systematic exclusion of the sent symbol carries information. It is therefore not decreasing over the entire probability interval.

Conditions and units

The channel is discrete and memoryless: the sent symbol survives with probability \(1-p\), and each of the other \(q-1\) symbols occurs with probability \(p/(q-1)\), independently across uses. Uniform input attains the stated capacity. The rate is an asymptotic coding limit with arbitrarily small decoding error, not error-free communication on every individual use.

Worked example or interpretation

For q=2 and p=1/2, the expression is 1+(1/2)(−1)+(1/2)(−1)=0 bits per use. For q=2 and p=1, the output is always the flipped input, so the receiver can reverse it and capacity returns to 1 bit per use. Error probability alone does not describe how informative the channel is.

Notation details

The last logarithm acts on the entire fraction p/(q−1). The products outside the logarithms weight conditional outcomes, and the endpoint convention takes limits of zero-weighted logarithms.

Source

Claude E. Shannon, A Mathematical Theory of Communication.
