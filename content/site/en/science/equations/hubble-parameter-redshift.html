Hubble Parameter and Redshift

Equation

$$ H(z)=H_0\sqrt{\Omega_r(1+z)^4+\Omega_m(1+z)^3+\Omega_k(1+z)^2+\Omega_\Lambda} $$

Literal Reading

H of z equals H naught times the square root of omega r times one plus z to the fourth, plus omega m times one plus z cubed, plus omega k times one plus z squared, plus omega Lambda.

Common Reading

\(H(z)\) gives the cosmic expansion rate at redshift \(z\).

Symbols

\(H(z)\): expansion rate at redshift \(z\).

\(H_0\): present-day Hubble constant.

\(\Omega_r\): radiation density parameter.

\(\Omega_m\): matter density parameter.

\(\Omega_k\): curvature density parameter.

\(\Omega_\Lambda\): dark-energy density parameter.

\(z\): redshift.

Meaning of the Equation

Radiation, matter, curvature, and dark energy scale differently with \(1+z\) in an FLRW model.

Conditions and units

This is the expanding branch of the Friedmann equation for separately conserved radiation, pressureless matter, curvature, and a cosmological constant. The \(\Omega\) parameters are their present-day dimensionless coefficients and sum to one; \(\Omega_k\) is a geometric curvature term, not a material density. \(\Omega_\Lambda\) represents constant dark energy, not an arbitrary evolving component. Cosmological redshift obeys \(1+z=a_0/a\). Both \(H\) and \(H_0\) have units s⁻¹, often quoted as km/s/Mpc. The parameters must be inferred from a model and observations, not assigned universal fixed values.

Equivalent notation

$$H(z)=H_0\sqrt{\Omega_m(1+z)^3+\Omega_r(1+z)^4+\Omega_\Lambda}$$The age-of-the-universe article uses the spatially flat case, \(\Omega_k=0\). Its square root contains matter, radiation and cosmological-constant terms only, with present-day fractions adding to one.

Worked example or interpretation

With illustrative present fractions Ωm=0.3, ΩΛ=0.7 and zero radiation and curvature, z=1 gives H/H₀=√3.1≈1.761. These selected values illustrate the formula; they are not presented as a new observational fit.

Source
