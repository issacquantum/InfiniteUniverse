Electromagnetic Wave Equation

Equation

$$\nabla^2\mathbf{E}-\frac{1}{c^2}\frac{\partial^2\mathbf{E}}{\partial t^2}=0$$

Literal Reading

The spatial Laplacian of vector E minus one over c squared times the second partial time derivative of vector E equals zero.

Common Reading

The electric field can propagate as a wave through empty space.

Symbols

  • \(\nabla^2\): spatial Laplacian, applied to each field component.
  • \(\partial^2/\partial t^2\): second time change.
  • \(c\): wave speed in vacuum.

Meaning of the Equation

This is the vacuum, source-free form of the electromagnetic wave equation.

Conditions and units

\(\mathbf E\) is the electric field (V/m), \(t\) time (s), and spatial derivatives use meters. Both terms have units V/m³. The equation follows in flat, source-free vacuum; Maxwell’s divergence constraints and initial data must also hold, so arbitrary componentwise wave solutions are not automatically electromagnetic fields.

Worked example or interpretation

A transverse plane wave depending on x−ct satisfies the vacuum wave equation: two spatial derivatives and two time derivatives differ by the factor c². Here c=299792458 m/s exactly. Boundary conditions and Maxwell’s divergence constraints still restrict the admissible vector fields.

Source

David Tong, Electromagnetism.
