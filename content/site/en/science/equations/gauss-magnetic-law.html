Gauss’s Law for Magnetism

Equation

$$\nabla\cdot\mathbf B=0$$

Literal reading

Nabla dot bold B equals zero; that is, the divergence of the magnetic field vanishes.

Conceptual reading

The magnetic field has no local magnetic-charge sources in ordinary Maxwell theory.

Symbols

\(\mathbf B\) is the magnetic flux-density vector. \(\nabla\cdot\) denotes divergence: the sum of the spatial derivatives of the three matching Cartesian components. Zero is the absence of magnetic charge in this equation, not the absence of a magnetic field.

Values, units and domains

B has units tesla (T), and its divergence has units T/m. The zero on the right has those same units. There is no physical constant or fixed field magnitude in this relation.

Meaning

The net magnetic flux through any closed surface is zero when the divergence theorem applies. This does not require all magnetic field lines to be closed curves; they may also extend to infinity.

Assumptions and limits

This is part of classical electromagnetism without magnetic monopoles. A theory admitting magnetic charge would modify it. Singular idealizations must be treated consistently as distributions.

Example

A uniform magnetic field of 2 T in the z direction has all spatial derivatives zero, hence zero divergence. The field is nonzero even though the equation’s right-hand side vanishes.

Source

David Tong, Lectures on Electromagnetism; NIST, 2022 CODATA constants.
