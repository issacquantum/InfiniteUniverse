Quantum Inequality Bound

Equation

$$ \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \rho(\tau) f(\tau)\,d\tau \ge -\frac{C}{\tau_0^4} $$

Literal Reading

The integral from minus infinity to infinity of rho of tau times f of tau, with respect to tau, is greater than or equal to minus C divided by tau subscript zero to the fourth power.

Common Reading

Quantum field theory allows local negative energy in special states, but it places strict magnitude-duration limits on it.

Symbols

\(\rho(\tau)\): energy density measured along an observer's worldline.

\(f(\tau)\): sampling function over a timescale \(\tau_0\).

\(C\): positive constant depending on the field and sampling choice.

Meaning of the Equation

This schematic bound uses natural units \(\hbar=c=1\) and a normalized sampling function. The inverse fourth power of the sampling time is characteristic of suitable free-field bounds in four-dimensional flat spacetime. Field choice, sampling, acceleration, curvature, and dimension matter; this is not a universal inequality for every quantum field or geometry, nor a demonstration that traversable wormholes are feasible.

Conditions and units

\(\tau\) is observer proper time, \(\tau_0>0\) the sampling width, and \(f\) a suitable nonnegative smooth sampling function with integral one, hence inverse-time units. \(\rho\) is a renormalized expectation value, not a classical mass density. In natural units energy density has inverse-length-to-the-fourth units. For a fixed dimensionless sampling profile in flat four-dimensional spacetime, restoring SI gives a bound proportional to \(-C\hbar/(c^3\tau_0^4)\), in J/m³; C is model- and sampling-dependent, not a universal numerical constant.

Reference

C. J. Fewster and S. P. Eveson, Bounds on negative energy densities in flat spacetime.

Worked example or interpretation

With the field and normalized sampling profile fixed, doubling the sampling width reduces the magnitude of the negative lower bound by sixteen. This restricts sustained negative averaged energy; it does not forbid every brief negative local expectation value.
