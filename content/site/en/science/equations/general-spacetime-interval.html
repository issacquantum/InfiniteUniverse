General Spacetime Interval

Equation

$$ds^2 = g_{\mu\nu}\,dx^\mu dx^\nu$$

Literal Reading

d s squared equals g subscript mu nu times d x superscript mu times d x superscript nu, summed over both repeated indices.

Common Reading

The spacetime interval combines time and space into one geometric separation.

Symbols

\(ds^2\): infinitesimal spacetime interval.

\(g_{\mu\nu}\): metric tensor of the spacetime geometry.

\(dx^\mu\) and \(dx^\nu\): infinitesimal coordinate separations.

Meaning of the Equation

Einstein summation convention is used. Metric-signature conventions vary, so signs depend on whether the page uses \((-+++)\) or \((+---)\).

Conditions and units

Indices run from 0 to 3. With signature \((-+++)\), a timelike interval has \(ds^2=-c^2d\tau^2\), where \(\tau\) is proper time. Choosing \(x^0=ct\) and length-valued spatial coordinates makes \(g_{\mu\nu}\) dimensionless and \(ds^2\) a squared length. Angular or other coordinate choices change individual metric-component units while leaving the interval unchanged. This is a local differential relation, not generally the squared finite coordinate separation.
