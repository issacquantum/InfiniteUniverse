Electron-Positron Pair Production

Equation

$$\gamma + \gamma \rightarrow e^- + e^+$$

Literal Reading

Gamma plus gamma produces e minus plus e plus: two photons produce an electron and a positron.

Common Reading

Two photons can produce an electron-positron pair when enough energy is available.

Symbols

\(\gamma\): photon.

\(e^-\): electron.

\(e^+\): positron.

\(\rightarrow\): transforms into or produces.

Meaning of the Equation

A single photon cannot produce an electron-positron pair in empty space while conserving four-momentum; pair production requires another photon, a nucleus, or an external field.

Conditions and units

The signs on \(e^-\) and \(e^+\) label charge, not exponentiation. In the center-of-momentum frame the available energy must be at least \(2m_ec^2\), about 1.022 MeV. Photon energies and their collision angle both affect that invariant threshold; total laboratory energy alone is insufficient. This is an allowed reaction channel, not a rate formula.

Worked example or interpretation

Two head-on photons of equal energy 0.6 MeV each have center-of-momentum energy 1.2 MeV, above the pair threshold. Collinear photons travelling together cannot meet that threshold merely by raising their total lab energy. The electron mass is approximately 9.1093837139 × 10⁻³¹ kg, c=299792458 m/s and one eV=1.602176634 × 10⁻¹⁹ J.

Source

Breit and Wheeler (1934), Collision of Two Light Quanta.
