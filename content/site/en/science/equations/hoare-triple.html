Hoare Triple

Equation

$$ \{P\}\;C\;\{Q\} $$

Literal Reading

P in braces, followed by C, followed by Q in braces.

Common Reading

If the precondition holds and the command terminates, the postcondition holds.

Symbols

\(P\): precondition, what must be true before execution.

\(C\): command or program fragment.

\(Q\): postcondition, what must be true afterward.

Meaning of the Equation

Under the usual partial-correctness interpretation, the triple asserts that execution from a state satisfying P, if it terminates, yields a state satisfying Q. The assertion needs a proof; writing it does not prove it. Total correctness additionally requires termination.

Worked example or interpretation

Take precondition x=2, command x:=x+1 and postcondition x=3. With ordinary integer arithmetic, executing the command from a state satisfying the precondition gives the postcondition. The braces delimit assertions; they are not sets to multiply with the program.

Notation details

P and Q are logical predicates on program states, while C is executable syntax interpreted under specified program semantics. The triple has a truth value, not SI units. Integer overflow, exceptions and nontermination depend on those semantics and must be included when relevant.

Source

C. A. R. Hoare, An Axiomatic Basis for Computer Programming.
