Weight, Spring Force and Friction

Equation

$$\mathbf W=m\mathbf g,\quad F_x=-kx,\qquad |f_s|\leq\mu_s N,\quad |f_k|=\mu_k N$$

Literal Reading

Vector W equals m times vector g. F sub x equals minus k times x. The magnitude of f sub s is less than or equal to mu sub s times N; the magnitude of f sub k equals mu sub k times N.

Common Reading

Gravity supplies weight, a linear spring restores displacement, and dry friction depends on contact conditions.

Symbols

  • \(\mathbf W,m,\mathbf g\): Weight (N), mass (kg), gravitational acceleration (m/s²).
  • \(F_x,k,x\): Spring-force component (N), stiffness (N/m), signed extension from equilibrium (m).
  • \(f_s,f_k,N\): Static and sliding friction components and nonnegative normal-force magnitude (N). Vertical bars denote magnitude, not subtraction.
  • \(\mu_s,\mu_k\): Dimensionless friction coefficients for the chosen surfaces and conditions.

Meaning of the Equation

Static friction adjusts to prevent relative sliding up to its limit. With N = 20 N and mu sub s = 0.4, its maximum is 8 N; a 3 N tangential demand needs only 3 N. Sliding friction opposes relative sliding, not necessarily the center-of-mass motion. The normal force is perpendicular to the contact surface and is not always mg.

Scope and Limits

Uniform local g, small elastic deformation for the spring, and approximate Coulomb friction. These are force models, not universal material laws.
