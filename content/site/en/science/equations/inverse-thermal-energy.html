Inverse Thermal Energy Parameter

Equation

$$\beta=\frac{1}{k_B T}$$

Literal reading

Beta equals one divided by the product k subscript B times T.

Conceptual reading

Beta converts an energy into a dimensionless thermal weight exponent.

Symbols

\(\beta\) is the inverse thermal energy parameter, \(k_B\) the Boltzmann constant and \(T\) absolute thermodynamic temperature. B labels Boltzmann. The denominator is the whole product; the numerator one is dimensionless.

Values, units and domains

k_B = 1.380649 × 10⁻²³ J/K exactly. T is in K and beta in J⁻¹. For ordinary positive-temperature canonical states T is greater than zero. Temperature is not in degrees Celsius in this formula.

Meaning

Large positive beta means a low thermal energy scale and stronger suppression of excited states. Small positive beta means higher temperature.

Assumptions and limits

The formula defines the canonical parameter away from zero temperature. Zero temperature is a limit. Negative-temperature ensembles require bounded spectra and special preparation; they are not colder than zero kelvin.

Example

At T = 300 K, k_B T = 4.141947 × 10⁻²¹ J and beta ≈ 2.41432 × 10²⁰ J⁻¹.

Source

David Tong, Statistical Physics; NIST, CODATA 2022.
