What is the universe?

The word universe comes from the Latin term universum, formed from uni, meaning one, and versus, meaning turned or combined. In Latin it referred to all things considered as a single whole. By the late sixteenth century, universe was recorded in English with the meaning of the totality of all that exists.

Source of etymological origin: Online Etymology Dictionary: universe.

In modern science, the universe is defined as the entirety of space, time, matter, and energy, together with the physical laws that govern their behavior.

Together, these elements form the structure of physical reality. The universe therefore refers to the complete system of space and time and all matter and energy within it.

This section has always felt personal to me because it is the closest formal answer to one of the earliest questions I can remember carrying: where did all of this come from. Long before I had real physics, the scale of the universe already felt like the place where existence, time, and structure stopped being separate questions.
