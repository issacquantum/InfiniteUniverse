Fixed-Axis Rotational Dynamics

Fixed-Axis Rotational Dynamics

Equation

$$\tau_z=I_z\alpha$$

Literal reading

Tau subscript z equals I subscript z times alpha.

Conceptual reading

For a rigid body rotating about a fixed axis, axial torque determines angular acceleration.

Symbols

\(\tau_z\) is net external torque along the z axis, \(I_z\) the moment of inertia about that axis and \(\alpha\) angular acceleration. Subscript z specifies the same axis for both quantities.

Values, units and domains

Torque is in N·m, inertia in kg·m² and angular acceleration in rad/s²; radians are dimensionless in SI. I is positive for mass away from the axis and depends on its distribution.

Meaning

A greater moment of inertia requires greater torque for the same angular acceleration.

Assumptions and limits

Rigid mass distribution with constant moment of inertia about a fixed axis. For unrestricted three-dimensional rotation the inertia tensor and changing angular-momentum direction matter.

Example

A net torque of 6 N·m on a body with I_z = 2 kg·m² gives angular acceleration 3 rad/s².

Source

MIT OpenCourseWare, 8.01SC Classical Mechanics (2016).
