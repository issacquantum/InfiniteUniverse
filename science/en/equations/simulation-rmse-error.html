Simulation RMSE (root mean square error)

Equation

$$ \mathrm{RMSE}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{N}\sum_i(\hat y_i-y_i)^2} $$

Literal Reading

R M S E equals the square root of one over N times the sum over i of the square of the entire difference y-hat subscript i minus y subscript i.

Common Reading

RMSE is the square root of the mean squared prediction error.

Symbols

\(y_i\): reference or measured value.

\(\hat{y}_i\): simulated value.

\(N\): number of compared points.

Meaning of the Equation

Squaring makes large misses count more strongly. Taking the square root returns the error to the same units as the simulated quantity.

Conditions and units

The sum includes the N paired real scalar values, with \(N>0\), all in matching units. The square root is nonnegative. RMSE includes bias and dispersion relative to the reference; it is not automatically a statistical standard error or a confidence bound. Complex data require modulus squared, and quantities with incompatible physical units cannot be combined directly in this scalar sum.

Worked example or interpretation

For two position errors of 3 m and −4 m, the mean square is (9+16)/2=12.5 m². Taking its square root gives RMSE≈3.536 m. Opposite error signs do not cancel because each error is squared before averaging.

Source

Driscoll and Braun, Fundamentals of Numerical Computation, least squares.
