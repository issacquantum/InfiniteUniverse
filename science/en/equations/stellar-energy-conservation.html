Energy Balance of a Stellar Mass Shell

Energy Balance of a Stellar Mass Shell

Equation

$$\frac{\partial L}{\partial m}=\epsilon_{\rm nuc}-\epsilon_\nu-\left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}\right)_m-P\left(\frac{\partial (1/\rho)}{\partial t}\right)_m$$

Literal reading

The increase in luminosity per unit mass is nuclear heating minus neutrino losses, internal-energy accumulation, and pressure-volume work.

Symbols and units

L: outward luminosity, W; m: enclosed mass coordinate, kg; epsilons: heating or loss rates, W/kg; u: specific internal energy, J/kg; t: s; P: Pa; rho: kg/m³. A subscript m means following a fixed mass shell. Every term is W/kg.

Physical meaning

A shell can radiate more than its nuclear production by losing stored energy or contracting. Mechanical equilibrium does not remove these terms.

Derivation

Apply the first law to a material shell: heating minus net outward energy flow changes internal energy and performs expansion work. Divide by shell mass and rearrange.

Assumptions and limits

Quasistatic spherical evolution without explicit kinetic-energy transport. L includes modeled transport channels. Nuclear binding-energy conventions in u and nuclear heating must be consistent; extra sources require explicit terms.

Worked example

In a thermally steady shell with negligible neutrino loss, luminosity increases outward by its nuclear heating. A cooling white dwarf instead has an important internal-energy term.

Source

Princeton: Equations of Stellar Structure
