Kraus Trace-Preservation Condition

Kraus Trace-Preservation Condition

Equation

$$\sum_k K_k^\dagger K_k=I$$

Literal reading

The sum over k of K sub k dagger times K sub k equals the identity. The adjoint occurs to the left of each original operator.

Conceptual reading

Every input state retains its total probability when all Kraus contributions are combined.

Symbols

\(K_k\) maps the input Hilbert space to the output Hilbert space; \(k\) labels Kraus operators and \(\dagger\) takes the adjoint. \(K_k^\dagger K_k\) is a positive operator on the input space. \(\sum_k\) sums all terms, and \(I\) is the identity on that input space.

Values, units and domains

All entries are dimensionless. The condition is an operator equality, not a statement about one particular input vector. In finite dimensions it implies trace preservation for the associated operator-sum map on every input operator.

Meaning

Cyclicity of the trace moves the adjoint next to its Kraus operator. The sum then becomes the identity, leaving the input trace unchanged. Complete positivity comes from the operator-sum form, not from this equality in isolation.

Assumptions and limits

The condition applies to the full channel. A single selected outcome generally satisfies only a trace-nonincreasing inequality. Kraus operators need not be square, and their individual products need not be the identity.

Example

For K₀ = √(1−p) I and K₁ = √p Z, where Z is the qubit phase-flip operator and 0 ≤ p ≤ 1, the products are (1−p) I and p I. Their sum is I for every permitted p, so the phase-flip channel preserves trace.

Source

John Watrous, The Theory of Quantum Information, Chapter 2.
