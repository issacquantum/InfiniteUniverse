Construction: Fixed Spatial Support

Construction: Fixed Spatial Support

Equation

$$\operatorname{supp}\mathbf u(\cdot,t)\cup\operatorname{supp}p(\cdot,t)\subset K\quad(0\le t<1)$$

Literal reading

The support of u at time t, union the support of p at time t, is contained in K, for t greater than or equal to zero and strictly less than one. The centered dots stand for the spatial argument.

Conceptual reading

Both fields vanish outside one fixed bounded closed region before the singular time.

Symbols

\(\mathbf u\) is velocity, \(p\) pressure divided by constant density, \(t\) normalized time and \(K\) a fixed compact subset of \(\mathbb R^3\). \(\operatorname{supp}\) is the closure of the spatial set where a field is nonzero. \(\cup\) takes the union and \(\subset\) means set containment here, without requiring strict containment. \(\cdot\) is a placeholder, not multiplication.

Values, units and domains

Time lies in [0,1) after normalization. Support and K are spatial sets, not numerical field amplitudes. In physical coordinates velocity is in m/s and density-normalized pressure in m²/s²; reference scales are needed to restore those units.

Meaning

The support restriction controls where the fields can be nonzero, not how large they can become inside K. A fixed support can contain a smaller region concentrating toward a point.

Assumptions and limits

This is one property of the forced construction reported by OpenAI, with positive viscosity, zero initial velocity and a smooth force compactly supported in space and time. It is not a claim about every flow or the unforced problem, and this property alone does not prove that the momentum and incompressibility equations hold.

Example

A field supported in a ball of radius 0.1 also has support in a fixed concentric closed ball of radius 1. Increasing its amplitude does not change that containment. This elementary example illustrates support only, not a fluid solution.
