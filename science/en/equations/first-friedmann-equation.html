First Friedmann Equation

First Friedmann Equation

Equation

$$\left(\frac{\dot{a}}{a}\right)^2 = \frac{8\pi G}{3}\rho - \frac{k c^2}{a^2} + \frac{\Lambda c^2}{3}$$

Literal Reading

the square of dot a over a equals 8 pi G over 3 times rho minus k c squared over a squared plus Lambda c squared over 3.

Common Reading

The equation constrains the squared Hubble parameter through mass-equivalent density, curvature, and the cosmological constant.

Symbols

\(a\): scale factor of the universe.

\(\dot{a}\): time derivative of the scale factor.

\(\rho\): mass density / energy density divided by \(c^2\).

\(G\): gravitational constant.

\(k\): spatial curvature parameter.

\(c\): speed of light.

\(\Lambda\): cosmological constant.

Meaning of the Equation

\(H=\dot a/a\). Because this equation constrains \(H^2\), it does not choose between expansion (\(H>0\)) and contraction (\(H<0\)). Initial data and the evolution equations select the history. Pressure enters the acceleration and energy-conservation equations.

Units and convention

Here \(\rho\) is total fluid energy density divided by \(c^2\), in kg/m³; it excludes the separately written cosmological constant. \(p\) is pressure in Pa. Cosmic time \(t\) is in seconds; overdots denote derivatives with respect to it. Choose \(a\) in meters and dimensionless \(k=0,\pm1\); then \(\dot a\) is in m/s and \(\ddot a\) in m/s². \(G\) has units m³ kg⁻¹ s⁻², \(c\) m/s, and \(\Lambda\) m⁻². Every term in the equation has units s⁻². These cosmological quantities depend on the model and epoch.

Scope and sources

Assumes a homogeneous, isotropic FLRW spacetime in general relativity. It is not a local description of individual galaxies. David Tong, Cosmology, The Expanding Universe.

Equivalent notation

$$H^2=\left(\frac{\dot a}{a}\right)^2=\frac{8\pi G}{3}\rho-\frac{kc^2}{a^2}+\frac{\Lambda c^2}{3}$$The intermediate equality explicitly replaces H by a dot divided by a; the dot denotes a derivative with respect to cosmic time.
