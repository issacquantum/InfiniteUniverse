Optimized Single-Use Holevo Information

Optimized Single-Use Holevo Information

Equation

$$C^{(1)}(\mathcal{E})=\max_{\{p_x,\rho_x\}}\chi(\{p_x,\mathcal{E}(\rho_x)\})$$

Literal reading

C superscript parenthesized one of channel E equals the maximum, over ensembles of p subscript x and rho subscript x, of chi for the ensemble of p subscript x and E applied to rho subscript x.

Conceptual reading

Optimizing the output ensemble’s Holevo information gives the classical communication rate available with product-state encoding and collective decoding.

Symbols

\(\mathcal E\) is a quantum channel, a completely positive trace-preserving map. \(C^{(1)}\) denotes optimized single-use Holevo information; the superscript labels one use, not a power. \(x\) labels ensemble members, \(p_x\) their probabilities and \(\rho_x\) their input density operators. \(\max\) optimizes over all allowed ensembles. \(\chi\) is the entropy of the average output state minus the probability-weighted average of its entropies. Braces specify the ensemble and parentheses the function arguments.

Values, units and domains

Probabilities are nonnegative and sum to one; density operators are positive with trace one. With entropy logarithms base two, the result is bits per channel use. Channel input and output dimensions must be specified.

Meaning

The HSW coding theorem concerns asymptotically many uses and collective decoding, even though this optimization contains one channel use. Entangled inputs across uses can require regularization for the unrestricted classical capacity.

Assumptions and limits

Finite-dimensional memoryless channel with product-state encoding. This single-letter quantity is not generally the unrestricted classical capacity and is not the quantum capacity.

Example

For a noiseless qubit channel, an equally likely pair of orthogonal input states has output Holevo information one bit. This reaches the one-bit single-use classical maximum.

Source

A. S. Holevo, The Capacity of Quantum Channel with General Signal States; John Watrous, The Theory of Quantum Information (2018).
