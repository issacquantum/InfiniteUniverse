Newtonian Gravitational Force Vector

Newtonian Gravitational Force Vector

Equation

$$\mathbf F=-\frac{GMm}{r^3}\mathbf r$$

Literal reading

Vector F equals minus G times M times m divided by r cubed, multiplied by vector r.

Conceptual reading

Gravity attracts the test body toward the source mass.

Symbols

\(\mathbf F\) is force on mass \(m\); \(M\) is the source mass; \(G\) is the gravitational constant. \(\mathbf r\) points from M to m and \(r\) is its magnitude. The minus sign reverses that direction. The denominator cubes the scalar distance; multiplying by the vector leaves an inverse-square magnitude.

Values, units and domains

G = 6.67430(15) × 10⁻¹¹ m³·kg⁻¹·s⁻²; parentheses give the standard uncertainty in the last digits. Masses are in kg, separation in m and force in N. Require positive separation; masses and positions depend on the system.

Meaning

The vector equation provides both the inverse-square strength and the attractive direction. It is a central force, parallel to the line joining the bodies.

Assumptions and limits

Newtonian point masses or nonoverlapping spherically symmetric bodies. The field is not accurate for relativistic strong-field dynamics; the point-mass formula is singular at zero separation.

Example

For two 1 kg point masses separated by 1 m, the force magnitude is approximately 6.67430 × 10⁻¹¹ N and points toward the other mass.

Source

Newton, Philosophiae Naturalis Principia Mathematica, Book I (HTML text); NIST, CODATA 2022.
