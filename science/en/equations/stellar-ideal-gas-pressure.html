Ideal Stellar Gas Pressure

Ideal Stellar Gas Pressure

Equation

$$P_{\rm gas}=\frac{\rho k_{\rm B}T}{\mu m_{\rm u}}$$

Literal reading

Gas pressure equals density times Boltzmann constant times temperature, divided by mean particle mass.

Symbols and units

Pgas: Pa; rho: kg/m³; kB: Boltzmann constant, J/K; T: K; mu: dimensionless mean particle mass in atomic mass units; m_u: atomic mass constant, kg.

Physical meaning

Both free electrons and ions contribute particle pressure. Ionization and composition change mu.

Derivation

The ideal-gas law gives pressure as particle number density times kB T. Divide mass density by mean particle mass to obtain that number density.

Assumptions and limits

Nondegenerate, weakly interacting ideal gas in thermal equilibrium. Radiation and degeneracy need additional terms or a more general equation of state.

Worked example

At fixed density and composition, doubling temperature doubles gas pressure. Holding pressure instead requires density to change.

Source

Princeton: Equations of Stellar Structure
