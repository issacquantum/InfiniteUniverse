Local Hamiltonian Expansion

Local Hamiltonian Expansion

Equation

$$ H=\sum_{a=1}^{m}\lambda_a E_a $$

Literal Reading

H equals the sum, from a equals one to m, of lambda subscript a times E subscript a.

Common Reading

A many-body Hamiltonian can be decomposed into local interaction terms, each with an unknown strength.

Symbols

\(H\): full Hamiltonian of the system.

\(E_a\): known local observable or interaction term.

\(\lambda_a\): coefficient measuring the strength of term \(E_a\).

\(a\): index labeling interaction terms.

\(m\): total number of terms.

Meaning of the Equation

The total energy operator is written as a sum of smaller interaction pieces rather than one opaque all-at-once object. In quantum complexity, that structure lets researchers ask whether the lowest possible energy of a many-part system can be checked or computed efficiently.

Conditions and units

For dimensionless Hermitian basis operators \(E_a\), real coefficients \(\lambda_a\) carry energy units and \(H\) has the same units. Locality means bounded support on subsystems; geometric locality is a further restriction. Coefficients are unknown in a learning problem but may be specified in a simulation or decision problem. Not every many-body operator admits an efficient expansion with only a polynomial number of bounded-locality terms.

Worked example or interpretation

For one qubit, choose the Pauli Z operator as the single dimensionless basis term and its coefficient as 2 J. The Hamiltonian then has eigenvalues +2 J and −2 J. With more local terms, coefficients need not be eigenvalues of the total Hamiltonian because the terms need not commute.

Source

Haah, Kothari and Tang, Optimal learning of quantum Hamiltonians from high-temperature Gibbs states.
