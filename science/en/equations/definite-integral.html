Definite Integral

Definite Integral

Equation

$$\int_a^b f(x)\,dx$$

Literal Reading

The integral from a to b of f of x with respect to x.

Common Reading

This adds up many tiny contributions of \(f(x)\) from \(a\) to \(b\).

Symbols

  • \(a,b\): the starting and ending limits.
  • \(f(x)\): the quantity being accumulated.
  • \(dx\): a tiny piece of the input axis.

Meaning of the Equation

A definite integral turns a changing rate or density into a total over an interval. It is the continuous version of adding many small pieces, so it appears in area, work, probability, charge, and accumulated change.

Conditions and units

\(x\) is the integration variable, and \(a,b\) have its units. The integral has units of \(f\) times units of \(x\). Existence requires the relevant integrability conditions; signed contributions can cancel, so the result is not always geometric area. Reversing limits changes its sign.

Worked example or interpretation

A constant velocity of 3 m/s integrated over times from 0 to 2 s gives displacement 6 m. If velocity changes sign, its signed integral gives net displacement, not total distance travelled.

Notation details

The integral sign with lower a and upper b gives the ordered limits. The differential dx specifies the variable and measure of integration; it is not an extra independent function to choose.

Source

MIT 18.01SC, Single Variable Calculus.
