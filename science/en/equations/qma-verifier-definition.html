QMA Verifier Definition

QMA Verifier Definition

Equation

$$ x\in L \Rightarrow \exists|\psi\rangle:\Pr[V_x(|\psi\rangle)=1]\ge a, \qquad x\notin L \Rightarrow \forall|\psi\rangle:\Pr[V_x(|\psi\rangle)=1]\le b, \qquad a-b\ge \frac{1}{\mathrm{poly}(n)} $$

Literal Reading

If x belongs to L, there is a witness state psi that verifier V subscript x accepts with probability at least a. If x does not belong to L, every witness state is accepted with probability at most b. The gap a minus b is at least the inverse of a polynomial in n.

Common Reading

QMA is efficient quantum proof checking with a guaranteed acceptance gap.

Symbols

\(|\psi\rangle\): the quantum witness or proof state.

\(V_x\): the verifier circuit for input \(x\).

\(a\): completeness threshold for accepting true statements.

\(b\): soundness threshold limiting false statements.

\(\mathrm{poly}(n)\): a polynomial in the input size.

Meaning of the Equation

A quantum witness can help verify hard quantum statements efficiently. The verifier still has bounded error, so the acceptance gap matters.

Conditions and units

\(x\) is an n-bit classical input and \(L\) the language of yes instances. The verifier belongs to a uniformly generated polynomial-size circuit family, and the normalized witness contains polynomially many qubits. The dimensionless thresholds obey \(0\le b<a\le1\). For a promise problem the second condition applies to promised no instances; inputs in the promise gap need not be classified. The definition does not assert that finding a witness is efficient.

Worked example or interpretation

With completeness a=2/3 and soundness b=1/3, a yes instance needs at least one acceptable witness reaching 2/3. A no instance must keep every witness at or below 1/3. Exhibiting one poor witness for a yes instance does not violate completeness; finding one successful witness for a no instance violates soundness.

Notation details

The existential quantifier means at least one witness, while the universal quantifier means every witness. The colon introduces the acceptance condition, output 1 means accept, and poly(n) denotes a positive polynomial bound, not one prescribed numerical polynomial.

Source

John Watrous, Quantum Computational Complexity.
