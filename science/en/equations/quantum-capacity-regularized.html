Regularized Quantum Capacity

Regularized Quantum Capacity

Equation

$$ Q(\mathcal{E})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\max_{\rho} I_c(\rho,\mathcal{E}^{\otimes n}) $$

Literal Reading

Q of E equals the limit as n goes to infinity of one over n times the maximum coherent information using n copies of the channel E.

Common Reading

The best asymptotic rate for reliably transmitting unknown quantum states through a noisy quantum channel.

Symbols

\(Q(\mathcal{E})\): quantum capacity of the channel.

\(\mathcal{E}^{\otimes n}\): \(n\) uses of the channel treated jointly.

\(I_c\): coherent information.

\(\max_\rho\): optimization over possible input states.

\(\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\): the asymptotic rate per channel use.

Meaning of the Equation

The regularization appears because the best quantum communication rate may depend on collective coding across many channel uses.

Conditions and units

This is the unassisted capacity of a finite-dimensional memoryless channel: each use applies the same completely positive trace-preserving map independently. \(\rho\) ranges over normalized joint input states of all n uses and may be entangled across them. With entropy measured using base-two logarithms, \(I_c\) is in bits and \(Q\) in qubits per use. Side communication, prior entanglement, memory, or energy constraints change the capacity problem; the displayed expression need not be directly computable.

Reference

John Watrous, The Theory of Quantum Information.

Worked example or interpretation

For the noiseless qubit identity channel, a maximally mixed input has output entropy 1 bit and can have a trivial environment of entropy zero. Its coherent information is 1 bit per use. Jointly using n such channels yields n bits of coherent information, so division by n gives capacity 1 qubit per use.

Notation details

For a channel with a chosen isometric dilation, coherent information is output von Neumann entropy minus environment von Neumann entropy for the same input. The environment output is obtained by tracing out the channel output from that dilation. Different dilations give the same entropy difference. The tensor power denotes independent channel uses, not an ordinary power of a matrix.
