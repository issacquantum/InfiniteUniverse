Two-Body Relative Orbital Energy

Two-Body Relative Orbital Energy

Equation

$$E_{\mathrm{rel}}=\tfrac12\mu v_{\mathrm{rel}}^2-\frac{GMm}{r}$$

Literal reading

E subscript rel equals one half mu times v subscript rel squared, minus G M m divided by r.

Conceptual reading

After subtracting centre-of-mass translation, orbital energy is relative kinetic energy plus gravitational potential energy.

Symbols

\(E_{\mathrm{rel}}\) is relative orbital energy; \(v_{\mathrm{rel}}\) relative speed, with rel meaning relative. \(M,m\) are the masses and \(\mu\) is their reduced mass, the product Mm divided by their sum. \(G\) is the gravitational constant and \(r\) positive separation. The square applies to speed; subtraction adds the negative potential.

Values, units and domains

G = 6.67430(15) × 10⁻¹¹ m³·kg⁻¹·s⁻²; parentheses give the standard uncertainty in the last digits. Masses and reduced mass are in kg, speed in m/s, separation in m and energy in J. Potential energy is zero at infinite separation.

Meaning

For noncollision Kepler motion, negative energy gives an ellipse, zero a parabola and positive energy a hyperbola. The classification concerns relative motion, not arbitrary bulk motion of the pair.

Assumptions and limits

Isolated Newtonian point masses or nonoverlapping spherical bodies. Dissipation, propulsion and other bodies change the energy balance.

Example

For illustrative values μ = 1 kg, relative speed 2 m/s and GMm/r = 3 J, the relative energy is 2 J − 3 J = −1 J, a bound energy.

Source

Newton, Philosophiae Naturalis Principia Mathematica, Book I (HTML text); NIST, CODATA 2022.

Orbital geometry and derivation

For an isolated pair, introduce reduced mass \(\mu=Mm/(M+m)\) and relative speed \(v_{\mathrm{rel}}\). Energy after subtracting center-of-mass translation is \(E_{\mathrm{rel}}=\tfrac12\mu v_{\mathrm{rel}}^2-GMm/r\), with zero potential at infinite separation. For noncollision Kepler trajectories, negative energy gives an ellipse, zero a parabola, and positive a hyperbola. At the escape threshold, final separation tends to infinity and relative speed tends to zero, so \(\tfrac12\mu v_{\mathrm{esc}}^2=GMm/r\), yielding \(v_{\mathrm{esc}}=\sqrt{2G(M+m)/r}\). For \(m\ll M\), this becomes \(\sqrt{2GM/r}\). There is no atmosphere, propulsion, or third-body interaction in this derivation. Comparable masses both accelerate: their distances from the center of mass are \(mr/(M+m)\) and \(Mr/(M+m)\).
