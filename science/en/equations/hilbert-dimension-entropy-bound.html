Hilbert Dimension Entropy Bound

Hilbert Dimension Entropy Bound

Equation

$$ \dim \mathcal{H}_R \lesssim e^{S_{\max}/k_B} $$

Literal Reading

the dimension of the Hilbert space for region R is less than or approximately bounded by e to the maximum entropy divided by Boltzmann constant.

Common Reading

Entropy counting the number of distinguishable quantum states available to a region.

Symbols

\(\dim \mathcal{H}_R\): the effective Hilbert-space dimension / maximum number of mutually distinguishable states assigned to region \(R\) under an entropy bound.

\(S_{\max}\): the maximum entropy allowed for that region.

\(k_B\): Boltzmann constant.

\(\lesssim\): less than or approximately bounded by.

Meaning of the Equation

If entropy bounds count the maximum number of distinguishable states in a finite region, then the independent Hilbert-space states available to that region are also bounded. This is an information-capacity statement about a region, not a proof that the whole of reality is finite.

Conditions and units

This inference requires an accessible finite-dimensional state space on which a maximally mixed state is allowed: its entropy is \(k_B\ln d\), with \(d=\dim\mathcal H_R\). A bound on the entropy of particular states or energy-constrained states alone does not bound the full Hilbert-space dimension. \(S_{\max}\) and \(k_B\) have units J/K, making the exponent dimensionless. The displayed approximate bound is conditional on the assumed gravitational entropy bound and the meaning of the effective regional state space, not a general theorem of continuum quantum field theory.
