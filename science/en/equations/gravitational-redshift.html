Gravitational Redshift

Gravitational Redshift

Equation

$$\frac{\Delta f}{f} = \frac{\Delta\Phi}{c^2}$$

Literal Reading

Delta f over f equals delta gravitational potential over c squared.

Common Reading

In the weak-field approximation, a frequency shift is proportional to the change in gravitational potential.

Symbols

\(\Delta f\): change in measured frequency.

\(f\): reference frequency.

\(\Delta\Phi\): difference in gravitational potential.

\(c\): speed of light.

Meaning of the Equation

This is not the full Schwarzschild redshift formula; it is the weak-field limit.

Conditions and units

Let \(f=f_{\rm em}\) be the frequency measured by a stationary emitter and \(\Delta f=f_{\rm obs}-f_{\rm em}\), with \(f_{\rm obs}\) measured by a stationary receiver. Define \(\Delta\Phi=\Phi_{\rm em}-\Phi_{\rm obs}\), in m²/s². The displayed equality holds to first order in weak potential differences, with no relative-motion Doppler shift. Since \(c=299792458\) m/s exactly, both sides are dimensionless. Light climbing from a deeper potential has \(\Delta\Phi<0\) and loses frequency. In Schwarzschild spacetime the exact static-observer ratio is \(f_{\rm obs}/f_{\rm em}=\sqrt{(1-2GM/(r_{\rm em}c^2))/(1-2GM/(r_{\rm obs}c^2))}\). Here \(M\) is the central mass, \(G\) the gravitational constant, and both areal radii lie outside the horizon. Its weak-field expansion with \(\Phi=-GM/r\) gives the stated sign.

Worked example or interpretation

For an illustrative upward height difference of 100 m in approximately uniform gravity 9.8 m/s², the chosen potential difference is −980 m²/s². The fractional frequency change is about −1.0904 × 10⁻¹⁴. The negative sign denotes a redshift under the emitter-minus-receiver convention.

Source

Sean M. Carroll, Lecture Notes on General Relativity.
