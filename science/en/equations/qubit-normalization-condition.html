Qubit Normalization Condition

Qubit Normalization Condition

Equation

$$ |\alpha|^2 + |\beta|^2 = 1 $$

Literal Reading

The squared modulus of alpha plus the squared modulus of beta equals one.

Common Reading

The total probability of all possible measurement outcomes for a single qubit must sum to one.

Symbols

\(|\alpha|^2\): the probability of measuring the qubit in state \(|0\rangle\).

\(|\beta|^2\): the probability of measuring the qubit in state \(|1\rangle\).

\(= 1\): the total probability is normalized, so no probability is missing or exceeds the allowed total.

Meaning of the Equation

The two basis probabilities of a qubit must add to one. The amplitudes can be complex, but their squared magnitudes are the measurable probabilities.

Conditions and units

\(\alpha,\beta\) are dimensionless complex amplitudes in \(|\psi\rangle=\alpha|0\rangle+\beta|1\rangle\), with an orthonormal computational basis. Their squared moduli, not their ordinary complex squares, are nonnegative probabilities. This is a pure-state representation; a general mixed qubit is described by a density matrix.

Worked example or interpretation

Take alpha=3/5 and beta=4i/5, with i the imaginary unit. Their squared moduli are 9/25 and 16/25, adding to one. Squaring beta without taking its modulus would give a negative number and would not be a probability.

Source

John Watrous, The Theory of Quantum Information.
