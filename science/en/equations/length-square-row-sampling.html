Length-Square Row Sampling

Length-Square Row Sampling

Equation

$$\Pr[i]=\frac{\|A_{i,*}\|_2^2}{\|A\|_F^2}$$

Literal reading

The probability of row i equals the squared Euclidean norm of row i of A divided by the squared Frobenius norm of A.

Conceptual reading

Rows are selected in proportion to their total squared entry magnitude.

Symbols

\(A\) is a finite matrix; \(i\) is a row index; \(A_{i,*}\) denotes its entire ith row, with the star standing for all columns. \(\Pr[i]\) is its selection probability. Double bars denote a norm, the subscript 2 specifies the Euclidean norm, and F specifies the Frobenius norm. The outer squares square each norm.

Values, units and domains

The matrix must be nonzero. The squared Frobenius norm sums squared absolute values of all entries, while the numerator sums only row i. Common entry units cancel, yielding dimensionless probabilities between zero and one. Zero rows have zero probability.

Meaning

The row probabilities sum to one because rows partition the matrix entries. The identity defines a distribution, not the time needed to draw a sample.

Assumptions and limits

Efficient implementation requires the sampling data-access model or appropriate preprocessing. An arbitrary matrix does not become cheap to read merely because this distribution is defined.

Example

For rows (3,4) and (0,0), their squared norms are 25 and 0. The Frobenius norm squared is 25, so row probabilities are 1 and 0.

Source

Ewin Tang, A quantum-inspired classical algorithm for recommendation systems.
