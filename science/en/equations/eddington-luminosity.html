Eddington Luminosity

Eddington Luminosity

Equation

$$L_{\rm Edd} = \frac{4\pi GMm_p c}{\sigma_T}$$

Literal Reading

L sub Edd equals 4 pi GMm sub p c over sigma sub T.

Common Reading

The luminosity where outward radiation pressure balances inward gravity for ionized hydrogen.

Symbols

\(L_{\rm Edd}\): Eddington luminosity.

\(M\): mass of the compact object.

\(m_p\): proton mass.

\(\sigma_T\): Thomson scattering cross section.

Meaning of the Equation

This assumes a spherical radiation field acting on ionized hydrogen plasma and balance between gravity and radiation pressure through electron scattering opacity.

Scope and Limits

The formula assumes spherical ionized hydrogen and Thomson scattering. Disks, magnetic fields, anisotropic radiation, and composition can shift the practical limit, so it is a benchmark rather than an absolute wall.

Conditions and units

\(L_{\rm Edd}\) is in watts, \(M,m_p\) in kg, \(G\) in m³ kg⁻¹ s⁻², \(c\) in m/s and \(\sigma_T\) in m². The opacity model uses roughly one electron per proton and photon energies in the Thomson regime. The balance is a Newtonian spherical estimate and does not require matter actually to be accreting.

Worked example or interpretation

For an illustrative central mass of 10³⁰ kg, the ionized-hydrogen estimate is approximately 6.32 × 10³⁰ W. It scales linearly with mass. The constants are mp≈1.67262192595 × 10⁻²⁷ kg and σT≈6.6524587051 × 10⁻²⁹ m²; the electron scattering and proton mass enter different parts of the force balance.

Source

Richard Mushotzky, Accreting Sources, lecture 4.

Physical constants

G≈6.67430 × 10⁻¹¹ m³ kg⁻¹ s⁻²; c=299792458 m/s exactly. NIST, CODATA 2022.
