Cosmological Horizons

Cosmological Horizons

Equation

$$d_p(t)=a(t)\int_0^t\frac{c\,dt'}{a(t')},\qquad d_e(t)=a(t)\int_t^\infty\frac{c\,dt'}{a(t')}$$

Literal Reading

d subscript p of t equals a of t times the integral from zero to t of c d t prime divided by a of t prime; d subscript e of t equals a of t times the integral from t to infinity of the same integrand.

Common Reading

These are limits on what could have influenced us and what we may ever influence or observe.

Symbols

\(d_p(t)\): particle horizon distance.

\(d_e(t)\): event horizon distance.

\(a(t)\): scale factor.

Meaning of the Equation

These integrals fold the travel of light through cosmic expansion. The particle horizon asks how far signals could have reached us by time t; the event horizon asks what signals can ever reach us in the future, depending on the later expansion history.

Scope and Limits

The event-horizon integral only exists as a finite distance when the future integral converges; not every FLRW cosmology has a finite event horizon.

Conditions and units

\(t,t^{\prime}\) are cosmic times in seconds, with \(t^{\prime}\) a dummy integration variable; \(c\) is in m/s. These are radial proper distances on a constant-time FLRW slice, in meters. The normalization of \(a\) cancels. The lower limit assumes an initial boundary at \(t=0\); replace it for a different past history. The upper limit assumes an indefinitely existing future; use its endpoint otherwise. Horizon existence depends on integral convergence, not merely on instantaneous expansion.
