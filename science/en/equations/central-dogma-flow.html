Central Dogma Flow

Central Dogma Flow

Equation

$$\mathrm{DNA}\rightarrow\mathrm{RNA}\rightarrow\mathrm{protein}$$

Literal Reading

DNA goes to RNA goes to protein.

Common Reading

Genetic sequence information is commonly copied from DNA to RNA and then used to build proteins.

Symbols

  • DNA: long-term hereditary sequence storage.
  • RNA: working transcript or regulatory molecule.
  • Protein: folded molecule that often performs cellular work.

Meaning of the Equation

Standard gene expression is DNA → RNA → protein, but biology also includes reverse transcription, RNA editing, noncoding RNA, epigenetic regulation, and feedback.

Conditions and units

The arrows denote sequence-information transfer by transcription and translation, not material conversion or a kinetic equation. DNA is deoxyribonucleic acid; RNA is ribonucleic acid. Reverse transcription transfers RNA sequence information to DNA and does not contradict Crick’s restriction against transfer from protein sequence back to nucleic-acid sequence. No rates or physical units are encoded by this diagram.

Worked example or interpretation

For a protein-coding gene, transcription produces RNA using a DNA template, and translation uses an RNA sequence to specify an amino-acid sequence. The arrows describe sequence-information transfer. They do not imply that all RNA is translated or exclude reverse transcription from RNA to DNA.

Source

Francis Crick (1970), Central Dogma of Molecular Biology.
