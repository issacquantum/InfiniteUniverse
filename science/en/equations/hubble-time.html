Hubble Time

Hubble Time

Equation

$$t_H = \frac{1}{H_0}$$

Literal Reading

t sub H equals 1 over H0.

Common Reading

\(1/H_0\) is an expansion timescale; it is not exactly the universe’s age except in special cosmological models.

Symbols

\(t_H\): Hubble time.

\(H_0\): present-day Hubble constant.

Meaning of the Equation

The Hubble time is a rough expansion timescale from today's Hubble constant. It is useful for scale, but the true age of the universe depends on the full expansion history.

Conditions and units

For positive \(H_0\) in s⁻¹, \(t_H\) is in seconds. A quoted value in km/s/Mpc must first be converted to inverse time; one Mpc is approximately \(3.086\times10^{19}\) km. \(H_0\) is a measured, model-dependent cosmological parameter rather than a universal constant.

Worked example or interpretation

For illustrative H₀=70 km/s/Mpc and one Mpc≈3.085677581 × 10¹⁹ km, the inverse is about 4.408 × 10¹⁷ s, or 13.97 billion years. This is the Hubble timescale for that chosen value, not by itself a determination of cosmic age.

Source

David Tong, Cosmology, The Expanding Universe.
