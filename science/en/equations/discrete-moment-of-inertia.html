Moment of Inertia about an Axis

Moment of Inertia about an Axis

Equation

$$I=\sum_i m_i r_{\perp i}^2$$

Literal reading

I equals the sum over i of m subscript i times r subscript perpendicular i squared.

Conceptual reading

Mass farther from a rotation axis contributes more strongly to rotational inertia.

Symbols

\(I\) is the scalar moment of inertia about the chosen axis. \(i\) labels mass elements, \(m_i\) is their mass and \(r_{\perp i}\) their perpendicular distance to the axis. The sum adds all elements; the exponent squares distance. The perpendicular symbol labels the distance, not a product.

Values, units and domains

Masses are in kg, distances in m and I in kg·m². Distances are nonnegative. I depends on the mass distribution and axis.

Meaning

The distance is measured from the axis, not from an arbitrary point along it. Moving mass outward raises I quadratically with distance.

Assumptions and limits

Discrete point-mass description; for a continuous body the sum is replaced by a mass integral. A scalar I describes a specified axis, not arbitrary three-dimensional rotation.

Example

Two 1 kg masses each 0.5 m from the axis have I = 1 × 0.25 + 1 × 0.25 = 0.5 kg·m².

Source

MIT OpenCourseWare, 8.01SC Classical Mechanics (2016).
