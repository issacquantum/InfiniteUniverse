Natural Angular Frequency of a Spring Oscillator

Natural Angular Frequency of a Spring Oscillator

Equation

$$\omega_0=\sqrt{k/m}$$

Literal reading

Omega subscript zero equals the square root of k divided by m.

Conceptual reading

Spring stiffness relative to inertia sets the undamped oscillation rate.

Symbols

\(\omega_0\) is natural angular frequency, \(k\) positive spring stiffness and \(m\) positive mass. Subscript 0 labels the undamped value; the root covers the ratio k/m.

Values, units and domains

k is in N/m, m in kg and ω₀ in rad/s; radians are dimensionless in SI. The ratio k/m has units s⁻². This is angular frequency, not cycles per second.

Meaning

A stiffer spring oscillates faster with the same mass; a larger mass oscillates more slowly with the same stiffness.

Assumptions and limits

Linear oscillator without damping or drive. Damping changes the oscillation frequency; a force’s imposed frequency is a separate parameter.

Example

For k = 4 N/m and m = 1 kg, ω₀ = 2 rad/s. The period is 2π/ω₀ = π s, about 3.14 s.

Source

MIT OpenCourseWare, 8.01SC Classical Mechanics (2016).
