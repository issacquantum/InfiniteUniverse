Page-Wootters Stationary State

Page-Wootters Stationary State

Equation

$$H_{\mathrm{tot}}|\Psi\rangle = 0,\qquad |\psi(t)\rangle \propto \langle t|\Psi\rangle$$

Literal Reading

the total Hamiltonian acting on Psi equals zero, and the subsystem state at t is proportional to the projection of Psi onto the clock state t.

Common Reading

A relational time construction: the whole can be stationary while subsystems show evolution through correlations.

Symbols

\(H_{\mathrm{tot}}\): total Hamiltonian.

\(|\Psi\rangle\): global state.

\(|t\rangle\): idealized clock state.

\(|\psi(t)\rangle\): conditional subsystem state.

Meaning of the Equation

The construction shows how subsystem time can arise by conditioning a stationary total state on a clock reading. The full equation has no external time parameter, but correlations between the clock and the rest of the system can reproduce effective evolution.

Scope and Limits

This is an idealized relational-clock construction; realistic clocks, interactions, and constraints make the general problem harder.

Conditions and units

The zero on the right fixes a total-energy constraint, not the absence of subsystem energies. In the ideal noninteracting construction, \(H_{\rm tot}=H_C\otimes I+I\otimes H_S\), with clock and system Hamiltonians \(H_C,H_S\) in energy units. A partial inner product with the clock bra leaves a system ket, which is normalized conditionally when nonzero; the proportionality factor need not be constant for a realistic clock. Schrödinger evolution requires appropriate covariant clock states and these dynamical assumptions, not stationarity alone. Ideal continuous clock kets can be generalized, nonnormalizable states.
