Weight in a Gravitational Field

Weight in a Gravitational Field

Equation

$$\mathbf W=m\mathbf g$$

Literal reading

Vector W equals m times vector g.

Conceptual reading

Weight is the local gravitational force on a mass.

Symbols

\(\mathbf W\) is the weight vector, \(m\) the scalar mass and \(\mathbf g\) the local gravitational acceleration vector. Juxtaposition multiplies the vector by the mass; bold type denotes vectors.

Values, units and domains

Mass is in kg, gravitational acceleration in m/s² and weight in N. Local g depends on location; 9.81 m/s² is an illustrative near-Earth value, not a universal constant.

Meaning

The force follows the direction of the gravitational field. Weight is distinct from mass and from the support force indicated by a scale.

Assumptions and limits

Newtonian treatment of a test body in the local field. A uniform g is an approximation over a sufficiently small region.

Example

For m = 2 kg and downward g = 9.81 m/s², the weight is 19.62 N downward.

Source

MIT OpenCourseWare, 8.01SC Classical Mechanics (2016).
