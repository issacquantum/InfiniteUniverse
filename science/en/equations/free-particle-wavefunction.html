Free Particle Wavefunction

Free Particle Wavefunction

Equation

$$ \psi(x,t)=Ae^{i(kx-\omega t)} $$

Literal Reading

Psi of x and t equals A times e raised to i times the entire quantity k x minus omega t.

Common Reading

A time-dependent plane-wave solution for a free particle.

Symbols

\(\psi(x,t)\): the particle's wavefunction at position \(x\) and time \(t\).

\(A\): the amplitude of the wave.

\(k\): wave number, related to momentum.

\(\omega\): angular frequency, related to energy.

Meaning of the Equation

This is an ideal plane-wave momentum eigenstate and is not normalizable over all space unless treated with box normalization or wave packets.

Form used in the article

$$\psi(\mathbf{r}) = Ae^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}}$$

Psi of vector r equals A times e raised to i times the dot product of vector k and vector r.

This is the spatial part of the three-dimensional plane wave. \(\mathbf r\) is position (m), \(\mathbf k\) is wave vector (m⁻¹), \(i\) is the imaginary unit, and \(A\) is an amplitude fixed by normalization convention. For box normalization, \(A\) and \(\psi\) have units \(\mathrm{m}^{-3/2}\). Including time multiplies it by \(e^{-i\omega t}\), with angular frequency \(\omega\) in s⁻¹ and \(t\) in seconds. For a nonrelativistic free particle of mass \(m\), \(\hbar\omega=\hbar^2|\mathbf k|^2/(2m)\). A localized particle requires a superposition of plane waves.

Conditions and units

For the one-dimensional formula, \(x\) is in m, \(t\) in s, \(k\) in m⁻¹, and \(\omega\) in s⁻¹. With box length \(L\), a normalized plane wave has \(|A|=L^{-1/2}\), in m⁻¹ᐟ². Its momentum is \(\hbar k\), its kinetic energy \(\hbar\omega=\hbar^2k^2/(2m)\), and \(m\) is the particle mass. A wave packet is a superposition, not a normalization of a single infinite plane wave.

Worked example or interpretation

In a periodic one-dimensional box of length L, choose k=2π/L and A=1/√L. The probability density is the constant 1/L even though the phase changes in space and time. Here e is the exponential base, i²=−1, and ħ≈1.054571817 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s relates wave number to momentum.
