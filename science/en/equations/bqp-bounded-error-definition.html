BQP Bounded-Error Definition

BQP Bounded-Error Definition

Equation

$$ x\in L \Rightarrow \Pr[C_x\ \mathrm{accepts}]\ge \frac{2}{3}, \qquad x\notin L \Rightarrow \Pr[C_x\ \mathrm{accepts}]\le \frac{1}{3} $$

Literal Reading

If x belongs to L, circuit C subscript x accepts with probability at least two thirds. If x does not belong to L, that probability is at most one third.

Common Reading

A BQP algorithm is allowed to be wrong sometimes, but only with a controlled probability gap.

Symbols

\(L\): the decision problem or language being solved.

\(x\): the input string.

\(C_x\): the quantum circuit generated for input \(x\) or its input length.

\(\Pr\): probability after quantum measurement.

Meaning of the Equation

The constants \(2/3\) and \(1/3\) can be amplified; the exact constants are conventional.

Conditions and units

The circuits form a polynomial-time uniformly generated family with polynomially many gates in input length. The gap applies to every input, not just an average. Probabilities and thresholds are dimensionless; independent repetition and majority vote reduce the error. The inequalities without the uniformity and resource bounds do not define BQP.

Worked example or interpretation

On a yes instance, acceptance probability 0.9 satisfies the required lower bound. On a no instance, acceptance probability 0.2 satisfies the upper bound. A machine that accepts every input with probability 0.9 fails the no-instance condition and therefore does not decide the language with these guarantees.

Notation details

The membership signs distinguish yes and no instances. Each implication specifies a separate required case of the same decision criterion; the thresholds include equality. Acceptance is the designated measured output of the circuit.

Source

John Watrous, Quantum Computational Complexity.
