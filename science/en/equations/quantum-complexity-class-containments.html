Quantum Complexity Class Containments

Quantum Complexity Class Containments

Equation

$$ P \subseteq BPP \subseteq BQP \subseteq PP \subseteq PSPACE $$

Literal Reading

P is contained in BPP, which is contained in BQP, which is contained in PP, which is contained in PSPACE.

Common Reading

Efficient quantum computation fits inside a larger hierarchy of known complexity classes.

Symbols

\(P\): deterministic polynomial time.

\(BPP\): randomized polynomial time with bounded error.

\(BQP\): quantum polynomial time with bounded error.

\(PP\): probabilistic polynomial time with majority acceptance.

\(PSPACE\): problems solvable with polynomial memory.

Meaning of the Equation

These are containments, not known strict separations.

Conditions and units

The classes concern decision problems with uniform computational models and asymptotic resource bounds in input length. In BPP and BQP the error is bounded away from one half and can be amplified; in PP the acceptance majority can be arbitrarily small. Containment does not imply that every problem in the larger class has an efficient quantum algorithm.

Worked example or interpretation

A deterministic polynomial-time procedure is also a bounded-error randomized procedure that simply never uses randomness. This explains the first containment without asserting that it is strict. The later containments need their respective simulation theorems, not the assumption that all quantum problems are easy.

Notation details

Each subset-or-equal sign states inclusion of sets of decision problems. These are mathematical classes, not numerical quantities with SI units. Equality between adjacent classes is not excluded by the displayed chain.

Source

John Watrous, Quantum Computational Complexity.
