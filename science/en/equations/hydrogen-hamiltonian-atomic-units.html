Hydrogen Hamiltonian in Atomic Units

Hydrogen Hamiltonian in Atomic Units

Equation

$$ \left(-\frac{1}{2}\nabla^2 - \frac{1}{r}\right)\psi = E\psi $$

Literal Reading

Minus one half times the Laplacian of psi, minus psi divided by r, equals E times psi.

Common Reading

In atomic units, the hydrogen Hamiltonian is the electron kinetic-energy operator plus the attractive Coulomb potential.

Symbols

\(-\nabla^2/2\): the kinetic-energy operator in atomic units.

\(-1/r\): the Coulomb attraction in atomic units.

\(\psi\): the hydrogen wavefunction.

\(E\): the corresponding energy eigenvalue.

atomic units: a system where several physical constants are set to one to simplify the equation.

Meaning of the Equation

The Hamiltonian balances electron kinetic energy against the attractive Coulomb potential of the proton. Solving it gives the allowed bound states of hydrogen.

Conditions and units

Hartree atomic units set \(\hbar=m_e=e=4\pi\epsilon_0=1\). Here \(r\) is electron-proton separation in Bohr radii (approximately \(5.292\times10^{-11}\) m), \(\nabla^2\) differentiates with respect to those dimensionless spatial coordinates, and \(E\) is in hartrees (approximately 27.2114 eV). The formula assumes a fixed point proton, no spin or relativistic corrections, and a normalized spatial wavefunction. Finite nuclear mass replaces the electron mass by the reduced mass in the dimensional kinetic term.

Worked example or interpretation

The normalized ground-state spatial function is exp(−r)/√π in these coordinates. Applying the displayed operator gives −1/2 times that function, so E=−1/2 hartree, approximately −13.6057 eV, in the fixed-proton approximation.

Source

MIT 8.04, Quantum Physics I, lecture notes (publication page).
