Friedmann Acceleration Equation

Friedmann Acceleration Equation

Equation

$$\frac{\ddot a}{a}=-\frac{4\pi G}{3}\left(\rho+\frac{3p}{c^2}\right)+\frac{\Lambda c^2}{3}$$

Literal Reading

double dot a over a equals minus 4 pi G over 3 times rho plus 3p over c squared, plus Lambda c squared over 3.

Common Reading

The equation determines the sign and magnitude of the scale factor’s acceleration, not the sign of its velocity.

Symbols

\(\ddot{a}\): second time derivative of the scale factor.

\(a\): scale factor.

\(\rho\): mass density / energy density divided by \(c^2\).

\(p\): pressure.

\(G\): gravitational constant.

\(c\): speed of light.

\(\Lambda\): cosmological constant.

Meaning of the Equation

For \(a>0\), the sign of \(\ddot a/a\) is the sign of \(\ddot a\). A negative value slows expansion when \(\dot a>0\), but increases the speed of contraction when \(\dot a<0\). A positive value speeds expansion or slows contraction. At a turning point the full solution is needed. The density-pressure combination and the cosmological constant jointly determine this sign.

Units and convention

Here \(\rho\) is total fluid energy density divided by \(c^2\), in kg/m³; it excludes the separately written cosmological constant. \(p\) is pressure in Pa. Cosmic time \(t\) is in seconds; overdots denote derivatives with respect to it. Choose \(a\) in meters and dimensionless \(k=0,\pm1\); then \(\dot a\) is in m/s and \(\ddot a\) in m/s². \(G\) has units m³ kg⁻¹ s⁻², \(c\) m/s, and \(\Lambda\) m⁻². Every term in the equation has units s⁻². These cosmological quantities depend on the model and epoch.

Scope and sources

Assumes a homogeneous, isotropic FLRW spacetime in general relativity. It is not a local description of individual galaxies. David Tong, Cosmology, The Expanding Universe.

Natural-unit form

The cosmic-fate article uses \(c=1\). In that convention, density and pressure share energy-density units:

$$\frac{\ddot{a}}{a} = - \frac{4\pi G}{3}(\rho + 3p) + \frac{\Lambda}{3}$$

Worked example or interpretation

For positive-density pressureless matter and Λ=0, the right side is negative. An expanding solution therefore decelerates even though H can remain positive. For pure positive Λ with no fluid density or pressure, the right side is positive. These are different model contents, not opposite readings of the same sign.

Source

David Tong, Cosmology, The Expanding Universe.

Physical constants

G≈6.67430 × 10⁻¹¹ m³ kg⁻¹ s⁻²; c=299792458 m/s exactly. NIST, CODATA 2022.
