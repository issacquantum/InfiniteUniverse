Circular Orbit Speed

Circular Orbit Speed

Equation

$$v_c=\sqrt{\frac{GM}{r}}$$

Literal reading

V subscript c equals the square root of G times M divided by r. The root covers the entire fraction.

Conceptual reading

The speed of a circular test-body orbit is fixed by central mass and orbital radius.

Symbols

\(v_c\) is circular speed, with c meaning circular, not light speed; \(G\) is the gravitational constant, \(M\) central mass and \(r\) the positive distance from its centre. The square root gives the positive speed.

Values, units and domains

G = 6.67430(15) × 10⁻¹¹ m³·kg⁻¹·s⁻²; parentheses give the standard uncertainty in the last digits. Mass is in kg, radius in m and speed in m/s. GM/r has units m²/s². The satellite’s mass is negligible in this expression.

Meaning

Gravity supplies the inward acceleration needed for circular motion. Larger radius gives smaller circular speed for the same central mass.

Assumptions and limits

Newtonian circular orbit around a point mass or spherical body, negligible orbiting mass and no other forces. For comparable masses the relative circular speed uses the sum of masses.

Example

With an illustrative GM = 4 × 10¹⁴ m³/s² and radius 10⁷ m, the circular speed is about 6325 m/s.

Source

Newton, Philosophiae Naturalis Principia Mathematica, Book I (HTML text); NIST, CODATA 2022.
