RK4 (Runge-Kutta fourth-order method) Global Error Order

RK4 (Fourth-Order Runge-Kutta) Global Error Order

Equation

$$ \|e_N\|=O(h^4) $$

Literal Reading

The norm of e subscript N is big O of h to the fourth power.

Common Reading

For smooth problems, RK4 gains much more accuracy from a smaller step than Euler does.

Symbols

\(\|e_N\|\): final accumulated error.

\(O(h^4)\): fourth-order global scaling.

\(h\): numerical time step.

Meaning of the Equation

The global error order applies for smooth problems with sufficiently regular solutions.

Conditions and units

\(e_N=y_N-y(T)\) is numerical minus exact state at a fixed final time \(T=Nh\), with positive step \(h\) and integer N. The bound concerns \(h\to0\) over that fixed interval under smoothness and stability assumptions. The hidden constant supplies the units needed to turn time to the fourth power into state units. Halving a sufficiently small step typically reduces the leading global error by about sixteen, not exactly for every problem. Stiffness, discontinuities, or roundoff can invalidate that observed scaling.

Worked example or interpretation

If a problem has a nonzero leading global error proportional to h⁴ in the asymptotic regime, replacing h by h/2 multiplies that leading term by 1/16. An illustrative leading error of 0.0016 state units would become 0.0001. This conditional comparison excludes dominance by roundoff or a lower-order boundary or forcing treatment.

Source

Driscoll and Braun, Fundamentals of Numerical Computation, initial-value problems (publication page).
