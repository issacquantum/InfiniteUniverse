Kerr Horizon Radius

Kerr Horizon Radius

Equation

$$r_+ = \frac{GM}{c^2} + \sqrt{\left(\frac{GM}{c^2}\right)^2 - a^2}$$

Literal Reading

r sub plus equals GM over c squared plus the square root of the quantity GM over c squared squared minus a squared.

Common Reading

The outer event horizon radius of a rotating uncharged black hole.

Symbols

\(r_+\): outer Kerr horizon radius.

\(M\): black hole mass.

\(a\): Kerr spin parameter, commonly \(a=J/(Mc)\).

\(G\): gravitational constant.

\(c\): speed of light.

Meaning of the Equation

Here \(a\) is the Kerr spin parameter. Common conventions use \(a=J/(Mc)\), or \(a=J/M\) in geometric units.

Scope and Limits

The square root requires \(a^2\le(GM/c^2)^2\), equivalently \(|a_*|\le1\) for \(a_*=cJ/(GM^2)\). If the spin term were too large, the classical horizon would disappear, signaling that the assumed black-hole solution is no longer valid.

Conditions and units

In the displayed SI formula, \(a=J/(Mc)\) is a length in meters, with angular momentum \(J\) in kg m²/s. It is not the dimensionless spin \(a_*\). \(r_+\) is the Boyer-Lindquist radial coordinate, not the areal radius or proper distance. This is the stationary, asymptotically flat, uncharged Kerr solution. The mass \(M\) is in kg; \(GM/c^2\) and the square root are lengths.

Equivalent notation

$$r_+=\frac{GM}{c^2}+\sqrt{\left(\frac{GM}{c^2}\right)^2-\left(\frac{J}{Mc}\right)^2}$$This substitutes \(a=J/(Mc)\) into the horizon radius; the square root includes the difference of the two squared lengths.

Worked example or interpretation

Writing rg=GM/c², a=0 gives r+=2rg. For a=0.6rg the square root is 0.8rg, so r+=1.8rg. The limiting extremal case |a|=rg gives r+=rg in these coordinates.

Source

Sean M. Carroll, Lecture Notes on General Relativity.

Physical constants

G≈6.67430 × 10⁻¹¹ m³ kg⁻¹ s⁻²; c=299792458 m/s exactly. NIST, CODATA 2022.
