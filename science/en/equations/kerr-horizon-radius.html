Kerr Horizon Radius

Kerr Horizon Radius

Equation

$$r_+ = \frac{GM}{c^2} + \sqrt{\left(\frac{GM}{c^2}\right)^2 - a^2}$$

Literal Reading

r sub plus equals GM over c squared plus the square root of the quantity GM over c squared squared minus a squared.

Common Reading

The outer event horizon radius of a rotating uncharged black hole.

Symbols

\(r_+\): outer Kerr horizon radius.

\(M\): black hole mass.

\(a\): Kerr spin parameter, commonly \(a=J/(Mc)\).

\(G\): gravitational constant.

\(c\): speed of light.

Meaning of the Equation

Here \(a\) is the Kerr spin parameter. Common conventions use \(a=J/(Mc)\), or \(a=J/M\) in geometric units.

Scope and Limits

The square root requires \(a^2\le(GM/c^2)^2\), equivalently \(|a_*|\le1\) for \(a_*=cJ/(GM^2)\). If the spin term were too large, the classical horizon would disappear, signaling that the assumed black-hole solution is no longer valid.
