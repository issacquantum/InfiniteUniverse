Atomic Recombination

Atomic Recombination

Equation

$$\text{nuclei} + \text{electrons} \rightarrow \text{atoms}$$

Literal Reading

Nuclei plus electrons form atoms.

Common Reading

A proton and electron form neutral hydrogen while emitting a photon.

Symbols

nuclei: atomic nuclei.

electrons: negatively charged leptons.

atoms: neutral atomic systems.

Meaning of the Equation

In cosmology this marks recombination and last scattering, despite the historical name.

Conditions and units

This is a schematic sequence, not a balanced reaction or rate law. Radiative capture transfers binding energy to photons; cosmological recombination involves cascades, two-photon transitions and radiative transfer. Recombination reduces the free-electron abundance, allowing photon decoupling over a finite interval; the two processes are related but not identical. No numerical time or reaction rate follows from this sequence.

Worked example or interpretation

Radiative capture of an electron by a proton can produce neutral hydrogen and a photon carrying away energy. The schematic line omits that photon and the intermediate levels, so it should not be used as a balanced reaction or to compute a capture rate.

Notation details

The plus sign lists incoming constituents and the arrow indicates formation, not numerical equality. The words denote species; the diagram supplies no stoichiometric coefficients, units or numerical reaction constants.

Source

Seager, Sasselov and Scott, A New Calculation of the Recombination Epoch.
