Quantum Harmonic Oscillator Energy Levels

Quantum Harmonic Oscillator Energy Levels

Equation

$$ E_n = \hbar\omega\left(n + \frac{1}{2}\right), \quad n = 0, 1, 2, \ldots $$

Literal Reading

E subscript n equals h-bar times omega times the quantity n plus one half; n equals zero, one, two, and so on.

Common Reading

The oscillator has equally spaced quantum energies with a nonzero zero-point ground state.

Symbols

\(E_n\): the allowed energy of level \(n\).

\(\hbar\): the reduced Planck constant.

\(\omega\): the angular frequency of the oscillator.

\(n\): the excitation level, starting at zero.

\(1/2\): the zero-point contribution that never disappears.

Meaning of the Equation

The zero-point term means the oscillator cannot have zero energy. The evenly spaced ladder underlies photons, phonons, and many small-oscillation models.

Units and scope

\(E_n\) is in joules, \(\omega\) in rad/s, \(\hbar\) in J s, and \(n\) is a dimensionless nonnegative integer. This is the spectrum of a one-dimensional ideal harmonic oscillator with the potential minimum chosen as zero energy. Energy differences are observable; adding a constant to the Hamiltonian shifts all levels. Anharmonic potentials do not generally have equal spacing. The frequency is system-dependent.

Sources

MIT, Quantum Physics I, lecture notes.

Worked example or interpretation

If the system has ħω=2 meV, its first three levels are 1, 3 and 5 meV. The spacing is 2 meV while the chosen ground-state energy is half that spacing. Here ħ≈1.054571817 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s and 1 eV=1.602176634 × 10⁻¹⁹ J.
