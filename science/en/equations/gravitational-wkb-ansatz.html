Gravitational WKB Ansatz

Gravitational WKB Ansatz

Equation

$$\Psi[h,\phi]\approx A[h]e^{iMS_0[h]/\hbar}\chi[h,\phi]$$

Literal reading

Capital psi of h and phi is approximately A of h times the exponential of i M S subscript zero of h divided by h-bar, times chi of h and phi.

Conceptual reading

The approximation separates a rapidly varying gravitational phase from a matter state on a nearly classical geometry.

Symbols

\(\Psi\) is the joint wave functional; \(h\) denotes spatial geometry, not Planck’s constant; \(\phi\) denotes matter fields. \(A[h]\) is the gravitational prefactor, \(S_0[h]\) the leading gravitational Hamilton-Jacobi function, \(M\) a gravitational expansion parameter and \(\chi[h,\phi]\) the matter-sector functional. Brackets indicate dependence on entire field configurations. \(i\) is the imaginary unit, e the natural exponential, \(\hbar\) reduced Planck constant and \(\approx\) an approximation.

Values, units and domains

The product MS₀ has action units J·s, so the exponent is dimensionless. The separate normalizations of M and S₀ depend on convention. Reduced Planck constant is exact h_P/(2π), with h_P = 6.62607015 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s. Functional amplitude units depend on the configuration-space measure; no universal numerical amplitudes are assigned.

Meaning

A gravitational Hamilton-Jacobi branch supplies an approximate classical background. This ansatz is part of a semiclassical expansion; it does not by itself prove an emergent time equation.

Assumptions and limits

Conditional approximation in canonical quantum gravity. Turning points, interference between branches, backreaction and nonadiabatic effects can invalidate the separation. It is not a complete theory of quantum gravity or a derivation of consciousness.

Example

On one approximately classical expanding-geometry branch, the phase can vary rapidly while the matter functional changes more slowly. Near a turning point the separation can fail; selecting this ansatz does not justify extending it through that point.

Source

Claus Kiefer, The semiclassical approximation to quantum gravity.
