Proton-Neutron Nucleosynthesis

Proton-Neutron Nucleosynthesis

Equation

$$\text{protons} + \text{neutrons} \rightarrow \text{atomic nuclei}$$

Literal Reading

Protons plus neutrons form atomic nuclei.

Common Reading

Protons and neutrons form bound nuclei through a network of nuclear reactions.

Symbols

protons: positively charged baryons.

neutrons: neutral baryons.

atomic nuclei: bound nuclear states such as deuterium or helium nuclei.

Meaning of the Equation

This is an early step in Big Bang nucleosynthesis.

Conditions and units

The display is a schematic sequence, not a balanced reaction or rate equation. One early Big Bang nucleosynthesis reaction is \(p+n\to{}^2\mathrm H+\gamma\): a proton and neutron form a deuterium nucleus while a photon carries energy and momentum. Further reactions produce helium and traces of other light nuclei under suitable temperature and density conditions. Nuclear formation is distinct from the later capture of electrons into neutral atoms.

Worked example or interpretation

The capture of one proton and one neutron into deuterium retains two baryons and one positive elementary charge; a photon removes the released energy. At high enough temperature, photons can dissociate deuterium again, so a possible reaction does not by itself guarantee accumulation of stable nuclei.

Source

Particle Data Group, Big-Bang Nucleosynthesis.
