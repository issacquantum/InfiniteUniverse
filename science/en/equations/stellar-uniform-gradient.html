Pressure Gradient in a Uniform-Density Sphere

Pressure Gradient in a Uniform-Density Sphere

Equation

$$\frac{dP}{dr}=-\frac{4\pi G}{3}\rho_0^2 r$$

Literal reading

The outward pressure gradient equals minus four pi G over three times density squared times radius.

Symbols and units

P: pressure in Pa; r: radius in m; rho₀: constant density in kg/m³; G: gravitational constant, 6.67430 × 10⁻¹¹ m³ kg⁻¹ s⁻²; pi: dimensionless. dP/dr has units Pa/m. R denotes the outer radius in m.

Physical meaning

The pressure decreases outward. Although enclosed mass grows with radius cubed, dividing by radius squared makes the required gradient proportional to radius in this model. The gradient tends smoothly to zero at the center.

Derivation

Substitute the integrated uniform-density mass into Newtonian hydrostatic balance. One factor of density comes from the enclosed mass and the other from the weight of the local material. Cancel two powers of radius. This is the explicit connection between the mass and pressure equations.

Assumptions and limits

Static, spherical, Newtonian balance with constant density and isotropic pressure. Rotation, magnetic stresses and relativistic corrections are excluded.

Worked example

At rho₀=1,000 kg/m³, R=1.0 × 10⁹ m and r=R/2, the gradient is about −1.398 × 10⁵ Pa/m. Across 1 km it produces a pressure drop of about 1.398 × 10⁸ Pa if the gradient is treated as locally constant.

Source

Princeton: Equations of Stellar Structure
