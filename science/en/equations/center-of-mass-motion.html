Motion of the Centre of Mass

Motion of the Centre of Mass

Equation

$$M_{\mathrm{tot}}\ddot{\mathbf R}_{\mathrm{CM}}=\mathbf F_{\mathrm{ext}}$$

Literal reading

Total mass times the second time derivative of the centre-of-mass position vector equals the net external force vector.

Conceptual reading

The centre of mass accelerates as if the total mass were concentrated there under the net external force.

Symbols

\(M_{\mathrm{tot}}\) is total mass; tot means total. \(\mathbf R_{\mathrm{CM}}\) is the mass-weighted mean position, with CM meaning centre of mass. Two dots indicate two time derivatives. \(\mathbf F_{\mathrm{ext}}\) is the vector sum of external forces; ext means external.

Values, units and domains

Total mass is in kg, position in m, its second time derivative in m/s² and force in N. The mass is constant and positive.

Meaning

Internal interactions can redistribute motion without changing the acceleration of the centre of mass if their net force cancels.

Assumptions and limits

Closed Newtonian system in an inertial frame with cancelling internal forces. Variable-mass open systems require momentum-flux terms.

Example

A 4 kg system under a net external force of 8 N along x has centre-of-mass acceleration 2 m/s² along x.

Source

MIT OpenCourseWare, 8.01SC Classical Mechanics (2016).
