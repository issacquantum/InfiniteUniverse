Centre of Mass

Centre of Mass

Equation

$$\mathbf R_{\mathrm{CM}}=\frac{\sum_i m_i\mathbf r_i}{M_{\mathrm{tot}}},\quad M_{\mathrm{tot}}=\sum_i m_i$$

Literal reading

Vector R subscript CM equals the sum over i of mass i times position vector i, divided by total mass. Total mass is the sum of the individual masses.

Conceptual reading

The centre of mass is the mass-weighted average position.

Symbols

\(\mathbf R_{\mathrm{CM}}\) is the centre-of-mass position; CM labels centre of mass. \(i\) indexes particles; \(m_i\) and \(\mathbf r_i\) are their masses and positions. \(M_{\mathrm{tot}}\) is total mass, defined by the accompanying sum. The summation adds all terms; the fraction divides each vector component by total mass.

Values, units and domains

Masses are in kg and positions in m. Total mass must be positive. Particle labels and the weighted fractions are dimensionless.

Meaning

The definition compresses a mass distribution to its mean position for translational dynamics. The point need not lie inside material.

Assumptions and limits

Newtonian mass-weighted definition with all positions in one coordinate frame. Continuous distributions require integrals. Relativistic energy-dependent centre definitions are different.

Example

For 1 kg at x = 0 m and 3 kg at x = 4 m, total mass is 4 kg and the centre is at x = 12/4 = 3 m.

Source

MIT OpenCourseWare, 8.01SC Classical Mechanics (2016).
