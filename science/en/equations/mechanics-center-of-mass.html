Center of Mass

Center of Mass

Equation

$$\mathbf R_{\mathrm{CM}}=\frac{\sum_i m_i\mathbf r_i}{M_{\mathrm{tot}}},\quad M_{\mathrm{tot}}=\sum_i m_i$$

Literal Reading

Vector R sub CM equals the sum over i of m sub i times vector r sub i divided by total mass. Total mass equals the sum of particle masses.

Common Reading

Mass-weighted position locates the center of mass.

Symbols

  • \(i,\sum_i\): particle index and sum over particles. \(m_i,M_{\mathrm{tot}}\): particle and total mass (kg). \(\mathbf r_i,\mathbf R_{\mathrm{CM}}\): particle and center-of-mass position (m), from the same origin. CM means center of mass; tot means total.

Meaning of the Equation

For a chosen collection of particles with constant masses, \(\mathbf R_{\mathrm{CM}}=\sum_i m_i\mathbf r_i/M_{\mathrm{tot}}\), with \(M_{\mathrm{tot}}=\sum_i m_i\). The index \(i\) labels each particle; all positions use the same origin and are measured in metres. A 1 kg mass at \(x=0\) and a 3 kg mass at \(x=4\) m have \(X_{\mathrm{CM}}=(1\cdot0+3\cdot4)/4=3\) m. The center lies nearer the heavier mass. Differentiating twice gives \(M_{\mathrm{tot}}\ddot{\mathbf R}_{\mathrm{CM}}=\mathbf F_{\mathrm{ext}}\) when internal forces cancel. A rocket or another system exchanging matter across its boundary needs the momentum carried by that matter; the constant-mass formula is not its complete balance.

Scope and Limits

Constant particle masses and a specified boundary; an open-system momentum balance also includes matter crossing it.
