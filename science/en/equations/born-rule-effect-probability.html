Born Probability for a Measurement Effect

Born Probability for a Measurement Effect

Equation

$$P(m)=\operatorname{Tr}(E_m\rho)$$

Literal reading

P of m equals the trace of E subscript m times rho.

Conceptual reading

A state and a measurement effect determine the probability of the corresponding outcome.

Symbols

\(P(m)\) is the probability of outcome \(m\); \(E_m\) its positive effect operator; \(\rho\) the input density operator. Juxtaposition is operator multiplication. \(\operatorname{Tr}\) takes the trace of the full product. For a pure state, the same probability is the inner product ⟨ψ|Eₘ|ψ⟩; if Eₘ = Mₘ†Mₘ, it is ⟨ψ|Mₘ†Mₘ|ψ⟩.

Values, units and domains

Probabilities are real, dimensionless and between zero and one. Rho is positive with trace one; the complete set of positive effects sums to identity. Matrix entries are dimensionless in normalized finite-dimensional bases.

Meaning

The formula covers projective and generalized measurements. It determines statistics, without choosing a physical or metaphysical interpretation of measurement.

Assumptions and limits

Normalized state and valid POVM on the same Hilbert space. Outcome statistics alone do not fix the post-measurement state.

Example

For a diagonal qubit state with probabilities 0.7 and 0.3, measuring the projector onto |0⟩ gives trace 0.7, hence a 70% probability.

Source

John Watrous, The Theory of Quantum Information (2018).

Equivalent pure-state form

$$P(m)=\langle\psi|M_m^\dagger M_m|\psi\rangle$$

Read: “P of m equals bra psi, M subscript m dagger, M subscript m, ket psi.” The rightmost measurement operator acts first on the normalized ket; dagger is the adjoint, and the bra is the ket’s adjoint. The scalar inner product is the squared norm of the transformed ket. The operator Mₘ has effect Eₘ = Mₘ†Mₘ. This is the same probability as the trace formula for a pure input, not another measurement law.
