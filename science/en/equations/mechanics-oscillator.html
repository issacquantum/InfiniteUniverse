Damped and Driven Oscillator

Damped and Driven Oscillator

Equation

$$m\ddot x+b\dot x+kx=F_0\cos(\Omega t),\qquad\omega_0=\sqrt{k/m}$$

Literal Reading

M times x double dot plus b times x dot plus k x equals F zero times cosine of capital omega t. Omega zero equals the square root of k divided by m.

Common Reading

Inertia, damping and restoring force balance a periodic drive.

Symbols

  • \(x,\dot x,\ddot x,t\): Displacement from equilibrium (m), velocity (m/s), acceleration (m/s²), time (s).
  • \(m,b,k\): Mass (kg), nonnegative viscous damping coefficient (kg/s), positive stiffness (N/m).
  • \(F_0,\Omega,\omega_0\): Drive-force amplitude (N), drive angular frequency and undamped natural angular frequency (rad/s).

Meaning of the Equation

Without damping or drive, \(x=A\cos(\omega_0t+\phi)\). Here A is amplitude (m), and phi is initial phase (rad), fixed by initial position and velocity. For m = 0.50 kg and k = 200 N/m, \(\omega_0=20\) rad/s and period is 2π/20, approximately 0.314 s. Damping removes mechanical energy. A drive replenishes it and can produce a large response near resonance; positive damping keeps the steady response finite.

Scope and Limits

Linear restoring force, linear viscous drag and sinusoidal forcing. A pendulum is approximately harmonic only for small angles. With damping, the displacement-resonance frequency need not equal omega zero.
