Nondeterministic Polynomial Time: NP

Nondeterministic Polynomial Time: NP

Equation

$$NP=\bigcup_{k\ge1}\mathrm{NTIME}(n^k)$$

Literal reading

NP equals the union, over k greater than or equal to one, of NTIME of n to the k.

Conceptual reading

NP contains decision problems with polynomially bounded certificates verifiable in deterministic polynomial time.

Symbols

\(NP\) is a class of decision problems; \(n\) is input length; \(k\) is a fixed positive integer exponent. \(\mathrm{NTIME}\) denotes a nondeterministic time class: acceptance requires at least one accepting computation branch. \(\bigcup\) unions these classes over exponents and \(\ge\) specifies the lower bound one.

Values, units and domains

Input length, certificate length and computation steps are dimensionless counts. Polynomial exponents are fixed for each machine. Nondeterminism is an abstract computation rule, not a probability distribution.

Meaning

A yes-instance has a short witness that a verifier can check efficiently; a no-instance has no accepted witness. Finding a witness need not be efficient merely because checking one is.

Assumptions and limits

Worst-case decision complexity in the standard nondeterministic model. NP does not mean “non-polynomial,” and membership alone does not establish NP-completeness or separation from P.

Example

For Boolean satisfiability, a proposed truth assignment is a polynomial-size certificate. Evaluating the formula checks it in polynomial time, so satisfiability belongs to NP.

Source

Stephen A. Cook, The Complexity of Theorem-Proving Procedures (1971).
