Work and Kinetic Energy

Work and Kinetic Energy

Equation

$$W_{\rm net}=\int_A^B\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r=\Delta K,\qquad K=\tfrac12mv^2$$

Literal Reading

W sub net equals the integral from A to B of vector F dot d vector r, and equals delta K. K equals one half m times v squared.

Common Reading

Net work changes the kinetic energy of a constant-mass particle.

Symbols

  • \(W_{\rm net},K,\Delta K\): Net work, kinetic energy and final minus initial kinetic energy, in J. W here is work, not weight.
  • \(A,B,\mathbf F,d\mathbf r\): Path endpoints, net force (N), infinitesimal displacement (m). The dot is a scalar product.
  • \(m,v\): Constant mass (kg) and speed (m/s), not a velocity component.

Meaning of the Equation

A constant 6 N force parallel to a 5 m displacement does 30 J of work. If it is the only force doing work on a 2 kg object initially at rest, its final speed is the square root of 30, approximately 5.48 m/s. For conservative forces, define potential U so their work is minus delta U; then K + U is constant if no nonconservative work is done.

Scope and Limits

Newtonian particle motion in an inertial frame. For deformable bodies, account separately for rotation, internal energy and the actual displacement at each force application point.
