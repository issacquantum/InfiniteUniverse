Matter Evolution in Semiclassical WKB Time

Matter Evolution in Semiclassical WKB Time

Equation

$$i\hbar\,\partial_{t_{\mathrm{WKB}}}\chi\approx\hat H_m\chi$$

Literal reading

I h-bar times the partial derivative of chi with respect to t subscript WKB is approximately H-hat subscript m acting on chi.

Conceptual reading

Along a suitable semiclassical gravitational branch, matter approximately obeys a Schrödinger equation with a background-defined time.

Symbols

\(i\) is the imaginary unit, \(\hbar\) reduced Planck constant and \(\chi\) the matter state functional. \(t_{\mathrm{WKB}}\) is time defined along the chosen gravitational Hamilton-Jacobi branch; WKB names the Wentzel-Kramers-Brillouin approximation. \(\partial\) differentiates chi in that time. \(\hat H_m\) is the matter Hamiltonian; the hat denotes an operator and m labels matter, not multiplication by mass. \(\approx\) records omitted corrections.

Values, units and domains

With time in s and Hamiltonian in J, both sides have energy times state-amplitude units. Reduced Planck constant equals h_P/(2π), with exact h_P = 6.62607015 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s. The matter state’s normalization depends on the adopted inner product.

Meaning

The time derivative comes from the nearly classical gravitational phase, rather than an external time parameter in the underlying constraint. Its validity is tied to that approximation and branch.

Assumptions and limits

Semiclassical expansion with suitable branch selection and adiabatic separation. Quantum-gravitational corrections, backreaction and turning points can alter or invalidate the effective equation. It is not a solution of the full problem of time.

Example

For a matter energy eigenstate of constant energy E along an approximately stationary background, the leading equation changes its phase at angular rate E/ħ while leaving its norm unchanged. This statement is limited to the regime in which that effective Hamiltonian and clock are valid.

Source

Claus Kiefer, The semiclassical approximation to quantum gravity.
