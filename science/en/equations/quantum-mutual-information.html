Quantum Mutual Information

Quantum Mutual Information

Equation

$$ I(A:B) = S(A) + S(B) - S(AB) $$

Literal Reading

I of A colon B equals S of A plus S of B minus S of AB.

Common Reading

Quantum mutual information measures total correlation between A and B, including classical correlation and quantum correlation.

Symbols

\(I(A:B)\): the quantum mutual information between subsystems A and B.

\(S(A)\): the von Neumann entropy of subsystem A.

\(S(B)\): the von Neumann entropy of subsystem B.

\(S(AB)\): the entropy of the joint system containing A and B together.

Meaning of the Equation

It is not only an entanglement measure; mixed states can have mutual information from classical correlations as well.

Conditions and units

The marginal density operators are partial traces of the same normalized joint state. \(S\) uses a common logarithm base: base two gives bits and the natural logarithm nats. In finite dimensions the result is nonnegative and vanishes exactly for a product density operator. Infinite-dimensional cases need care with potentially divergent entropies; the relative-entropy definition avoids an undefined subtraction of infinities.

Worked example or interpretation

A pure Bell pair has joint entropy zero and each qubit has entropy 1 bit, giving mutual information 2 bits. A classical equal mixture of 00 and 11 instead has joint entropy 1 bit, giving mutual information 1 bit. The latter is correlated but not entangled.

Source

John Watrous, The Theory of Quantum Information.
