Incompressible Vorticity Transport

Incompressible Vorticity Transport

Equation

$$\partial_t\boldsymbol\omega+(\mathbf u\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol\omega=(\boldsymbol\omega\cdot\nabla)\mathbf u+\nu\Delta\boldsymbol\omega+\nabla\times\mathbf f$$

Literal reading

Partial t of bold omega plus the operator bold u dot nabla acting on bold omega equals the operator bold omega dot nabla acting on bold u, plus nu times the Laplacian of bold omega, plus the curl of bold f. Parentheses specify the directional derivative operators.

Conceptual reading

Following the fluid, vorticity changes through stretching and tilting, viscous diffusion, and the curl of external forcing.

Symbols

\(t\) is time; \(\partial_t\) differentiates at fixed position. \(\mathbf u\) is velocity and \(\boldsymbol\omega=\nabla\times\mathbf u\) vorticity. \(\nabla\) differentiates in space, \(\cdot\) contracts vector components, and \(\Delta\) is the sum of second spatial derivatives acting componentwise. \(\nu\) is constant kinematic viscosity and \(\mathbf f\) external force per unit mass. Curl is denoted by \(\nabla\times\).

Values, units and domains

For position in m and time in s, velocity is in m/s, vorticity in s⁻¹, viscosity in m²/s and force per unit mass in m/s². Every term has units s⁻². Viscosity is nonnegative; its value depends on the fluid.

Meaning

Taking the curl of incompressible momentum eliminates the pressure gradient. Stretching can amplify vorticity in three dimensions and has no definite sign in the enstrophy balance. For a genuinely planar velocity field independent of the third coordinate, the stretching term vanishes.

Assumptions and limits

This pointwise equation assumes sufficient smoothness, incompressibility, constant density and constant viscosity in Cartesian Euclidean space. Compressible or variable-density flow generally introduces additional terms. The equation alone does not establish global regularity in three dimensions.

Example

For the shear flow \(\mathbf u=(ay,0,0)\) with constant shear rate a and zero forcing, vorticity is \(\boldsymbol\omega=(0,0,-a)\). It is constant, so the time derivative, advection, stretching and diffusion all vanish. Both sides equal zero, even when a and the vorticity are nonzero.

Source

David Tong, Lectures on Fluid Mechanics.
