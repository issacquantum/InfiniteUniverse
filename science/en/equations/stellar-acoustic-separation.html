Large Acoustic Frequency Separation

Large Acoustic Frequency Separation

Equation

$$\Delta\nu\simeq\left(2\int_0^R\frac{dr}{c_s(r)}\right)^{-1}$$

Literal reading

The large frequency separation is approximately the inverse of twice the center-to-surface acoustic travel time.

Symbols and units

Delta nu: separation between successive radial orders at the same low angular degree, Hz; r and R: m; cs(r): adiabatic sound speed, m/s; the integral: s. The approximate sign is essential.

Physical meaning

Mode spacing measures an acoustic path through the interior, linking surface signals to internal structure.

Derivation

At high radial order, adjacent standing-wave modes differ by approximately one phase cycle across the acoustic cavity. The resulting frequency spacing is the inverse round-trip travel time.

Assumptions and limits

Asymptotic acoustic modes, slowly rotating near-spherical star. Low-order modes, mixed modes, sharp structural features, and surface phase shifts require corrections; this is not an exact mean-density law.

Worked example

If the center-to-surface acoustic time is 4,000 s, the estimated separation is 1/8,000 Hz, or 125 microhertz.

Source

Christensen-Dalsgaard (2002)
