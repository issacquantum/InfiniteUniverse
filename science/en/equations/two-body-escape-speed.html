Two-Body Relative Escape Speed

Two-Body Relative Escape Speed

Equation

$$v_{\mathrm{esc}}=\sqrt{\frac{2G(M+m)}{r}}$$

Literal reading

V subscript esc equals the square root of two G times the sum of M and m, divided by r.

Conceptual reading

Escape requires enough relative kinetic energy to reach infinite separation with zero limiting relative speed.

Symbols

\(v_{\mathrm{esc}}\) is the threshold relative speed; esc means escape. \(G\) is the gravitational constant, \(M,m\) the masses and \(r\) their positive separation. The root covers the full fraction and selects a nonnegative speed; 2 is dimensionless.

Values, units and domains

G = 6.67430(15) × 10⁻¹¹ m³·kg⁻¹·s⁻²; parentheses give the standard uncertainty in the last digits. Masses are in kg, separation in m and speed in m/s. The expression inside the root has units m²/s².

Meaning

Both bodies can accelerate. When m is negligible compared with M, the total mass can be approximated by M. This is relative speed, not a speed in any arbitrary external frame.

Assumptions and limits

Isolated Newtonian two-body energy threshold, with potential zero at infinity and no collisions, atmosphere or further propulsion. Strong-field relativistic escape requires another treatment.

Example

With G(M+m) = 3.986 × 10¹⁴ m³/s² and separation 7 × 10⁶ m, escape speed is about 10.67 km/s. If the central radius is 6.37 × 10⁶ m, this separation corresponds to altitude 630 km, not 7000 km.

Source

Newton, Philosophiae Naturalis Principia Mathematica, Book I (HTML text); NIST, CODATA 2022.
