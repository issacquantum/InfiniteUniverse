Equilibrium Radiation Pressure

Equilibrium Radiation Pressure

Equation

$$P_{\rm rad}=\frac{aT^4}{3}$$

Literal reading

Radiation pressure equals one third of a times temperature to the fourth power.

Symbols and units

Prad: Pa; a: radiation constant, J m⁻³ K⁻⁴; T: radiation temperature, K.

Physical meaning

Photons carry momentum and contribute to the total pressure of an opaque stellar interior.

Derivation

An isotropic photon gas has pressure equal to one third of its energy density. The equilibrium blackbody energy density is a times T to the fourth power.

Assumptions and limits

Thermal, nearly isotropic radiation. A directional beam requires a radiation stress tensor; gas temperature can replace radiation temperature only when thermal coupling is adequate.

Worked example

Doubling temperature increases radiation pressure sixteenfold. Whether it dominates also depends on gas density and composition.

Source

Princeton: Equations of Stellar Structure
