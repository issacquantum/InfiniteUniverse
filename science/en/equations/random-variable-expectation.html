Expectation of a Random Variable

Expectation of a Random Variable

Equation

$$\mu=\mathbb E[X]=\int_\Omega X\,dP$$

Literal reading

Mu equals the expectation of X, which equals the integral over omega of X with respect to probability measure P.

Conceptual reading

The expectation is the probability-weighted mean of a random variable.

Symbols

\(X\) is a real-valued random variable on sample space \(\Omega\); \(P\) is its underlying probability measure. \(\mu\) names the mean; \(\mathbb E\) is the expectation operator. The integral sums X over the sample space according to the measure differential \(dP\), not a uniform coordinate interval. The equalities give equivalent forms of the same mean.

Values, units and domains

P and its total mass one are dimensionless. Mu has the same units as X, if X represents a dimensional measurement. A finite real mean requires absolute integrability of X; no universal mean is assigned.

Meaning

The measure automatically handles discrete, continuous and mixed distributions. An expectation need not be a value that can occur in one observation.

Assumptions and limits

X must be measurable; the displayed finite mean requires integrability. Heavy-tailed distributions can lack a finite expectation even if samples are finite.

Example

A fair six-sided die has mean (1+2+3+4+5+6)/6 = 3.5. This dimensionless mean is not itself a possible face value.

Source

Siva Balakrishnan, Carnegie Mellon University, Probability and Mathematical Statistics.
