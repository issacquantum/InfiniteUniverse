Finite Difference Heat Equation

Finite Difference Heat Equation

Equation

$$ u_i^{n+1}=u_i^n+\alpha\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x^2}(u_{i+1}^n-2u_i^n+u_{i-1}^n) $$

Literal Reading

u at spatial index i and time index n plus one equals u at i and n, plus alpha delta t divided by delta x squared times the quantity u at i plus one and n, minus twice u at i and n, plus u at i minus one and n. The superscript n is a time index, not a power.

Common Reading

This is a discrete update for heat diffusion, replacing continuous spatial curvature with neighboring differences.

Symbols

\(u_i^n\): field value at grid point \(i\) and time step \(n\).

\(\alpha\): diffusion coefficient.

\(\Delta t\): time step.

\(\Delta x\): grid spacing.

Meaning of the Equation

This is one-dimensional explicit forward-time, centered-space (FTCS) discretization of \(u_t=\alpha u_{xx}\), with constant positive diffusivity and a uniform grid. The standard stability requirement is \(0\le\alpha\Delta t/\Delta x^2\le1/2\), with compatible boundary conditions. In two dimensions the corresponding sum \(\alpha\Delta t(1/\Delta x^2+1/\Delta y^2)\) must be at most one half; an equal grid spacing gives one quarter per direction.

Conditions and units

\(\alpha\) is in m²/s, \(\Delta t\) in seconds and \(\Delta x\) in meters; their ratio is dimensionless. For temperature, \(u\) is in kelvins. Initial values and boundary data are required. For smooth solutions the scheme is first order in time and second order in space; stability alone does not establish accuracy at a chosen grid size.

Reference

Praveen Chandrashekar, Numerical solution of the heat equation.

Worked example or interpretation

With diffusion number alpha delta t/delta x²=0.25 and neighbouring temperatures 300 K, 320 K and 300 K, the middle value updates to 320+0.25×(300−640+300)=310 K. The weights are nonnegative in the stated stable range; boundary treatment remains part of the numerical problem.
