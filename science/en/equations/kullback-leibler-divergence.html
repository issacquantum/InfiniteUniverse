Kullback Leibler Divergence

Kullback Leibler Divergence

Equation

$$ D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P\|Q)=\sum_x P(x)\log_2\frac{P(x)}{Q(x)} $$

Literal Reading

D subscript K L of P relative to Q equals the sum over x of P of x times the base-two logarithm of the ratio P of x to Q of x.

Common Reading

It measures the information cost of treating data from distribution \(P\) as if it came from distribution \(Q\).

Symbols

\(P(x)\): the reference or true distribution.

\(Q(x)\): the comparison distribution or model.

\(\log_2\): base two logarithm, giving the result in bits.

Meaning of the Equation

KL divergence is not symmetric and is not a metric.

Conditions and units

Here \(x\) ranges over a common discrete outcome space; \(P,Q\) are normalized probability distributions. Terms with \(P(x)=0\) contribute zero by continuity. If \(P(x)>0\) but \(Q(x)=0\), the divergence is infinite. The result is nonnegative and measured in bits, not a physical energy or length.

Worked example or interpretation

For P=(1,0) and Q=(1/2,1/2), the only nonzero weighted term is log₂(2)=1 bit. Reversing the arguments gives infinity because Q assigns positive probability to an outcome with zero probability under P. This demonstrates the asymmetry.

Notation details

The double bar separates the reference and comparison distributions, not a norm. The summation covers the common outcome alphabet, and the entire probability ratio is inside the logarithm.

Source

Kullback and Leibler, On Information and Sufficiency.
