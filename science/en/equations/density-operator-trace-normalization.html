Trace Normalization of a Density Operator

Trace Normalization of a Density Operator

Equation

$$\operatorname{Tr}(\rho)=1$$

Literal reading

The trace of rho equals one.

Conceptual reading

Total probability in a normalized quantum state is one.

Symbols

\(\rho\) is a positive density operator. \(\operatorname{Tr}\) sums its diagonal entries in an orthonormal basis, equivalently its eigenvalues. Parentheses delimit the operator on which the trace acts; 1 is the normalization value.

Values, units and domains

The trace and density-matrix eigenvalues are dimensionless. Eigenvalues must also be nonnegative. In infinite dimensions rho must be trace class.

Meaning

Trace one fixes the overall scale of a positive state operator so its measurement probabilities sum to one. Trace normalization alone does not establish positivity.

Assumptions and limits

Necessary normalization condition, not a complete characterization without Hermiticity and positivity. A conditional unnormalized state can have a smaller trace representing an outcome probability.

Example

A diagonal matrix with entries 0.7 and 0.3 has trace one. Entries 1.2 and −0.2 also sum to one but do not form a valid density operator because of the negative eigenvalue.

Source

John Watrous, The Theory of Quantum Information (2018).
