Quark-Antiquark Pair Production

Quark-Antiquark Pair Production

Equation

$$\gamma + \gamma \rightarrow q + \bar{q}$$

Literal Reading

Two gamma photons produce q and q bar.

Common Reading

Two photons can produce a quark-antiquark pair if the available energy exceeds the threshold.

Symbols

\(\gamma\): photon.

\(q\): quark.

\(\bar{q}\): antiquark.

Meaning of the Equation

Free isolated quarks are not observed because of confinement; produced quarks hadronize into jets or hadrons.

Conditions and units

The bar denotes an antiquark, not complex conjugation of a numerical variable. This is a partonic reaction channel; the threshold depends on center-of-momentum energy, quark flavor, and the hadronic final state, not merely either photon’s laboratory energy. It supplies no cross section. For top quarks, weak decay occurs before ordinary hadronization, so the statement about forming hadrons has that exception.

Worked example or interpretation

Producing an up quark and its antiquark gives charges +2e/3 and −2e/3, so total charge remains zero as for the initial photons. Conservation of charge is necessary but not sufficient: collision energy and other quantum numbers must also permit the channel. The hadronic final state is not a pair of observable isolated up quarks.

Source

Particle Data Group, Quark Model.
