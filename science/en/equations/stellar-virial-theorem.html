Ideal-Gas Stellar Virial Relation

Ideal-Gas Stellar Virial Relation

Equation

$$2U+\Omega=0$$

Literal reading

Twice the internal thermal energy plus the gravitational potential energy is zero.

Symbols and units

U: total thermal internal energy, J; Omega: gravitational potential energy, J, negative for a bound star with zero at infinite separation. The coefficient 2 is dimensionless.

Physical meaning

Contraction through nearby equilibria raises internal thermal energy as gravitational energy becomes more negative. Total energy remains negative and can decrease while temperature rises.

Derivation

Multiply hydrostatic balance by the radial volume weight and integrate. With negligible boundary pressure this gives three times the pressure integral plus gravitational energy equal to zero. A monatomic ideal gas has internal energy equal to three halves of that pressure integral.

Assumptions and limits

Hydrostatic, isolated, nonrotating, nonmagnetic, monatomic ideal-gas sphere; negligible radiation energy and surface-pressure term. Do not apply this form unchanged to degenerate or radiation-dominated matter.

Worked example

A gravitational-energy change of minus 2 J implies a thermal-energy increase of 1 J along these equilibria; the remaining 1 J can escape. This is a scaled illustration, not a stellar energy estimate.

Source
