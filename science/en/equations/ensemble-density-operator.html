Density Operator of an Ensemble

Density Operator of an Ensemble

Equation

$$\rho=\sum_i p_i|\psi_i\rangle\langle\psi_i|$$

Literal reading

Rho equals the sum over i of p subscript i times ket psi subscript i times bra psi subscript i.

Conceptual reading

A probabilistic ensemble is represented by the weighted sum of its pure-state projectors.

Symbols

\(\rho\) is the density operator; \(i\) labels preparations; \(p_i\) is the probability of preparation i. \(|\psi_i\rangle\) is its normalized ket and \(\langle\psi_i|\) its adjoint. The sum adds operators; ket times bra is an outer product. Subscripts identify ensemble members.

Values, units and domains

Probabilities are dimensionless and nonnegative with sum one. States have unit norm. Matrix entries in a normalized finite-dimensional basis are dimensionless. The states need not be mutually orthogonal.

Meaning

The ensemble reproduces measurement statistics by averaging the outcomes of its preparations. Its decomposition into weighted pure states is generally not unique.

Assumptions and limits

Normalized quantum states and ordinary classical probabilities. Rho does not by itself identify which preparation procedure produced the ensemble.

Example

An equal mixture of |0⟩ and |1⟩ gives the diagonal matrix with entries 1/2 and 1/2. An equal mixture of the plus and minus superpositions gives the same density operator.

Source

John Watrous, The Theory of Quantum Information (2018).
