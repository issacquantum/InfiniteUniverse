Path Integral Kernel

Path Integral Kernel

Equation

$$K(b,a)=\int\mathcal{D}x\,e^{iS[x]/\hbar}$$

Literal Reading

K of b and a equals the integral with path measure script D x of e raised to i times the action S of x divided by h-bar.

Common Reading

A quantum amplitude comes from adding contributions from possible histories.

Symbols

\(K(b,a)\): transition amplitude or kernel.

\(\mathcal{D}x\): formal integration over paths.

\(S[x]\): action of a path.

\(\hbar\): reduced Planck constant.

Meaning of the Equation

The kernel is the quantum amplitude for going from one event or state to another. Instead of choosing one classical path first, the formalism adds contributions from possible paths, with each contribution weighted by the action of that path.

Conditions and units

In the nonrelativistic position-space kernel, \(a=(x_a,t_a)\) and \(b=(x_b,t_b)\) fix the endpoints of the paths. \(i^2=-1\); action \(S\) and \(\hbar\) have units J s, so the phase is dimensionless. The formal measure includes the time-slicing normalization; it is not a uniform probability distribution over paths. In d spatial dimensions the position kernel has units of inverse length to power d. Oscillatory path integrals need a specified limiting prescription, and interference does not mean that every path is a measured particle trajectory.

Worked example or interpretation

Two path contributions whose actions differ by πħ have opposite phase factors and can cancel if their prefactors have equal magnitude. This illustrates interference of amplitudes rather than addition of positive classical probabilities. Here e is the exponential base and ħ≈1.054571817 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s.

Source

David Tong, Applications of Quantum Mechanics, path integrals.
