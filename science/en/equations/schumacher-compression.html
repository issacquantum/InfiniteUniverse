Schumacher Compression

Schumacher Compression

Equation

$$ n\ \text{signals} \longrightarrow nS(\rho)\ \text{qubits} $$

Literal Reading

n signals are compressed into n times S of rho qubits.

Common Reading

N signals can be compressed into approximately n times S of rho qubits.

Symbols

\(n\): the number of emitted quantum signals.

\(\rho\): the source's average density matrix.

\(S(\rho)\): the von Neumann entropy of the source.

\(nS(\rho)\): the asymptotic number of qubits needed to preserve the source output with high fidelity.

Meaning of the Equation

This is an asymptotic many-copy source-coding result, not compression of one arbitrary unknown state.

Conditions and units

The source emits independent identical quantum signals with density operator \(\rho\), and \(S(\rho)=-\operatorname{Tr}(\rho\log_2\rho)\) is in bits. For any fixed positive overhead per signal, rates above S allow fidelity tending to one as n grows; the schematic nS omits finite-size overhead and integer rounding. It is not an exact lossless finite-n bit count, and correlated sources require different analysis.

Reference

John Watrous, The Theory of Quantum Information.

Worked example or interpretation

An independent qubit source with average-state eigenvalues 1/2 and 1/2 has entropy 1 bit, so its asymptotic storage rate is 1 qubit per signal. A source emitting one fixed known pure state has entropy zero; the receiver can prepare that state without a nonzero asymptotic quantum transmission rate.
