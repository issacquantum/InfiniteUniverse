Navier-Stokes Scaling

Navier-Stokes Scaling

Equation

$$\begin{aligned}\mathbf u_\lambda(x,t)&=\lambda\mathbf u(\lambda x,\lambda^2t),\\ p_\lambda(x,t)&=\lambda^2p(\lambda x,\lambda^2t),\\ \mathbf f_\lambda(x,t)&=\lambda^3\mathbf f(\lambda x,\lambda^2t)\end{aligned}$$

Literal Reading

u subscript lambda at x and t equals lambda times u at lambda x and lambda squared t. p subscript lambda at x and t equals lambda squared times p at lambda x and lambda squared t. f subscript lambda at x and t equals lambda cubed times f at lambda x and lambda squared t.

Common Reading

Spatial concentration, faster time, and increased velocity can be rescaled together without changing viscosity.

Symbols

  • \(\lambda>0\): Dimensionless scaling factor.
  • \(x,t\): Coordinates of the rescaled solution.
  • \(\mathbf u_\lambda,p_\lambda,\mathbf f_\lambda\): Rescaled velocity, pressure, and forcing.
  • \(\mathbf u,p,\mathbf f\): Original solution fields and force.

Meaning of the Equation

On \(\mathbb R^3\), every momentum term acquires the factor \(\lambda^3\), and zero divergence is preserved. Initial data and the time interval also rescale. A mixed norm gains \(\lambda^{1-2/p-3/q}\); zero exponent defines criticality. The squared energy gains \(\lambda^{-1}\), showing why energy bounds do not exclude fine-scale concentration. On a fixed unit torus arbitrary rescaling changes periodicity, so it is not a global fixed-domain symmetry.
