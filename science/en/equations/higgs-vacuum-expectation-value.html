Higgs Vacuum Expectation Value

Higgs Vacuum Expectation Value

Equation

$$\langle H \rangle \neq 0$$

Literal Reading

The expectation value of H is nonzero.

Common Reading

The Higgs field acquires a nonzero vacuum expectation value.

Symbols

\(\langle H \rangle\): vacuum expectation value of the Higgs field.

\(\neq 0\): not zero.

Meaning of the Equation

The nonzero background value changes how particles propagate through the field. Through their couplings, particles such as W and Z bosons acquire mass.

Conditions and units

Here \(H\) is the Higgs field, not a Hamiltonian. This is the conventional gauge-fixed description of the electroweak vacuum, not a gauge-invariant local order parameter. In the usual normalization \(\langle H\rangle=(0,v/\sqrt2)^T\), with \(v\approx246\) GeV in units \(\hbar=c=1\). The field has mass dimension one. Gauge-invariant particle masses are physical; this statement does not mean that every mass, including most of the proton’s mass, comes directly from a Higgs coupling.

Worked example or interpretation

In the stated gauge and doublet normalization, v≈246 GeV gives lower component v/√2≈174 GeV and upper component zero. The norm is therefore not 246 GeV in that convention. These are natural-unit field amplitudes, not a classical density of Higgs particles.

Source

Particle Data Group, Higgs Boson review.
