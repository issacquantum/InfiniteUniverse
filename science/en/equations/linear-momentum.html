Linear Momentum

Linear Momentum

Equation

$$\mathbf p=m\mathbf v$$

Literal reading

Vector p equals mass m times vector v.

Conceptual reading

Momentum measures translational motion weighted by mass.

Symbols

\(\mathbf p\) is momentum, \(m\) mass and \(\mathbf v\) velocity. Bold symbols denote vectors; juxtaposition multiplies a vector by a scalar.

Values, units and domains

Mass is in kg, velocity in m/s and momentum in kg·m/s. These quantities depend on the body and inertial frame.

Meaning

For fixed mass, changing velocity changes momentum in the same direction. Total momentum is the vector sum over the bodies in a system.

Assumptions and limits

Newtonian momentum for speeds small compared with light speed. Relativistic momentum is not generally mass times velocity in this form.

Example

A 2 kg body moving at 3 m/s along positive x has momentum 6 kg·m/s along positive x.

Source

MIT OpenCourseWare, 8.01SC Classical Mechanics (2016).
