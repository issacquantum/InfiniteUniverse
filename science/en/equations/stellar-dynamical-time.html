Stellar Dynamical Timescale

Stellar Dynamical Timescale

Equation

$$t_{\rm dyn}\sim\sqrt{\frac{R^3}{GM}}$$

Literal reading

The dynamical time is of order the square root of radius cubed divided by G times mass.

Symbols and units

tdyn: s; R: characteristic radius, m; M: total mass, kg; G: m³ kg⁻¹ s⁻². The tilde means an order-of-magnitude estimate.

Physical meaning

It measures a gravitational rearrangement scale, useful for testing whether a process is slow enough for hydrostatic evolution.

Derivation

Estimate acceleration by GM/R² and distance by R; a displacement scale of acceleration times time squared gives the stated estimate.

Assumptions and limits

Newtonian characteristic scale, not an exact collapse time or oscillation period. Density profile and pressure change coefficients; compact relativistic stars need further care.

Worked example

Using M=1.99×10³⁰ kg and R=6.96×10⁸ m gives about 1.59×10³ s, roughly 27 minutes.

Source

Princeton: Equations of Stellar Structure
