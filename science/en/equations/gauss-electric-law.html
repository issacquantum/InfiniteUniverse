Gauss’s Law for the Electric Field

Gauss’s Law for the Electric Field

Equation

$$\nabla\cdot\mathbf E=\frac{\rho}{\epsilon_0}$$

Literal reading

Nabla dot bold E equals rho divided by epsilon subscript zero. Nabla dot is the divergence of the entire electric field.

Conceptual reading

Electric charge is a local source or sink of electric flux.

Symbols

\(\mathbf E\) is the electric-field vector, \(\rho\) volume charge density, and \(\epsilon_0\) vacuum permittivity. \(\nabla\cdot\) takes the divergence, summing each Cartesian component’s derivative along its own direction. The subscript zero identifies the vacuum constant; the fraction divides the full charge density by it.

Values, units and domains

E is in V/m, rho in C/m³, and epsilon₀ ≈ 8.8541878188 × 10⁻¹² F/m (2022 CODATA; measured, not exact). Both sides have units V/m². Charge density may have either sign.

Meaning

Positive divergence corresponds to positive charge density. Zero divergence in a region does not mean that the electric field is zero there; charges outside the region may produce a field.

Assumptions and limits

This microscopic SI equation uses total charge density. In macroscopic matter, the displacement-field version instead uses free charge and incorporates polarization. Point charges require a distributional reading at their locations.

Example

For E=(a x,0,0), with constant a=1 V/m², the divergence is a. The corresponding charge density is epsilon₀ a ≈ 8.85419 × 10⁻¹² C/m³. This is a local illustrative field, not a finite isolated charge configuration.

Source

David Tong, Lectures on Electromagnetism; NIST, 2022 CODATA constants.
