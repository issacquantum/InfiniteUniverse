Morris Thorne Wormhole Metric

Morris Thorne Wormhole Metric

Equation

$$ ds^2=-e^{2\Phi(r)}c^2dt^2+\frac{dr^2}{1-b(r)/r}+r^2d\Omega^2 $$

Literal Reading

d s squared equals minus e raised to twice phi of r, times c squared d t squared, plus d r squared divided by the quantity one minus b of r over r, plus r squared d capital omega squared.

Common Reading

This is a static spherical wormhole geometry.

Symbols

\(ds^2\): spacetime interval.

\(\Phi(r)\): redshift function.

\(b(r)\): shape function.

\(r\): radial coordinate.

\(d\Omega^2\): angular part of a sphere.

Meaning of the Equation

\(\Phi(r)\) is the redshift function and \(b(r)\) is the shape function. Avoiding a horizon requires a finite redshift function \(\Phi(r)\).

Units and scope

\(t\) is time in seconds, \(c\) is light speed in m/s, \(r\) and \(b(r)\) are lengths in meters, and \(\Phi(r)\) is dimensionless. \(ds^2\) has units m². \(d\Omega^2=d\theta^2+\sin^2\theta\,d\varphi^2\) is the dimensionless angular line element; \(\theta\) and \(\varphi\) are angles. A throat at \(r_0\) requires \(b(r_0)=r_0\) and a flare-out condition, not merely finite \(\Phi\). The radial coordinate is singular there while proper radial distance can remain regular. This ansatz does not establish that traversable wormholes exist; in classical general relativity the throat requires null-energy-condition violation.

Sources

Morris and Thorne, Wormholes in spacetime and their use for interstellar travel (1988).

Worked example or interpretation

The illustrative choice Φ=0 and b(r)=r₀²/r gives b(r₀)=r₀ and b′(r₀)=−1. It satisfies the local throat and finite-redshift conditions, but substituting into Einstein’s equations requires an exotic stress-energy source. A geometric ansatz alone does not supply that source.

Source

Morris and Thorne (1988), Wormholes in spacetime and their use for interstellar travel.

Physical constants

c=299792458 m/s exactly. NIST, CODATA 2022.
