Light Cone Condition

Light Cone Condition

Equation

$$ds^2 = 0$$

Literal Reading

ds to the power of 2 equals 0.

Common Reading

The condition for lightlike separation between nearby events.

Symbols

\(ds^2\): spacetime interval.

\(0\): the boundary between timelike and spacelike separation.

Meaning of the Equation

The zero interval is the boundary of causal contact for light. Events inside, on, or outside the cone have different causal relationships, so the equation organizes what can influence what.

Conditions and units

For a nonzero tangent displacement, \(ds^2=g_{\mu\nu}dx^\mu dx^\nu\) has units of length squared and vanishes for a null direction. The metric determines the local light cone. In vacuum geometric optics, light rays follow null geodesics; the zero-norm condition alone does not impose the geodesic equation. Global causal boundaries in curved spacetime require tracing the relevant curves, not merely testing a finite coordinate difference.

Worked example or interpretation

In local inertial coordinates with no transverse displacement, dx=cdt gives −c²dt²+dx²=0. The displacement can be nonzero even though its spacetime norm is zero. Here c=299792458 m/s exactly.

Source

Sean M. Carroll, Lecture Notes on General Relativity.
