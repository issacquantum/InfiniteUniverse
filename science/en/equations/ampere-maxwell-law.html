Ampère-Maxwell Law

Ampère-Maxwell Law

Equation

$$\nabla\times\mathbf B=\mu_0\mathbf J+\mu_0\epsilon_0\frac{\partial\mathbf E}{\partial t}$$

Literal reading

Nabla cross bold B equals mu subscript zero times bold J, plus mu subscript zero times epsilon subscript zero times the partial derivative of bold E with respect to t.

Conceptual reading

Electric current and changing electric fields contribute to magnetic circulation.

Symbols

\(\mathbf B\) is magnetic flux density, \(\mathbf J\) total electric-current density, \(\mathbf E\) electric field and \(t\) time. \(\nabla\times\) takes spatial curl and \(\partial_t\) differentiates at fixed position. \(\mu_0\) and \(\epsilon_0\) are vacuum permeability and permittivity; their subscript zero labels vacuum constants.

Values, units and domains

B is in T, J in A/m², E in V/m and time in s. Mu₀ ≈ 1.25663706127 × 10⁻⁶ N/A² and epsilon₀ ≈ 8.8541878188 × 10⁻¹² F/m (2022 CODATA, neither exact). Every term has units T/m. Their product equals 1/c², with c=299792458 m/s exactly.

Meaning

The displacement-current term allows magnetic circulation even where no conduction current flows. Together with Gauss’s electric law, it makes local charge conservation consistent with time-dependent fields.

Assumptions and limits

This microscopic SI form uses total current density. Macroscopic material descriptions instead separate free current, polarization and magnetization. Omitting the time derivative requires an appropriate magnetostatic or controlled quasistatic approximation.

Example

Between ideal large charging-capacitor plates, take J=0 and a nearly uniform increasing electric field. The nonzero electric time derivative still contributes to curl B. Setting curl B to zero merely because no conduction current crosses the gap would omit Maxwell’s term.

Source

David Tong, Lectures on Electromagnetism; NIST, 2022 CODATA constants.
