Mode-Indexed Absolute Content

Mode-Indexed Absolute Content

Equation

$$C_{\mathrm{Abs-content}}=\{(x,m)\mid x\in\Omega,\ E_m(x)=1\}$$

Literal reading

C subscript absolute content equals the set of ordered pairs x comma m such that x belongs to capital omega and E subscript m of x equals one. Both conditions inside the braces must hold.

Conceptual reading

The definition records a form together with its mode of existence. Retaining the mode distinguishes mathematical existence, for example, from physical instantiation.

Symbols

\(C_{\mathrm{Abs-content}}\) names the content collection; its subscript is a label, not subtraction. \(x\) is a form in the total domain \(\Omega\); \(m\) is a mode label. \((x,m)\) is an ordered pair, not a product. \(E_m(x)\) is the predicate of existence in mode m, not energy. The value 1 means true. Braces delimit a collection, \(\mid\) means “such that,” \(\in\) means membership, and the comma joins required conditions. Equals signs define a collection or assert a predicate value.

Values, units and domains

All quantities are formal objects, labels or truth values; none has SI units. No measured constant or probability is assigned. A form may occur in several pairs with different modes.

Meaning

The collection preserves information lost by taking a union of domains alone. Identifying this content with Absolute Consciousness is a further metaphysical commitment, not a consequence of ordered-pair notation.

Assumptions and limits

This is my ontological definition, not an established equation of physics. It supplies no dynamics, probability measure or solution to the connection between physical organization and experience. Counterpossible content described by a mode is not thereby physically realized.

Example

A triangle description paired with a mathematical mode differs from the same description paired with a conceptual mode. Neither pair by itself asserts a physical triangle.

Source

Issac Tabares, My Theory.
