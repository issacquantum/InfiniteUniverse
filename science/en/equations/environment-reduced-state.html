Environment Reduced State

Environment Reduced State

Equation

$$ \rho_S=\operatorname{Tr}_E(\rho_{SE}) $$

Literal Reading

rho sub S equals the trace over E of rho sub S E.

Common Reading

The rule for describing what remains locally accessible when a system is entangled with an environment that is not being tracked in full detail.

Symbols

\(\rho_S\): the reduced density matrix of the system.

\(\rho_{SE}\): the joint density matrix of the system and its environment.

\(\operatorname{Tr}_E\): the partial trace over environmental degrees of freedom.

Meaning of the Equation

The partial trace removes the environment from the description while keeping its effect on the local system. That is why a subsystem can look mixed or decohered even when the full system is still described quantum mechanically.

Conditions and units

\(S,E\) label a chosen system-environment tensor-product split. Density operators and partial traces are dimensionless. The construction is exact and also applies without entanglement. It preserves expectations of system observables, but does not by itself specify a Markovian evolution or prove decoherence.

Worked example or interpretation

For an equal Bell superposition of system and environment, tracing out the environment leaves half the identity on the system qubit. Its two basis outcomes each have probability one half, even though the full two-part state is pure.

Source

John Watrous, The Theory of Quantum Information.
