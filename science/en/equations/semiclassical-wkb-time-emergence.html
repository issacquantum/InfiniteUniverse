Semiclassical WKB (Wentzel-Kramers-Brillouin) Time Emergence

Semiclassical WKB (Wentzel-Kramers-Brillouin) Time Emergence

Equation

$$ \Psi[h,\phi]\approx A[h]e^{iMS_0[h]/\hbar}\chi[h,\phi],\qquad i\hbar\,\partial_{t_{\mathrm{WKB}}}\chi\approx\hat H_m\chi $$

Literal Reading

Capital psi of h and phi is approximately A of h times e raised to i M S subscript zero of h divided by h-bar, times chi of h and phi. i h-bar times the partial derivative of chi with respect to t subscript WKB is approximately H-hat subscript m acting on chi.

Common Reading

In semiclassical quantum gravity, a nearly classical geometry can behave like a clock for matter fields.

Symbols

\(\Psi[h,\phi]\): wave functional of geometry \(h\) and matter fields \(\phi\).

\(S_0[h]\): leading gravitational Hamilton-Jacobi action.

\(\chi[h,\phi]\): matter-sector state on the approximate geometry.

\(t_{\mathrm{WKB}}\): emergent semiclassical time parameter.

\(\hat H_m\): matter Hamiltonian.

Meaning of the Equation

This approximation sketches one route by which ordinary time can reappear inside a timeless quantum-gravity equation. The gravitational part behaves almost classically, and matter fields evolve relative to that background clock, so the Schrödinger time is emergent rather than fundamental.

Conditions and units

\(A[h]\) is the gravitational WKB prefactor and \(M\) a large gravitational expansion parameter whose normalization depends on the chosen variables; the product \(MS_0\), not necessarily \(S_0\) alone, has action units J s. \(h\) denotes spatial geometry, not Planck’s constant. The hat marks an operator, i is the imaginary unit, and \(\hat H_m\) has energy units. WKB time follows a chosen approximately classical Hamilton-Jacobi branch; with time in seconds the second relation has matching energy units. This is a conditional semiclassical approximation, not a completed derivation of time in full quantum gravity. Turning points, branch interference, backreaction, and nonadiabatic effects can invalidate it.

Reference

C. Kiefer, The semiclassical approximation to quantum gravity.
