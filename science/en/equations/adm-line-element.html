ADM (Arnowitt-Deser-Misner) Line Element

ADM (Arnowitt-Deser-Misner) Line Element

Equation

$$ds^2 = -N^2c^2dt^2 + h_{ij}(dx^i + N^i dt)(dx^j + N^j dt)$$

Literal Reading

d s squared equals minus N squared c squared d t squared, plus h subscript i j times the product of d x superscript i plus N superscript i d t and d x superscript j plus N superscript j d t, summed over i and j.

Common Reading

Spacetime split into spatial slices evolving from one label time to another.

Symbols

\(N\): lapse, controlling separation between slices.

\(N^i\): shift, controlling how spatial coordinates slide between slices.

\(h_{ij}\): metric on each spatial slice.

Meaning of the Equation

ADM rewrites spacetime as a stack of spatial slices. The lapse says how much proper time separates slices, the shift says how coordinates slide between slices, and the spatial metric gives the geometry within each slice. This is a decomposition choice, not a preferred universal time.

Scope and Limits

The ADM split is a foliation-dependent decomposition; \(N\) and \(N^i\) are gauge variables, not physical fields by themselves.

Conditions and units

The signature is \((-+++)\). For spatial coordinates \(x^i\) in meters and coordinate time \(t\) in seconds, \(ds^2\) has units m², \(c\) is light speed in m/s, \(N\) and \(h_{ij}\) are dimensionless, and the shift \(N^i\) has units m/s. Indices \(i,j=1,2,3\) are summed. A spacelike foliation is assumed; the lapse relates coordinate time to proper time along the slice normal.

Worked example or interpretation

With lapse N=1, zero shift and spatial metric equal to the identity matrix, the expression reduces to the Minkowski line element in Cartesian coordinates. For a clock following the slice normal, dτ=Ndt; thus lapse N=2 would assign two seconds of normal proper time to one second of the chosen coordinate time. Here c=299792458 m/s exactly.

Source

Sean M. Carroll, Lecture Notes on General Relativity.
