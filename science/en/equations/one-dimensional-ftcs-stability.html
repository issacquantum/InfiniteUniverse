One-Dimensional FTCS Heat Stability

One-Dimensional FTCS Heat Stability

Equation

$$\lambda=\alpha\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x^2}\le\frac12$$

Literal reading

Lambda equals alpha times delta t divided by delta x squared, and is less than or equal to one half.

Conceptual reading

The explicit heat update is stable only when the time step is small enough relative to the squared spatial spacing.

Symbols

\(\lambda\) is the diffusion number, \(\alpha\) constant diffusivity, \(\Delta t\) time step and \(\Delta x\) uniform grid spacing. Delta labels finite increments; the square applies to the entire spacing. \(\le\) is an inclusive upper bound. FTCS means forward time, centred space.

Values, units and domains

Alpha is positive in m²/s, time step positive in s and spacing positive in m. Lambda and one half are dimensionless.

Meaning

The diffusion number controls the weights in the three-point update. Exceeding one half lets high-frequency grid errors grow instead of decay.

Assumptions and limits

Forward Euler time stepping with centred second differences on a uniform one-dimensional infinite or periodic grid. Boundary treatment must also be stable. This bound is not a rule for implicit solvers, and stability alone does not ensure accuracy.

Example

For alpha = 10⁻⁴ m²/s and spacing 0.01 m, lambda equals the numerical time step in seconds. A 0.4 s step gives lambda = 0.4 and satisfies the bound; 0.6 s does not.

Source

Praveen Chandrashekar, Numerical solution of the heat equation.
