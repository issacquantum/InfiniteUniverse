Variance of a Random Variable

Variance of a Random Variable

Equation

$$\operatorname{Var}(X)=\mathbb E[(X-\mu)^2]$$

Literal reading

The variance of X equals the expectation of the square of X minus mu. The square covers the whole deviation.

Conceptual reading

Variance measures mean squared deviation from the mean.

Symbols

\(X\) is a real random variable; \(\mu\) is its expectation; \(\mathbb E\) takes an average with its probability law. \(\operatorname{Var}\) names the variance function. Parentheses form the deviation, exponent 2 squares it, and brackets delimit the expectation’s argument.

Values, units and domains

The variance is nonnegative and has the square of X’s units. Finite variance requires a finite second moment. A dimensionless variable has dimensionless variance.

Meaning

Squaring prevents deviations of opposite sign from cancelling. The square root of variance, called standard deviation, returns to the original measurement units.

Assumptions and limits

This is population variance, not an unbiased finite-sample estimator. If the second moment diverges, a finite numerical variance is not justified.

Example

For equally likely values 1 m and 3 m, the mean is 2 m. Both squared deviations are 1 m², so variance is 1 m² and standard deviation 1 m.

Source

Siva Balakrishnan, Carnegie Mellon University, Probability and Mathematical Statistics.
