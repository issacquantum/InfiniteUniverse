Mode-Indexed Ontology

Mode-Indexed Ontology

Equation

$$\Omega=\bigcup_{m\in M}\Omega_m$$
$$C_{\mathrm{Abs-content}}=\{(x,m)\mid x\in\Omega,\ E_m(x)=1\}$$
$$R_{\mathrm{physical}}=\{x\in\Omega\mid E_{\mathrm{physical}}(x)=1,\ L(x)=1\}$$

Literal Reading

First: “Capital omega equals the union, over m belonging to capital M, of capital omega subscript m.” Second: “C subscript absolute content equals the set of ordered pairs x comma m such that x belongs to capital omega and E subscript m of x equals one.” Third: “R subscript physical equals the set of x belonging to capital omega such that E subscript physical of x equals one and L of x equals one.”

Common Reading

The first definition collects forms across modes of existence. The second records a form together with the mode under which it exists. The third selects physically instantiated forms compatible with physical law. These definitions express my ontological framework; they are not physical equations of motion.

Physical Interpretation and Status

A physicist can ask which forms are physically instantiated and compatible with a specified theory and evidence. The wider inclusion of mathematical, conceptual, possible, counterfactual and counterpossible forms is my metaphysical proposal. The set notation does not establish that such forms exist physically, derive consciousness, or determine whether the proposal is true. Identifying absolute content with Absolute Consciousness requires a further metaphysical commitment, not an inference from these definitions.

Symbols

  • \(\Omega\): the total domain of forms in this framework. It has no SI unit; it is not a solid angle or a density parameter.
  • \(M\): collection of modes; \(m\): a mode label, not mass or metres. \(\Omega_m\): the domain associated with that mode. A subscript selects a mode rather than multiplying by it.
  • \(\bigcup_{m\in M}\): union over the stated modes, collecting members of their domains. \(\in\) means “belongs to.” The union does not require the domains to be disjoint.
  • \(x\): a form under consideration; \((x,m)\): an ordered pair that retains both the form and its mode. Parentheses here form a pair, not a product.
  • \(C_{\mathrm{Abs-content}}\): the mode-indexed content collection. The subscript is a descriptive label, not an arithmetic subtraction.
  • \(E_m(x)\): predicate indicating existence under mode m; the value 1 means the condition holds. This E is not energy. \(E_{\mathrm{physical}}\) is the same predicate for physical instantiation.
  • \(L(x)\): predicate of compatibility with physical law; 1 means compatible. This L is neither a length nor a Lagrangian.
  • \(R_{\mathrm{physical}}\): the physical subset. Braces collect the elements satisfying the conditions. The vertical bar means “such that”; the comma between conditions means both must hold. Equals signs define collections or state that a predicate holds.

Values and Example

There are no dimensional constants or numerical predictions here. The symbol 1 is a truth indicator, not a measured quantity. A mathematical description of a triangle can be considered in a mathematical mode without asserting that a corresponding physical object exists. The second expression retains that mode label. For an object to belong to the third collection, both physical instantiation and compatibility with physical law must hold. Logical or mathematical description alone is insufficient.

Meaning of Each Definition

The union in the first expression answers which forms are included somewhere in the framework. It does not keep a separate copy for every mode. The ordered pairs in the second expression retain the mode, so the same form can occur paired with different modes. The third expression tests physical existence and law compatibility together. Compatibility alone admits unrealized possibilities, so it cannot replace the existence condition.

Counterpossible content belongs to the broader ontology by its mode, not by satisfying physical law. Describing such content does not assert that contradictory physical states are realized. The notation organizes these distinctions; it does not provide a dynamics or a probability measure over the collections.

Scope and Limits

These are definitions within my personal framework, not experimentally established results. They are schematic set notation, not a completed axiomatization of a universal set or a proof that the ontology is mathematically consistent. The connection between physical organization and first-person experience remains unresolved. No choice of these labels changes quantum mechanics or causes wavefunction collapse.

Source

Issac Tabares, My Theory, the discussion of mode-indexed Ultimate Totality and the final synthesis. These definitions are reproduced from that framework; no external paper is presented as establishing them.
