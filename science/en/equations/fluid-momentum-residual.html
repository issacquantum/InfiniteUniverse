Momentum Residual Under a Perturbation

Momentum Residual Under a Perturbation

Equation

$$\begin{aligned}\mathcal R(\mathbf u_B+\mathbf w,p_B+\pi)&=\mathcal R(\mathbf u_B,p_B)\\ &\quad+\partial_t\mathbf w+(\mathbf u_B\cdot\nabla)\mathbf w\\ &\quad+(\mathbf w\cdot\nabla)\mathbf u_B-\Delta\mathbf w\\ &\quad+\nabla\pi+\nabla\cdot(\mathbf w\otimes\mathbf w)\end{aligned}$$

Literal Reading

The residual R of u subscript B plus w and p subscript B plus pi equals the background residual, plus the time derivative of w, plus u subscript B dot gradient applied to w, plus w dot gradient applied to u subscript B, minus the Laplacian of w, plus the gradient of pi, plus the divergence of w tensor w.

Common Reading

A velocity correction changes momentum through linear interactions with the background and a quadratic momentum flux.

Symbols

  • \(\mathcal R(\mathbf u,p)=\partial_t\mathbf u+(\mathbf u\cdot\nabla)\mathbf u-\Delta\mathbf u+\nabla p\): Momentum residual at viscosity one; it equals the applied force for a solution.
  • \(\mathbf u_B,p_B\): Background velocity and pressure.
  • \(\mathbf w,\pi\): Divergence-free velocity correction and pressure correction.
  • \(\mathbf w\otimes\mathbf w\): Tensor with components w i times w j.
  • \(\nabla\cdot(\mathbf w\otimes\mathbf w)\): Vector with component i equal to the sum over j of the derivative with respect to coordinate j of the product w i times w j.

Meaning of the Equation

Expanding the nonlinear advection yields two background–correction terms and one correction–correction term. Zero divergence of the correction makes its quadratic advection equal to the divergence of its tensor product. This flux can have a nonzero average even when the correction has zero average. The identity itself does not guarantee cancellation of a background residual.

Scope and Limits

This exact identity assumes smooth fields and viscosity normalized to one. The background and corrected velocities must also satisfy incompressibility. Constructing a smooth forcing requires estimates beyond this algebraic expansion.
