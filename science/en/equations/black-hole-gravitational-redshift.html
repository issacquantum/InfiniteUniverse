Black Hole Gravitational Redshift

Black Hole Gravitational Redshift

Equation

$$\nu_\infty = \nu_{\text{local}}\sqrt{1 - \frac{2GM}{rc^2}}$$

Literal Reading

Nu subscript infinity equals local nu times the square root of one minus two G M divided by r times c squared.

Common Reading

Light losing frequency as it climbs out of the Schwarzschild gravitational field.

Symbols

\(\nu_\infty\): frequency measured far from the black hole.

\(\nu_{\text{local}}\): frequency measured near the emitting source.

\(r\): radius of emission.

\(M\): black hole mass.

Meaning of the Equation

Near a black hole, light emitted from deeper gravitational potential reaches far observers with lower frequency. The effect grows stronger closer to the horizon.

Scope and Limits

This is the static Schwarzschild redshift for an emitter held at fixed radius. It does not describe all Doppler shifts from orbiting plasma, infalling matter, or rotating Kerr geometry.

Conditions and units

Both frequencies are measured by static observers and have units Hz. The emitter is at areal radius \(r>2GM/c^2\) (m); the receiver is at infinity. \(M\) is in kg, \(G\) in m³ kg⁻¹ s⁻² and \(c\) in m/s. No static emitter can remain on the horizon itself. The square-root factor is dimensionless.

Worked example or interpretation

A static emitter at twice the Schwarzschild radius has the square-root factor 1/√2. Light emitted locally at 100 Hz is received at infinity at about 70.71 Hz. This hypothetical static setup excludes motion of the emitter.

Source

Sean M. Carroll, Lecture Notes on General Relativity.

Physical constants

G≈6.67430 × 10⁻¹¹ m³ kg⁻¹ s⁻²; c=299792458 m/s exactly. NIST, CODATA 2022.
