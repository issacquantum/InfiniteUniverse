Trapped Surface Condition

Trapped Surface Condition

Equation

$$\theta_{(\ell)} < 0,\qquad\theta_{(n)} < 0$$

Literal Reading

Theta subscript ell is less than zero and theta subscript n is less than zero.

Common Reading

Both future-directed null families orthogonal to the surface have locally decreasing cross-sectional area.

Symbols

\(\theta_{(\ell)}\): expansion of outgoing null geodesics.

\(< 0\): negative expansion, meaning the light front is shrinking.

Meaning of the Equation

A closed future-trapped surface is a compact spacelike two-surface without boundary for which both displayed inequalities hold everywhere. The future-directed null normals \(\ell\) and \(n\) label the outgoing and ingoing families. One negative expansion alone is insufficient; a vanishing outgoing expansion instead belongs to a marginal condition.

Scope and Limits

This is a geometric condition on null congruences. It is powerful because it does not depend on a simple escape-speed picture, but applying it in realistic collapse requires the full spacetime geometry.

Conditions and units

Each expansion is the fractional rate of area change along its null family. With an affine parameter measured in length units, it has units m⁻¹; its magnitude depends on normal normalization, while its sign is unchanged by positive rescaling. A trapped surface is not itself an event horizon. Relating it to global collapse or geodesic incompleteness requires additional hypotheses.

Worked example or interpretation

An ordinary round sphere in flat spacetime has one future null family expanding and the other contracting, so it is not trapped. Both future expansions must be negative everywhere on the closed surface. The ingoing label n is independent of the outgoing label ℓ, and each subscript identifies which family is differentiated.

Source

Sean M. Carroll, Lecture Notes on General Relativity.
