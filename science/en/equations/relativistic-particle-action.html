Relativistic Particle Action

Relativistic Particle Action

Equation

$$S=-mc\int\sqrt{-g_{\mu\nu}dx^\mu dx^\nu}=-mc^2\int d\tau$$

Literal Reading

S equals negative m c times the integral of the square root of negative g mu nu d x mu d x nu, which equals negative m c squared times the integral of d tau.

Common Reading

The action for a free massive relativistic particle.

Symbols

\(S\): action.

\(m\): particle mass.

\(g_{\mu\nu}\): spacetime metric.

\(dx^\mu\): infinitesimal displacement along the worldline.

\(d\tau\): proper time accumulated along a timelike path.

Meaning of the Equation

The action is proportional to the proper time accumulated along the particle worldline. Extremizing it gives the geodesic path for a free massive particle, so free motion is described by spacetime geometry rather than by a separate Newtonian time background.

Conditions and units

The signature is \((-+++)\), with repeated indices summed from 0 to 3. The path is timelike with fixed endpoints, \(m>0\) in kg, \(c\) in m/s, and \(\tau\) in s, giving action in J s. The first square root is a proper length. This test-particle action excludes nongravitational interactions and cannot describe a massless particle merely by setting m to zero; that requires another action formulation.

Worked example or interpretation

For a free particle of invariant mass 1 kg over 1 s of proper time, the action is −8.987551787368176 × 10¹⁶ J·s. Its negative sign follows the action convention and does not represent negative rest energy. Here c=299792458 m/s exactly.

Source

Sean M. Carroll, Lecture Notes on General Relativity.
