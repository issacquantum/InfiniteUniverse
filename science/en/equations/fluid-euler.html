Incompressible Euler Equations

Incompressible Euler Equations

Equation

$$\partial_t\mathbf u+(\mathbf u\cdot\nabla)\mathbf u=-\nabla p+\mathbf f,\qquad\nabla\cdot\mathbf u=0$$

Literal Reading

The partial time derivative of u plus u dot gradient applied to u equals minus the gradient of p plus f; the divergence of u equals zero.

Common Reading

An incompressible inviscid fluid accelerates under pressure gradients and external forcing, without viscous diffusion.

Symbols

  • \(\mathbf u,p\): Velocity and pressure divided by constant density.
  • \(\partial_t,\nabla\): Time derivative and spatial differential operator.
  • \((\mathbf u\cdot\nabla)\mathbf u\): Acceleration due to spatial transport.
  • \(\mathbf f\): External force per unit mass.
  • \(\nabla\cdot\mathbf u=0\): Incompressibility constraint.

Meaning of the Equation

Formally setting \(\nu=0\) in Navier–Stokes yields this system. It does not prove that viscous solutions converge to an Euler solution. Smooth unforced Euler conserves kinetic energy under suitable domain conditions, but general weak solutions need not. A forced Euler singularity theorem has different hypotheses from one with the full viscous Laplacian.
