Poynting Vector

Poynting Vector

Equation

$$\mathbf{S}=\frac{1}{\mu_0}\mathbf{E}\times\mathbf{B}$$

Literal Reading

Vector S equals one divided by mu subscript zero, multiplied by the cross product of E and B.

Common Reading

The electromagnetic energy-flow vector equals one over mu naught times E cross B.

Symbols

  • \(\mathbf{S}\): energy-flow vector.
  • \(\mathbf{E}\): electric field.
  • \(\mathbf{B}\): magnetic field.
  • \(\mu_0\): vacuum permeability.

Meaning of the Equation

This is the SI vacuum form: \(\mathbf S=(1/\mu_0)\mathbf E\times\mathbf B\). In material media, use the appropriate \(\mathbf E\times\mathbf H\) formulation.

Conditions and units

In SI, \(\mathbf S\) has units W/m², \(\mathbf E\) V/m, \(\mathbf B\) tesla, and \(\mu_0\) H/m. Its dot product with an oriented surface element gives the power crossing that surface. The displayed quantity is instantaneous; an oscillating field generally requires a time average for average power. The cross product fixes the orientation and does not assert that all field configurations are propagating plane waves.

Worked example or interpretation

For an instantaneous vacuum plane-wave electric field of magnitude 3 V/m, the magnetic magnitude is 3/c T and the energy flux is 9/(μ₀c)≈0.0239 W/m² along propagation. For sinusoidal fields specified by peak amplitudes, the cycle average is half that peak flux.

Source

David Tong, Electromagnetism.

Physical constants

μ₀≈1.25663706127 × 10⁻⁶ N/A²; c=299792458 m/s exactly. NIST, CODATA 2022.
