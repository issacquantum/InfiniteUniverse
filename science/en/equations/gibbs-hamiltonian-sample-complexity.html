Gibbs Hamiltonian Sample Complexity

Gibbs Hamiltonian Sample Complexity

Equation

$$ S=\mathcal{O}\!\left(\frac{\log N}{(\beta\varepsilon)^2}\right) $$

Literal Reading

S is big O of the logarithm of N divided by the square of beta times epsilon.

Common Reading

A high-temperature Hamiltonian-learning sample bound, with logarithmic system-size dependence and inverse-square dependence on inverse temperature and target error.

Symbols

\(S\): number of samples or copies required.

\(\mathcal{O}\): asymptotic scaling notation.

\(N\): system-size parameter.

\(\beta\): inverse temperature.

\(\varepsilon\): target accuracy.

Meaning of the Equation

The bound concerns learning the coefficients of a local Hamiltonian from copies of its Gibbs state under the theorem’s locality, bounded-interaction, high-temperature, and success-probability assumptions. Smaller \(\varepsilon\) or smaller inverse temperature \(\beta\) requires more samples in this regime. Smaller \(\beta\) means higher temperature. The bound must not be extrapolated to arbitrary low temperatures.
