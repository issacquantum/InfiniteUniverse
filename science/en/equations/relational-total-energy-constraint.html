Total-Energy Constraint in a Relational Clock Model

Total-Energy Constraint in a Relational Clock Model

Equation

$$H_{\mathrm{tot}}|\Psi\rangle=0$$

Literal reading

H subscript tot acting on ket capital psi equals the zero vector.

Conceptual reading

The total clock-plus-system state is stationary at the chosen zero of total energy.

Symbols

\(H_{\mathrm{tot}}\) is the total Hamiltonian operator; tot labels total. \(|\Psi\rangle\) is the joint state of clock and system. Juxtaposition applies the operator to the ket. Zero on the right is the zero vector, not the zero state as a physical preparation.

Values, units and domains

The Hamiltonian has energy units, conventionally J. The state is normalized in a finite-dimensional model, while ideal continuous-clock constructions may use generalized states. The zero constraint fixes a total-energy sector, not zero subsystem energies.

Meaning

A stationary joint state can contain correlations between clock readings and different system states. Those correlations, not external change of the total ket, underlie the conditional-time construction.

Assumptions and limits

Idealized Page-Wootters construction. Ordinary conditional Schrödinger evolution needs suitable covariant clock states and dynamical assumptions, often a noninteracting sum of clock and system Hamiltonians. Stationarity alone does not establish it or solve the general problem of time.

Example

A joint superposition can pair a clock energy ε with system energy −ε in each component, so each pair has zero total energy. This mathematical choice of energy offsets does not require each subsystem energy to vanish.

Source

Don N. Page and William K. Wootters, Evolution without evolution: Dynamics described by stationary observables (1983); Moreva et al., Time from quantum entanglement: an experimental illustration.
