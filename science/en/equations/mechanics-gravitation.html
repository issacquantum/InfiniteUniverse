Newtonian Gravitation and Circular Orbits

Newtonian Gravitation and Circular Orbits

Equation

$$\mathbf F=-\frac{GMm}{r^3}\mathbf r,\quad U=-\frac{GMm}{r},\qquad v_c=\sqrt{\frac{GM}{r}}$$

Literal Reading

Vector F equals minus G M m divided by r cubed, times vector r. U equals minus G M m divided by r. V sub c equals the square root of G M divided by r.

Common Reading

Gravity is attractive, its potential approaches zero at infinity, and a circular test-particle orbit has a radius-dependent speed.

Symbols

  • \(G\): Gravitational constant in m³/(kg s²).
  • \(M,m\): Central mass and orbiting mass (kg).
  • \(\mathbf r,r\): Displacement from M to m and its positive magnitude (m).
  • \(\mathbf F,U,v_c\): Force (N), potential energy (J), circular speed (m/s).

Meaning of the Equation

Equate inward GMm/r² with mv²/r to obtain circular speed. With GM = 3.986 × 10¹⁴ m³/s² and orbital radius 7.0 × 10⁶ m, speed is approximately 7.55 km/s and period 2πr/v approximately 97 minutes. Radius is measured from the central body’s center, not its surface. In an isolated inverse-square two-body system, bound noncollision orbits are ellipses.

Scope and Limits

Point masses or the external field of a spherical mass distribution; r greater than zero. The speed formula treats M as fixed, appropriate for m much smaller than M. With comparable masses use their common center of mass and GM + Gm for relative motion. No drag, other bodies or relativistic corrections.
