Partial Trace of a Bipartite State

Partial Trace of a Bipartite State

Equation

$$\rho_A=\operatorname{Tr}_B(\rho_{AB})$$

Literal reading

Rho subscript A equals the partial trace over B of rho subscript AB.

Conceptual reading

Discarding access to B leaves the state that predicts all measurements confined to A.

Symbols

\(\rho_{AB}\) is the joint density operator on the tensor product of subsystem spaces A and B. \(\rho_A\) is A’s reduced density operator. \(\operatorname{Tr}_B\) traces only the B indices in an orthonormal basis; the result is still an operator on A. Subscripts label systems, not multiplication.

Values, units and domains

Both operators are positive and trace one; their matrix entries in normalized finite-dimensional bases are dimensionless. Subsystem dimensions depend on the physical system.

Meaning

The partial trace does not describe a particular observed result on B. It removes the unobserved subsystem while retaining the probabilities of every local measurement on A.

Assumptions and limits

A specified tensor-product subsystem structure is required. Factorizing a Hilbert space arbitrarily does not by itself identify a physical observer or localized subsystem.

Example

Tracing one qubit out of the Bell state (|00⟩ + |11⟩)/√2 leaves the diagonal state (1/2, 1/2) on the other qubit. The joint state is pure but its local state is mixed.

Source

John Watrous, The Theory of Quantum Information (2018).
