Kelvin-Helmholtz Timescale

Kelvin-Helmholtz Timescale

Equation

$$t_{\rm KH}\sim\frac{GM^2}{RL}$$

Literal reading

The thermal contraction time is of order G times mass squared divided by radius times luminosity.

Symbols and units

tKH: s; G: m³ kg⁻¹ s⁻²; M: kg; R: m; L: luminosity in W. The numerator divided by R is an energy scale in J.

Physical meaning

This compares a binding-energy reservoir to an energy-loss rate.

Derivation

Estimate gravitational binding energy by GM²/R and divide by luminosity. The density-profile coefficient and the fraction available for radiation are omitted in this scale estimate.

Assumptions and limits

An approximate global timescale using characteristic values. Real contraction changes R and L; local thermal times differ, and nuclear energy can dominate the luminosity.

Worked example

Solar M=1.99×10³⁰ kg, R=6.96×10⁸ m, and L=3.83×10²⁶ W give about 31 million years.

Source

Princeton: Equations of Stellar Structure
