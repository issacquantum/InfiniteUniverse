Pendulum Torque and Small Angles

Equation

$$m\ell^2\ddot\theta=-mg\ell\sin\theta,\quad\ddot\theta+\frac{g}{\ell}\theta\approx0$$

Literal Reading

Mass times length squared times theta double dot equals minus mass times g times length times sine theta. At small angles, theta double dot plus g over length times theta is approximately zero.

Common Reading

Gravity supplies a restoring torque, approximately proportional to angle near equilibrium.

Symbols

  • \(m,\ell,g\): point mass (kg), rod length (m), uniform gravitational acceleration (m/s²). \(\theta,\ddot\theta\): angle from downward vertical (rad), its second time derivative (rad/s²). \(m\ell^2\): pivot inertia (kg m²). \(\sin\): sine, with angle in radians.

Meaning of the Equation

For an ideal pendulum, take a point mass \(m\), a massless rigid rod of length \(\ell\), a fixed frictionless pivot, and uniform gravity. With angle \(\theta\) from downward vertical, gravitational torque is \(-mg\ell\sin\theta\) and inertia about the pivot is \(m\ell^2\). Thus \(m\ell^2\ddot\theta=-mg\ell\sin\theta\). At \(|\theta|\ll1\), measured in radians, \(\sin\theta\approx\theta\) gives \(\ddot\theta+(g/\ell)\theta=0\) and \(\omega_0=\sqrt{g/\ell}\). Large-angle motion is nonlinear and amplitude-dependent; the ideal unforced single pendulum is not thereby chaotic.

Scope and Limits

Fixed frictionless pivot, massless rigid rod, point mass, no drag or drive. Only the second expression requires small angles.
