Impulse-Momentum Theorem

Impulse-Momentum Theorem

Equation

$$\Delta\mathbf p=\int_{t_1}^{t_2}\mathbf F_{\rm ext}\,dt$$

Literal reading

Delta vector p equals the integral from t subscript one to t subscript two of external force vector F with respect to time.

Conceptual reading

Accumulated external force gives the change in total momentum.

Symbols

\(\mathbf p\) is total momentum; \(\Delta\mathbf p\) means final minus initial momentum. \(\mathbf F_{\rm ext}\) is the net external force; ext labels external. \(t_1,t_2\) are initial and final times. The integral sums force over time and \(dt\) is the time differential.

Values, units and domains

Force has units N, time s and impulse N·s = kg·m/s. Momentum and force are vectors. The time interval and force history are system-dependent.

Meaning

A large short force and a smaller prolonged force can produce the same impulse. Internal forces cancel for a closed Newtonian system with reciprocal internal interactions.

Assumptions and limits

For a fixed material system in an inertial frame. Momentum carried across the boundary of an open system requires a flux term.

Example

A constant 5 N force along x acting for 0.2 s gives a momentum change of 1 kg·m/s along x.

Source

MIT OpenCourseWare, 8.01SC Classical Mechanics (2016).
