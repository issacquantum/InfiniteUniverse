Material Derivative

Material Derivative

Equation

$$\frac{D\mathbf u}{Dt}=\partial_t\mathbf u+(\mathbf u\cdot\nabla)\mathbf u$$

Literal Reading

The material derivative of u with respect to t equals the partial time derivative of u plus u dot gradient applied to u.

Common Reading

Change experienced by a moving fluid element combines local time variation with motion through spatial variation.

Symbols

  • \(D/Dt\): Derivative following a fluid trajectory.
  • \(\partial_t\): Time derivative at fixed spatial position.
  • \(\mathbf u\): Velocity of the fluid, also the transported field in this equation.
  • \(\mathbf u\cdot\nabla\): Directional differential operator \(\sum_j u_j\partial_{x_j}\).

Meaning of the Equation

This is the chain rule along \(\dot X=\mathbf u(X,t)\). Even a steady field with \(\partial_t\mathbf u=0\) can accelerate a particle: curved streamlines change the direction of its velocity. The material derivative is kinematics; momentum dynamics specify what that acceleration equals.
