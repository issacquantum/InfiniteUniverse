Tidal Acceleration

Tidal Acceleration

Equation

$$\Delta a \sim \frac{2GM}{r^3}\Delta r$$

Literal Reading

Delta a is approximately two G M over r cubed, multiplied by delta r.

Common Reading

An estimate of how differently gravity pulls on two nearby points.

Symbols

\(\Delta a\): difference in acceleration.

\(\Delta r\): separation between two points.

\(M\): mass causing the gravitational field.

\(r\): distance from the mass.

Meaning of the Equation

A body is stretched when gravity is noticeably stronger on one side than the other. The equation captures gravity's gradient, which matters near compact objects even when a small object feels weightless at its center.

Scope and Limits

This is an order-of-magnitude Newtonian tidal estimate. Precise relativistic tidal effects are described by curvature tensors and depend on the observer's frame.

Conditions and units

This is the leading radial differential acceleration for nearby freely falling points with \(|\Delta r|\ll r\), outside a spherical mass. With G in m³ kg⁻¹ s⁻², M in kg and both lengths in m, \(\Delta a\) is in m/s². Positive outward radial separation gives a stretching relative acceleration; transverse separations instead have a compressive coefficient \(-GM/r^3\). The factor two is specifically radial.

Worked example or interpretation

For illustrative GM=10¹⁴ m³/s², r=10⁷ m and radial separation 2 m, the estimate gives 4 × 10⁻⁷ m/s². Doubling r while keeping the separation fixed reduces the differential acceleration by eight.

Source

Sean M. Carroll, Lecture Notes on General Relativity.

Physical constants

G≈6.67430 × 10⁻¹¹ m³ kg⁻¹ s⁻². NIST, CODATA 2022.
