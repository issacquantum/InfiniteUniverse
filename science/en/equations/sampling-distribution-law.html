Sampling Distribution

Sampling Distribution

Equation

$$X_k\sim p(x)$$

Literal reading

X subscript k is distributed according to p of x. The tilde means distributed according to, not approximately equal.

Conceptual reading

The samples must come from the stated probability law.

Symbols

\(X_k\) is the random variable for draw k; \(k\) is a positive integer label. \(x\) is a possible value, \(p(x)\) the probability density, and \(\sim\) specifies a distribution rather than numerical equality.

Values, units and domains

X and x have the units of the sampled quantity. The density has reciprocal units to its integration measure and integrates to one. Sample labels and probabilities are dimensionless.

Meaning

This specifies each sample’s marginal distribution. It does not, by itself, state whether different samples are independent, nor how they are generated.

Assumptions and limits

Simple Monte Carlo additionally assumes independence across k. The notation does not imply uniform sampling. A density is one representation of a probability law; discrete laws can use probability masses instead.

Example

If p is uniform on the dimensionless interval [0,1], each draw has probability 0.25 of lying between 0 and 0.25. Reusing one random draw repeatedly preserves each marginal distribution but creates perfect dependence, which defeats the usual independent-sample error estimate.

Source

Art B. Owen, Monte Carlo theory, methods and examples, Chapter 2.
