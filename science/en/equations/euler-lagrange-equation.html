Euler-Lagrange Equation

Euler-Lagrange Equation

Equation

$$\frac{d}{dt}\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot q}-\frac{\partial L}{\partial q}=0$$

Literal Reading

The time derivative of the partial derivative of L with respect to q dot, minus the partial derivative of L with respect to q, equals zero.

Common Reading

Stationary action gives a differential equation for each independent coordinate.

Symbols

  • \(q,\dot q,t\): Coordinate, its time derivative and time (s). A dot means d/dt.
  • \(L\): Lagrangian, in J.
  • \(\partial L/\partial\dot q\): Partial derivative holding other arguments fixed; canonical momentum, in kg m/s when q is a length.
  • \(d/dt\): Total derivative along the evolving path; includes every time dependence.
  • \(\partial L/\partial q\): Coordinate derivative, in N when q is a length; in J when q is an angle in radians.

Meaning of the Equation

For \(L=\tfrac12m\dot q^2-\tfrac12kq^2\), with mass m (kg) and stiffness k (N/m), the partial derivatives are \(m\dot q\) and \(-kq\). Substitution gives \(m\ddot q+kq=0\), the spring equation. Here q is displacement (m). For several coordinates \(q_i\), there is one equation per index i. Eliminable constraints reduce the independent coordinates.

For the same unforced spring, Newton gives \(m\ddot x=-kx\). With \(L=\tfrac12m\dot x^2-\tfrac12kx^2\), the two derivatives in the Euler-Lagrange equation are \(d(m\dot x)/dt=m\ddot x\) and \(-kx\), returning \(m\ddot x+kx=0\). Momentum is \(p=m\dot x\). The Legendre transform exchanges the velocity variable for its momentum: substituting \(\dot x=p/m\) into \(p\dot x-L\) gives \(H=p^2/(2m)+kx^2/2\). Hamilton's equations give \(\dot x=p/m\) and \(\dot p=-kx\); differentiating the first reproduces the same Newton equation. This Hamiltonian is separable, meaning a sum of a momentum-only kinetic term and a position-only potential term.

Scope and Limits

The displayed homogeneous form excludes extra nonconservative generalized forces. Nonholonomic constraints require care; field equations use derivatives over space as well and are not literally this single-coordinate formula.
