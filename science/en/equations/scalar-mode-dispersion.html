Free Scalar-Mode Dispersion

Free Scalar-Mode Dispersion

Equation

$$\omega_{\mathbf k}=\sqrt{|\mathbf k|^2+m^2}$$

Literal reading

Omega subscript vector k equals the square root of the squared magnitude of vector k plus m squared. The root encloses both terms.

Conceptual reading

The free mode’s frequency combines its wave number with a mass scale.

Symbols

\(\omega_{\mathbf k}\) is the positive mode angular frequency, \(\mathbf k\) the wave vector, bars its Euclidean magnitude and \(m\) the nonnegative mass-like parameter. The vector subscript labels a mode. The square root selects the nonnegative branch.

Values, units and domains

This form uses c=1 and represents time as a length: omega, k and m are inverse lengths. The parameter m is physical mass times c divided by ħ, not a mass in kg inserted unchanged. In SI the angular frequency is c times the displayed square root. The model’s numerical coordinates are normalized.

Meaning

For zero mass the frequency is proportional to wave-number magnitude; nonzero mass produces a nonzero zero-wave-number frequency. This is a free-field dispersion relation, not a rule for arbitrary interacting media.

Assumptions and limits

The relation assumes a free relativistic scalar field in flat spacetime with nonnegative mass squared. The finite visualization uses a chosen subset of these modes and does not itself validate an interacting quantum theory.

Example

In consistent inverse-length units, a wave-number magnitude of 3 and mass parameter of 4 give omega=√(9+16)=5. The result is an inverse length; multiplying by c converts it to an angular frequency in s⁻¹.
