FLRW Cosmological Event Horizon

FLRW Cosmological Event Horizon

Equation

$$d_e(t)=a(t)\int_t^\infty\frac{c\,dt'}{a(t')}$$

Literal reading

D subscript e of t equals a of t times the integral from t to infinity of c divided by a of t prime, with respect to t prime.

Conceptual reading

A finite future light-travel integral limits which events can ever communicate with the observer.

Symbols

\(d_e\) is event-horizon proper distance; e labels event. \(a(t)\) is the scale factor, \(t\) cosmic time, \(t^{\prime}\) the dummy integration time and \(c\) light speed. \(dt^{\prime}\) is a differential, and infinity denotes an indefinitely extended future. The outside a converts comoving radial distance to proper distance at t.

Values, units and domains

c = 299792458 m/s exactly. Times are in s and distance in m. Scale-factor normalization cancels; a may be dimensionless. A finite horizon requires convergence of the future integral.

Meaning

This is a statement about the whole future expansion history. It cannot be inferred solely from an instantaneous expansion rate or from a recession speed exceeding c.

Assumptions and limits

FLRW geometry with positive scale factor. If the future ends at a finite time, use that endpoint instead of infinity. Some cosmologies have no finite event horizon.

Example

For eternal exponential expansion a proportional to exp(Ht) with constant positive H, the integral gives d_e = c/H. H is an inverse-time rate; its reciprocal sets the light-travel time scale.

Source

Sean M. Carroll, Lecture Notes on General Relativity, cosmology chapter.
