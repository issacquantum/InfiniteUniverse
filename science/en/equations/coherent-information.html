Coherent Information

Coherent Information

Equation

$$I_c(\rho,\mathcal{E})=S(\mathcal{E}(\rho))-S((\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathrm{id})(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|))$$

Literal reading

I subscript c of rho and E equals S of E of rho minus S of E tensor identity applied to the outer product ket psi bra psi.

Conceptual reading

Coherent information compares output entropy with the joint entropy of the output and a purifying reference.

Symbols

\(I_c\) is coherent information, with c a label. \(\rho\) is the input density operator and \(\mathcal E\) a quantum channel. \(|\psi\rangle\) is a purification of rho on input plus reference, and the bra is its adjoint. \(S\) is von Neumann entropy. \(\mathrm{id}\) is the identity channel on the reference, not an identity matrix. The tensor product \(\otimes\) applies the two maps to separate subsystems. Nested parentheses fix the order of channel application before entropy evaluation.

Values, units and domains

States have trace one; probabilities and finite-dimensional matrix entries are dimensionless. Entropies use the same base: bits for base two or nats for natural logarithms. Coherent information can be negative.

Meaning

The quantity is minus the conditional entropy of the reference given the output. Positive values can support quantum communication, but a single input value is not itself a channel capacity.

Assumptions and limits

Finite-dimensional channel and a genuine purification of the input. Its value does not depend on which purification is used. Quantum capacity generally requires optimization and regularization across many uses.

Example

For the noiseless qubit channel and a maximally mixed input purified by a Bell pair, output entropy is one bit while the joint output-reference state is pure. Coherent information is one bit.

Source

John Watrous, The Theory of Quantum Information (2018).
