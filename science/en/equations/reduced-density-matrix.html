Reduced Density Matrix

Reduced Density Matrix

Equation

$$ \rho_A = \operatorname{Tr}_B\!\left(|\Psi^{-}\rangle\langle\Psi^{-}|\right) = \frac{1}{2}I $$

Literal Reading

Rho subscript A equals the partial trace over B of ket psi minus times bra psi minus, and equals one half of the identity.

Common Reading

The reduced state of subsystem A is obtained by tracing out B, and for the Bell singlet it is the maximally mixed state one half I.

Symbols

\(\rho_A\): the reduced density matrix describing only subsystem A.

\(\operatorname{Tr}_B\): the partial trace over subsystem B, which removes B from the description.

\(|\Psi^{-}\rangle\langle\Psi^{-}|\): the projector built from the Bell singlet state.

\(\tfrac{1}{2}I\): one half times the identity matrix, giving equal probabilities for the two outcomes of any rank-one projective qubit measurement.

Meaning of the Equation

A local observer sees a mixed state because the partner system is inaccessible. A globally pure entangled state can therefore look locally random.

Conditions and units

Here \(|\Psi^-\rangle=(|01\rangle-|10\rangle)/\sqrt2\) is the normalized two-qubit singlet, and \(I\) is the two-dimensional identity on A. All matrix entries are dimensionless and \(\operatorname{Tr}\rho_A=1\). A general positive operator-valued measure (POVM), a measurement specified by positive operators, need not have equally likely outcomes: for effect \(E_k\), the probability is \(\operatorname{Tr}(E_k)/2\). The partial trace describes local statistics whether or not another observer can access B.

Example: random local spin, correlated joint measurements

What can a detector at A predict without receiving results from B? Prepare two spin-one-half particles in the singlet state. Let \(|0\rangle\) mean spin up and \(|1\rangle\) spin down along a chosen axis. In \(|01\rangle\), the first label belongs to A and the second to B. The singlet is the coherent difference of \(|01\rangle\) and \(|10\rangle\), divided by \(\sqrt{2}\).

Measuring both particles along that axis gives 01 or 10, each with probability one half. If only the record at A is retained, it contains equally likely up and down results. The reduced matrix \(\rho_A=I/2\) encodes these local predictions, and the singlet gives the same equal local probabilities for any spin axis. This does not mean that the full two-particle state is a classical mixture: joint measurements can reveal its entanglement.

The partial trace is the mathematical operation that produces the predictions for A alone. On expanding the joint-state projector, terms containing the inner product \(\langle0|1\rangle\) on B vanish because the two B states are orthogonal. The remaining terms assign weight one half to each A basis-state projector. Their sum is \(I/2\). Taking a partial trace is a change of description, not a physical destruction of particle B.
