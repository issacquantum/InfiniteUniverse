Hydrogen 3d₍z²₎ Wavefunction

Hydrogen 3d₍z²₎ Wavefunction

Equation

$$ \psi_{320}(r,\theta,\phi) = \frac{1}{81\sqrt{6\pi}}\, r^2\, e^{-r/3}\,(3\cos^2\theta - 1) $$

Literal Reading

Psi subscript three two zero of r, theta and phi equals one divided by the product eighty-one times the square root of six pi, times r squared, times e raised to minus r divided by three, times the quantity three cosine squared theta minus one.

Common Reading

This is the normalized atomic-units form of the hydrogen \(3d_{z^2}\)-type wavefunction.

Symbols

\(\psi_{320}\): the hydrogen wavefunction for the state \(n=3,\ell=2,m=0\).

\(r^2 e^{-r/3}\): the radial part, which sets how the state changes with distance.

\(3\cos^2\theta - 1\): the angular factor that creates the two lobes and the torus.

\(1/(81\sqrt{6\pi})\): the normalization prefactor for this state.

Meaning of the Equation

This wavefunction is the signed probability-amplitude shape for the hydrogen 3d z-squared state. Its nodes and angular structure determine the probability density seen in the orbital model.

Conditions and units

Here \(r\) is the dimensionless radius in Bohr radii, \(\theta\) the polar angle, and \(\phi\) the azimuth; the m = 0 state has no azimuthal dependence. The labels 3, 2, 0 are the principal, orbital, and magnetic quantum numbers. The formula assumes the nonrelativistic fixed-proton Coulomb model in atomic units. To recover an SI wavefunction multiply the amplitude by \(a_0^{-3/2}\) and use \(r=r_{\rm SI}/a_0\), where \(a_0\) is the Bohr radius; density then gains \(a_0^{-3}\). Normalization integrates the density with \(r^2\sin\theta\,dr\,d\theta\,d\phi\). The radial density factor alone is not the radial probability distribution, which includes the volume Jacobian.

Worked example or interpretation

At polar angles satisfying cos²θ=1/3, the angular factor vanishes for every radius. On the positive axis it is 2; in the equatorial plane it is −1. The sign change describes amplitude phase, not a negative probability. The Bohr radius is approximately 5.29177210544 × 10⁻¹¹ m.
