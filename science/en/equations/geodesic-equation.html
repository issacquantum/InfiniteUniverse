Geodesic Equation

Geodesic Equation

Equation

$$\frac{d^2x^\mu}{d\tau^2} + \Gamma^\mu_{\alpha\beta}\frac{dx^\alpha}{d\tau}\frac{dx^\beta}{d\tau} = 0$$

Literal Reading

The second derivative of x superscript mu with respect to tau, plus Gamma superscript mu subscript alpha beta times the derivative of x superscript alpha with respect to tau times the derivative of x superscript beta with respect to tau, equals zero.

Common Reading

The equation for free fall motion through curved spacetime.

Symbols

\(x^\mu\): spacetime coordinates along the path.

\(\tau\): proper time along the object's worldline.

\(\Gamma^\mu_{\alpha\beta}\): Christoffel symbols, encoding how coordinates curve and change.

\(\alpha,\beta,\mu\): spacetime indices.

Meaning of the Equation

The displayed equation uses proper time \(\tau\) for a freely falling massive test particle. For a null geodesic, proper time cannot parameterize the path; replace \(\tau\) by an affine parameter such as \(\lambda\). Christoffel symbols describe the connection in the chosen coordinates and may be nonzero even in flat spacetime.

Conditions and units

Repeated indices \(\alpha,\beta\) are summed from 0 to 3; \(\mu\) labels each of four equations. With all \(x^\mu\) in meters and proper time in seconds, \(\Gamma\) has units m⁻¹ and each term has units m/s². The connection is the metric’s Levi-Civita connection. Nongravitational forces and an extended body’s self-force or finite-size effects are excluded from this test-particle equation.

Equivalent notation

$$\frac{d^2x^\mu}{d\lambda^2}+\Gamma^\mu_{\alpha\beta}\frac{dx^\alpha}{d\lambda}\frac{dx^\beta}{d\lambda}=0$$The same equation with derivatives taken with respect to an affine parameter \(\lambda\) applies to both timelike and null geodesics. Its units depend on the chosen normalization of \(\lambda\); each term has coordinate units divided by squared parameter units.

Worked example or interpretation

In inertial Cartesian coordinates in Minkowski spacetime, every connection coefficient vanishes and each coordinate has zero second derivative with respect to an affine parameter. The resulting worldline is straight. Nonzero connection coefficients in polar coordinates would not by themselves demonstrate spacetime curvature.

Source

Sean M. Carroll, Lecture Notes on General Relativity.
