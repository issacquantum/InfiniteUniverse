Quantum State Fidelity

Quantum State Fidelity

Equation

$$ F(\rho, \sigma) = \left(\operatorname{Tr}\sqrt{\sqrt{\rho}\,\sigma\,\sqrt{\rho}}\right)^2 $$

Literal Reading

F of rho and sigma equals the square of the trace of the positive square root of the product square root of rho, sigma, square root of rho.

Common Reading

A measure of how close two quantum states are, especially when noise or imperfect operations are involved.

Symbols

\(F(\rho,\sigma)\): the fidelity between two states.

\(\rho\), \(\sigma\): density matrices being compared.

\(\sqrt{\rho}\): the positive square root of the density matrix.

\(\operatorname{Tr}\): trace operation.

Meaning of the Equation

This page uses the squared Uhlmann fidelity convention. With that convention, larger fidelity means the two states are harder to distinguish.

Conditions and units

For normalized positive density matrices this dimensionless fidelity lies between zero and one, equals one exactly for identical states, and zero for orthogonal supports. For two pure states it is their overlap modulus squared. The trace and operator square root are taken before the final scalar square. The operators need not commute, so the displayed product order matters.

Worked example or interpretation

For ket 0 and the equal superposition of ket 0 and ket 1, the overlap is 1/√2. The squared-fidelity convention therefore gives 1/2. Ket 0 compared with itself gives one, while comparison with orthogonal ket 1 gives zero.

Source

John Watrous, The Theory of Quantum Information.
