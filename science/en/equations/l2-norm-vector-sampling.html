L2-Norm (Euclidean Norm) Vector Sampling

Equation

$$ \Pr[i]=\frac{|x_i|^2}{\|x\|_2^2} $$

Literal Reading

P of i equals vertical bar x sub i vertical bar to the power of 2 over norm x norm sub 2 to the power of 2.

Common Reading

The same probability law produced by measuring an amplitude-encoded quantum state in the computational basis.

Symbols

\(\Pr[i]\): probability that the sampler returns index \(i\).

\(|x_i|^2\): squared magnitude of entry \(x_i\).

\(\|x\|_2^2\): sum of all squared magnitudes, \(\sum_i |x_i|^2\).

Meaning of the Equation

Entries with larger squared magnitude are sampled more often. Quantum-inspired classical algorithms imitate this sampling behavior without necessarily building a quantum state.

Conditions and units

The vector \(x\) must be nonzero with finite Euclidean norm. Its real or complex entries have common units that cancel in the ratio; \(i\) labels entries and the dimensionless probabilities sum to one. Zero entries are never selected. Efficient generation of these samples requires a suitable data structure or access model.

Worked example or interpretation

For x=(−3,4), the squared norm is 9+16=25. Selection probabilities are 9/25 and 16/25; the minus sign of the first entry does not make a negative probability.

Source

Ewin Tang, A quantum-inspired classical algorithm for recommendation systems.
